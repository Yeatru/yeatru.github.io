In-Process Quality Inspection (DUPRO) from China (2026)

During-production inspection at 20-50% catches defects early. When to use, what it checks, DUPRO vs PSI, frequency, cost, and rework savings.

Key Takeaways

  • DUPRO (during-production inspection) is done when 20–50% of an order is produced, to catch systemic defects early — before the whole lot is made and packed.
  • Book DUPRO for new suppliers, new products, high-defect-rate categories, and tight deadlines; repeat orders from proven suppliers usually rely on PSI only.
  • DUPRO costs $80–$200/man-day via agent QC; catching a wrong-material or wrong-process defect at 30% saves 50–90% of the cost of reworking a full 100% lot.
  • DUPRO has no formal Pass/Fail — it is diagnostic. The report lists defects, in-line defect rate, and corrective actions for the remaining production. See the full China QC inspection pipeline.
  • DUPRO does not replace PSI — you still need a final pre-shipment inspection at 100% to gate the balance payment. Pair with AQL 2.5 sampling at PSI.

What Is In-Process Quality Inspection (DUPRO)?

DUPRO (during-production inspection), also called in-process inspection or DPI, is an on-site QC check performed when 20–50% of an order is produced. Unlike pre-shipment inspection, which checks finished, packed goods, DUPRO inspects semi-finished and finished units on the production line. Its purpose is to catch systemic defects — wrong material, wrong process, poor workmanship — early, so the supplier can correct the remaining production instead of reworking the entire lot at the end.

When to Use DUPRO

DUPRO is worth booking in five scenarios:

  • New supplier — you don't yet know their workmanship or defect rate.
  • New product or new tooling — first run of a new mold, print film, or assembly process.
  • High defect-rate category — apparel, toys, electronics, injection-molded parts historically run 3–8% defect rates.
  • Tight deadline — a full-lot rework at PSI would miss the ship date; catching it at 30% keeps the schedule.
  • High order value — a $50k+ order where a 10% defect rate means $5k+ in rework; the $150 DUPRO is cheap insurance.

For repeat orders from proven suppliers with stable defect rates, you can skip DUPRO and rely on PSI only.

What DUPRO Checks

A DUPRO inspector focuses on in-line issues rather than the full PSI checklist:

Check AreaWhat's Inspected
WorkmanshipStitching, gluing, assembly, welding, print registration on finished and semi-finished units
Material & componentsMaterial grade, color, weight, thickness vs spec; correct components installed
In-line defect rateCount of defective units passing the line per hour; % defect rate vs acceptable
Production rateUnits produced vs planned schedule; risk of late shipment
Spec adherenceDimensions, function, logo, label vs approved golden sample
Process controlAre operators following the SOP? Is the machine calibrated? Is QC checking in-line?

Defects found are classified critical/major/minor per your defect classification sheet.

Worked Example: New-Supplier Order, DUPRO Saves $15k+

A German retailer orders 10,000 wooden cutting boards at $5.00/unit ($50,000) from a new supplier in Dongguan. They book one DUPRO at 30% (3,000 units made) through Yeatru at $150.

The DUPRO finds the supplier used pine instead of the specified beech — a material substitution that would fail the food-contact compliance test and get seized at EU customs. If caught at PSI, the entire 10,000-unit lot would need rework (re-cut from beech) costing ~$15,000 in material and labor, plus a 10-day delay.

DUPRO Frequency

  • Standard new order: one DUPRO at 30–40%.
  • High-risk / high-value order: two DUPROs — one at 20%, one at 50%.
  • Long production run (>30 days): weekly DUPROs to monitor the in-line defect rate.
  • Repeat order, proven supplier: skip DUPRO, PSI only.

How DUPRO Reduces Rework Cost

The economics are simple: reworking an unfinished unit on the line costs a fraction of reworking a finished, packed unit. A wrong print at the print stage costs $0.10 to fix; reprinting after assembly and packing costs $1.50. Wrong material caught before cutting costs the material delta; caught after the lot is finished costs labor to disassemble + material + re-assemble. Catching a systemic defect at 30% means only 30% of the lot needs rework, and the remaining 70% is produced correctly from the start.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a DUPRO (during-production inspection)?

DUPRO is an on-site QC check done when 20–50% of an order is produced. The inspector checks semi-finished and finished units on the line for workmanship, material, in-line defects, and spec adherence. The goal is to catch systemic issues early so the supplier can correct the remaining production.

2. When should I use a DUPRO?

Use DUPRO for: new suppliers, new products/tooling, high-defect-rate categories, tight deadlines, and high-value orders. Repeat orders from proven suppliers usually rely on PSI only.

3. DUPRO vs PSI — what's the difference?

DUPRO is mid-run (20–50%), diagnostic, no formal Pass/Fail. PSI is at 100%/80%+ packed, uses strict AQL, and issues a Pass/Fail that gates the balance payment. DUPRO reduces rework cost; PSI is the final gate. DUPRO does not replace PSI.

4. How often should I do DUPRO?

One DUPRO at 30–40% is standard. For high-risk/high-value orders, do two (20% and 50%). For runs over 30 days, do weekly DUPROs. Each costs $80–$200/man-day via agent QC.

5. How much does a DUPRO cost?

A DUPRO takes 1 man-day: $80–$200 via agent QC, $150–$300 mid-tier, $250–$500 big-4 TPI. Compared to a full-lot rework ($2,000–$20,000+), DUPRO is almost always worth it on new orders.

6. Does DUPRO replace the pre-shipment inspection (PSI)?

No. Even with a clean DUPRO, you still need a PSI at 100% production to confirm the finished, packed goods meet spec before paying the balance. A good DUPRO reduces the chance of PSI failure but does not eliminate it.

Conclusion

DUPRO is the most cost-effective QC tool for new suppliers and new products — it catches systemic defects at 20–50% production, when only a fraction of the lot needs rework and the rest can be corrected before it's made. At $80–$200/man-day, a single DUPRO routinely saves thousands in full-lot rework and days of delay.
