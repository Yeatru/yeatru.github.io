ETA vs ETD in China Shipping: What Each Date Means

September 23, 2026

What Do ETA and ETD Mean?

ETD (Estimated Time of Departure) is the date and time a vessel or aircraft is scheduled to leave the origin port or airport — for China sourcing, that means leaving a Chinese port such as Ningbo, Shanghai or Shenzhen (or an airport such as Guangzhou or Hong Kong). ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival) is the date and time it is scheduled to reach the destination port or airport. The two are linked: the carrier calculates the ETA by adding its published transit time to the ETD.

The confusion is that both read like a delivery date. Neither is. The ETD is a departure — your goods have not arrived anywhere yet — and the ETA is arrival at the port, not your warehouse. After the ETA, customs clearance, terminal handling and inland trucking still have to happen before the goods reach you.

ETA vs ETD: Side-by-Side Comparison

| | Point || ETD (Estimated Time of Departure) || ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival) |

| Full name || Estimated Time of Departure || Estimated Time of Arrival |

| Refers to || Leaving the origin port / airport || Reaching the destination port / airport |

| Direction || Outbound from China || Inbound at destination |

| When it appears || At booking / shipping advice || At booking, updated after sailing |

| How it is derived || Carrier sailing schedule || ETD + carrier transit time |

| Is it the delivery date? || No (goods still in transit) || No (port arrival, not your door) |

| Typical accuracy || ± a few days; can roll || ± several days (congestion, weather) |

| Used for || Production & pickup cut-off planning || Restock, clearance and cash-flow planning |

A Real China Shipping Timeline

Here is a typical sea freight China → US West Coast timeline for a 1,000-unit order, showing where ETD and ETA sit in the sequence:

| | Stage || Typical Duration || Milestone |

| Booking confirmed & container assigned || 3–7 days after order || Booking dated |

| Factory pickup & loading at origin || 1–3 days || Container gate-in |

| Vessel departs China || Sailing date || ETD |

| Ocean transit || 18–30 days || In transit |

| Vessel arrives at destination port || Arrival date || ETA |

| Customs clearance & terminal handling || 3–5 days || Released |

| Inland delivery to warehouse || 2–4 days || Delivered |

So for a sea shipment: booking → ETD ≈ 7–14 days, and ETA → delivered ≈ 5–9 days. By air freight the same order is much faster: ETD ≈ 2–5 days after booking, transit 3–7 days, and destination clearance plus delivery 2–4 days.

How to Use ETA and ETD to Plan a Restock

  • Plan production off the ETD. Work backwards from the ETD to set your factory completion and pickup dates; if the ETD rolls, everything upstream shifts.
  • Plan landing and cash flow off the ETA. The ETA is when duty and clearance fees become due (under DDU/DAP) or when the seller finalises the landed cost (under DDP).
  • Convert ETA into a delivery date by adding destination clearance and inland trucking: roughly 3–7 days by sea, 2–4 days by air.
  • Track weekly, not once. An ETA given at booking is a forecast; the reliable ETA arrives after the vessel passes its first transshipment port.
  • Add a buffer for deadlines. For FBA windows and retail launch dates, use ETA + 7–14 days as your working date.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the difference between ETA and ETD?

ETD (Estimated Time of Departure) is when the vessel or aircraft is scheduled to leave the origin port in China. ETA (Estimated Time of Arrival) is when it is scheduled to arrive at the destination port. ETD comes first; ETA is the ETD plus the transit time.

2. What does ETD mean in shipping?

ETD stands for Estimated Time of Departure: the planned date and time a container vessel or aircraft departs the origin port or airport. It is the date your goods leave China, and it is an estimate that can change if the sailing is rolled or delayed.

3. What does ETA mean in shipping?

ETA stands for Estimated Time of Arrival: the planned date and time the vessel or aircraft arrives at the destination port or airport. It is not the date the goods reach your warehouse, because customs clearance and inland delivery still have to happen after arrival.

4. Is the ETA the same as the delivery date?

No. The ETA is arrival at the destination port or airport. After that the goods still need customs clearance, terminal handling and inland trucking. Add roughly 3–7 days by sea and 2–4 days by air to turn an ETA into an expected delivery date.

5. Why does my ETD keep changing?

An ETD is an estimate. Carriers roll bookings when a vessel is overbooked, blank sailings are announced, or equipment is short. When the vessel actually departs, the carrier issues a confirmed on-board date and an updated ETA. Always re-check the ETA after sailing.

6. How accurate are ETA and ETD estimates from China?

ETD is usually accurate within a few days once the vessel has sailed. ETA can shift by several days due to port congestion, weather or transshipment. For planning, treat the ETA as a target and add a 7–14 day buffer for critical deadlines.

Conclusion

ETA and ETD look similar but answer opposite questions: ETD is when your goods leave China; ETA is when they arrive at the destination port. Neither is your delivery date — the goods still need clearance and inland trucking after the ETA. Plan production off the ETD, plan restock and cash flow off the ETA, confirm both after the vessel sails, and keep a 7–14 day buffer for deadlines.
