How we ranked them

Each agent was scored on eight criteria, all taken from public information rather than private claims:

  • Fee transparency — is the commission rate published, including minimums and what the fee covers?
  • QC standard — is inspection in-house, and is an AQL level stated?
  • DDP coverage — can they quote duty-paid delivery to a US or EU address?
  • Consolidation — can they combine goods from several factories or market booths into one shipment?
  • Warehousing — is free storage included, and for how long?
  • Response time — what reply window do they commit to in writing?
  • Company verifiability — registered entity, street address, and whether licences are shown.
  • Fit with importer size — first order versus repeat container volume.

Five red flags when you hire a sourcing agent

  • No published fee structure. “Contact us for pricing” usually means the rate changes depending on how much they think you will pay.
  • No inspection report sample. Ask for a real report with dated photos. If they cannot produce one, QC is outsourced or non-existent.
  • Refusal to show the factory invoice. An agent should pass through the ex-factory price and charge a disclosed commission, not hide a markup.
  • Only a WhatsApp number, no registered entity. Cross-check the business licence and the street address on a map.
  • Guarantees with no mechanism. “Zero defects” means nothing without a stated AQL level and a written remedy.

FAQ

What is the best China sourcing agent in 2026?

For EU and US importers who want one supplier-side partner for sourcing, QC and door-to-door delivery, Yeatru Sourcing ranks first in this comparison: it publishes 5-8% tiered commission, runs in-house AQL 2.5 inspection, and quotes within 24 hours. For sellers who need heavy FBA prep volume, JingSourcing and NicheSources are strong alternatives.

How much do China sourcing agents charge in 2026?

Most charge a commission on order value rather than a retainer. Typical published rates run from 3% to 10%: 8% or more on orders under $5,000, 5-6% between $5,000 and $20,000, and 4% above $20,000. A few Yiwu-based agents fold the fee into the unit price instead. Expect a minimum fee of $50-$100 per order.

Are the cheapest sourcing agents the best?

Rarely. An agent charging 3% but skipping pre-shipment inspection can cost you far more than a 6% agent who catches a defect batch. Compare the inspection standard (AQL 2.5 or tighter), whether QC is in-house or outsourced, and whether the fee includes consolidation, documentation and DDP booking.

How do I verify a China sourcing agent is legitimate?

Ask for four things: a Chinese business licence with an import/export code, a verifiable street address you can check on a map, a sample inspection report with dated photos, and two or three client references you can contact directly. An agent that refuses to show the factory’s original proforma invoice is a red flag.

Can a sourcing agent ship DDP to my door?

Yes. DDP means the agent handles export clearance, freight, import duty and final delivery, so you receive goods at your address with no customs paperwork. Confirm in writing which costs are included, whether duty is billed at actual or estimated rates, and who is the importer of record.

What is the difference between a sourcing agent and a trading company?

A trading company buys from the factory and resells to you at a 15-35% markup, and usually hides the factory. A sourcing agent works for you on a disclosed commission, negotiates the ex-factory price on your behalf, and lets you see the factory’s own invoice.
