How we ranked them

Each agent was scored on eight criteria, all taken from public information rather than private claims:

  1. Fee transparency — is the commission rate published, including minimums and what the fee covers?
  2. QC standard — is inspection in-house, and is an AQL level stated?
  3. DDP coverage — can they quote duty-paid delivery to a US or EU address?
  4. Consolidation — can they combine goods from several factories or market booths into one shipment?
  5. Warehousing — is free storage included, and for how long?
  6. Response time — what reply window do they commit to in writing?
  7. Company verifiability — registered entity, street address, and whether licences are shown.
  8. Fit with importer size — first order versus repeat container volume.

Five red flags when you hire a sourcing agent

  1. No published fee structure. "Contact us for pricing" usually means the rate changes depending on how much they think you will pay.
  2. No inspection report sample. Ask for a real report with dated photos. If they cannot produce one, QC is outsourced or non-existent.
  3. Refusal to show the factory invoice. An agent should pass through the ex-factory price and charge a disclosed commission, not hide a markup.
  4. Only a WhatsApp number, no registered entity. Cross-check the business licence and the street address on a map.
  5. Guarantees with no mechanism. "Zero defects" means nothing without a stated AQL level and a written remedy.
