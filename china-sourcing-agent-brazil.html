One agency from enquiry to unloading

Brazil rewards preparation. An order that reaches Santos with the wrong NCM, a missing licence or a label without Portuguese is not delayed by a week — it is stored, fined or rejected. The work that prevents that sits upstream of the shipment.

  • Supplier search — factories compared against the specification, with capacity and export history checked.
  • Costing before sampling — NCM classification first, because the tax rate decides whether the product is viable at all.
  • Sampling and localisation — Portuguese label, INMETRO or ANATEL requirements identified, manual and packaging adapted.
  • Production and inspection — AQL 2.5 at the factory, defects settled before the balance is paid.
  • Export and shipping — documents issued, cartons consolidated, booked on the lane that suits the volume.

Brazil: the four lines that move the landed cost

The tax stack

Import duty (II) is set by NCM and can run from zero to 35%. IPI is added by product type, PIS/COFINS-Importação applies to the import, ICMS is charged by the state at 17–20%, AFRMM adds 8% on the ocean freight, and Siscomex charges a transaction fee. Together these can add 60–100% to the FOB value, which is why a Brazilian landed-cost sheet looks nothing like a European one.

Certification

ANATEL homologation for anything that transmits or receives radio, INMETRO certification for toys and a defined list of electrical and safety products, ANVISA for cosmetics, food-contact and health items. Wood packaging must be ISPM 15 treated. The certificate is a condition of sale, not an optional extra.

Importer registration

The Brazilian importer needs a Siscomex/Radar profile to import commercially, and some NCM codes require a prior import licence. A buyer without an active Radar can still trade, but the goods need a licensed importer of record — a decision to make before production, not at the port.

Portuguese labelling

Consumer information, warnings, ingredients and instructions have to be in Portuguese, and the importer’s details usually appear on the label. Reprinting a label after the goods are made is the single most expensive correction in a Brazilian order.

Frequently asked questions

Why is importing into Brazil so much more expensive than into Europe?

Because several taxes stack on the same import: II by NCM, IPI, PIS/COFINS-Importação, state ICMS at 17–20%, AFRMM of 8% on ocean freight and the Siscomex fee. Together they can add 60–100% to the FOB value. There is no legal way around them; the only useful work is getting the NCM right and pricing the stack before you commit.

Do I need INMETRO or ANATEL certification?

It depends on the product. Toys and a defined list of electrical and safety goods need INMETRO certification; anything that transmits or receives radio needs ANATEL homologation; cosmetics and health items involve ANVISA. We identify the route before production, because a product that cannot be certified has no market regardless of its price.

Can you ship DDP to Brazil?

DDP is possible but unusual in Brazil, because the tax stack and the importer registration sit with the Brazilian entity. Most buyers act as importer of record with their own despachante and take FOB or CIF, which keeps the ICMS credit and the Radar profile in their own name. We quote both structures and explain what each one leaves with you.

What is the minimum order for Brazil?

50 pieces per SKU for custom work, with mixed SKUs consolidated; stock items from one piece. Because the fixed import costs are high, we usually recommend a slightly larger first shipment than we would for Europe — the tax and clearance cost per unit falls sharply with volume.

Who prepares the Portuguese label?

We manage it at the factory, based on the content your importer or regulatory consultant approves. The label carries the Portuguese consumer information and the importer details required for the market, and it is printed before production — reprinting after the goods are packed is the most expensive mistake in the order.
