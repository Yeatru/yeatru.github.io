Fake China Supplier Invoices & Bank Accounts: How to Verify

Fake China supplier invoices and bank account fraud: redirect scam, $5k-$50k loss. Verify USCC on gsxt.gov.cn, match account name to license, use Trade Assurance.

Key Takeaways

  • The most common fake invoice scam is the "invoice swap" — a "revised Proforma Invoice" with a different bank account, citing audit / tax / system upgrade. Typical loss: $5,000-$50,000; recovery under 10%.
  • Always match the bank account holder name to the gsxt.gov.cn registered entity name using the supplier's USCC before any wire. A personal name on the account is the #1 fraud signal.
  • Request the Bank Account Opening Permit (开户许可证) — a PBC-issued document confirming the account belongs to the registered company.
  • Run a ¥100 test transfer before the main deposit; confirm receipt via the supplier's finance team (not the sales rep).
  • Mid-stream account changes ("we changed banks, send balance to new account") are the #1 fake-invoice vector — re-verify everything before paying the balance.
  • Use Alibaba Trade Assurance for first orders; never wire to a personal account, never Western Union, never crypto — recovery under 10% on those methods.

Fake invoices and bank account fraud cost international buyers an estimated $300-700 million per year in China sourcing, with the typical single-incident loss running $5,000-$50,000. The single most common vector is the "invoice swap" — a supplier (or a scammer using a lookalike email) emails a "revised Proforma Invoice" with a different bank account, citing audit, tax, or system upgrade. The buyer wires to the new account — usually a personal name or shell company — and the funds are gone within hours. This guide walks through the 5 most common fake-invoice patterns, a 5-step bank account verification checklist, a cost-of-fraud table, and a worked example of a $35,000 invoice swap Yeatru intercepted in 2025.

The 5-Step Bank Account Verification Checklist

Run these five checks before any money moves. Total time: 1-2 business days per supplier.

  1. Match the bank account name to the gsxt.gov.cn entity name. Get the supplier's USCC and the bank account holder name; cross-check on the State Administration for Market Regulation registry. The registered entity name must match the bank account holder name exactly. A personal name on the account (vs. a company name) is the #1 fraud signal. See our Chinese supplier license verification guide for the full license check.
  2. Request the Bank Account Opening Permit (开户许可证). This PBC-issued document confirms the account belongs to the registered company. Legitimate factories have it on file and can send a redacted scan within 24 hours. Refusal to share is a hard red flag.
  3. Run a ¥100 test transfer. Before the main deposit, wire ¥100 to the company account and ask the supplier's finance team (not the sales rep) to send a screenshot of the bank's incoming notice showing the company name. A scammer with a shell account cannot produce this; the test costs under $15 and takes one business day.
  4. Cross-check the SWIFT/BIC code against the bank branch address. Look up the SWIFT/BIC on the bank's official website and confirm it routes to the branch city the supplier claims. A mismatch — SWIFT points to a different city or branch — signals an invoice swap or shell account.
  5. Use Trade Assurance or company-account T/T. For first orders, route payment through Alibaba Trade Assurance (escrow) or bank wire to the verified company account. Never wire to a personal account, never Western Union, never crypto — these leave no recovery path. See our how to pay Chinese suppliers safely guide for the full payment hierarchy.

Worked Example: The $35,000 Invoice Swap We Stopped

A UK buyer ordered $35,000 of silicone kitchenware from a Shenzhen supplier. After the 30% deposit (paid to the original company account, verified), the supplier emailed a "revised PI" for the 70% balance citing "audit reasons" — with a new bank account. Pre-balance verification:

  • Original account holder: Shenzhen ABC Plastics Co. Ltd. — matches gsxt.gov.cn entity name.
  • "Revised" account holder: Liu Wei (personal name) — no entity match on gsxt.gov.cn.
  • Bank Account Opening Permit: refused — "confidential".
  • ¥100 test transfer to new account: rejected by supplier — "just send the full balance".
  • SWIFT/BIC cross-check: new SWIFT routed to a different city than the registered address.
  • Email header: reply-to domain was abc-co.com — supplier's real domain is abc-co.cn.

The buyer refused, demanded the original account, and the supplier went silent within 48 hours. Two weeks later the same "supplier" scammed two other buyers for a combined $48,000 using the same invoice-swap pattern. The difference: 1-2 business days of bank account verification versus a $35,000 loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How common is fake invoice / bank account fraud in China sourcing?

Industry estimates put fake-invoice and bank-account fraud at $300-700 million per year in China sourcing, with the typical incident between $5,000 and $50,000. The highest-risk categories are electronics, kitchenware, and apparel — high-volume orders where a single wire is large enough to be worth stealing.

2. What is the average dollar loss from a fake invoice scam?

The average single-incident loss is roughly $12,000, with the range running $5,000-$50,000. Recovery depends on payment method: Trade Assurance recovers 70-90% within 30-60 days; bank wire to a verified company account recovers 30-50% via a Chinese lawyer; WU and crypto recover under 10% — effectively zero.

3. Is Alibaba Trade Assurance safe against invoice fraud?

Yes, when payment stays inside Trade Assurance. The fraud vector is the invoice swap that pulls payment off-platform — once you wire outside Trade Assurance, the escrow no longer applies. The rule: if a supplier asks to take payment off Trade Assurance citing audit, tax, or faster processing, treat it as a fraud signal and stay on-platform.

4. How can I tell if a Chinese commercial invoice is real?

Cross-check eight fields: company name and USCC on gsxt.gov.cn, bank account holder name (must match company, not personal), bank name + branch address on the bank's website, SWIFT/BIC routing to the claimed city, amount + currency (no decimal swap), payment terms (30/70 to company account), and a red company seal (公章) — not a personal stamp. One mismatch is a stop.

5. What should I do if the supplier changes bank accounts mid-order?

Stop. Mid-stream account changes — citing "audit", "tax", or "system upgrade" — are the #1 fake-invoice vector. Re-verify the new account against gsxt.gov.cn and the Bank Account Opening Permit, run another ¥100 test transfer, and confirm by phone (not email) with a known contact. If the supplier pushes, switch to Trade Assurance or walk.

6. Can a sourcing agent verify the supplier's bank account for me?

Yes. A licensed Yiwu-based agent verifies the USCC on gsxt.gov.cn, requests the Bank Account Opening Permit, runs a ¥100 test transfer to the company account, cross-checks the SWIFT/BIC, and routes payment through Trade Assurance — all before the main wire moves. The agent's local presence and license access close most fake-invoice vectors before money leaves your account.
