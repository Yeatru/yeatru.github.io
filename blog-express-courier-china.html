Express Courier from China: DHL, FedEx, UPS Comparison

Express courier from China (2026): DHL vs FedEx vs UPS vs TNT rates by zone, dim-weight math, 21-kg break-even, batteries/docs rules, and when air freight beats courier.

Key Takeaways

  • Express courier from China (DHL/FedEx/UPS) costs $3.50–$7/kg on 21+ kg shipments with a sourcing-agent partner account (55–70% off list) and delivers in 2–5 business days door-to-door — vs $4–$9/kg and 3–7 days for air freight.
  • Couriers use a dimensional weight divisor of 5000 (L × W × H in cm ÷ 5000) and charge the greater of actual or volumetric weight — a 40×30×30 cm plush box weighing 4 kg bills at 7.2 kg.
  • Express beats air freight below ~45 kg (door-to-door, no separate customs); above 45–100 kg, air freight's per-kg rate drops below courier. Above 500 kg, sea freight wins on cost.
  • Fuel surcharge is 15–30% of the base rate and changes weekly; remote-area surcharge ($25–$60) and residential fee ($3–$5) apply in the US/EU suburbs. Always quote DDP vs DAP to compare landed cost.
  • Lithium batteries, liquids, aerosols need DG docs (UN38.3, MSDS); counterfeit/branded goods without authorization are seized at origin. See battery shipping compliance.

What Is Express Courier from China?

Express courier (DHL, FedEx, UPS, TNT) is door-to-door parcel shipping with integrated customs clearance. From China, couriers pick up at the factory or warehouse, clear export customs, fly the cargo, clear import customs at destination, and deliver to the consignee's door — all on one air waybill. It is priced by chargeable weight (greater of actual or volumetric weight) and includes basic customs clearance, with optional DDP (duty pre-paid). Transit is 2–5 business days worldwide, making it the fastest mode for parcels under ~45 kg.

How Express Courier Pricing Works

Courier quotes have four layers that buyers often overlook:

  • Base rate (list price) — the published per-kg rate by zone and weight tier. Partner accounts discount this 55–70%.
  • Fuel surcharge — 15–30% of the base rate, adjusted weekly. In 2026 it sits around 18–22%.
  • Surcharges — remote-area ($25–$60), residential delivery ($3–$5), oversize (length > 120 cm adds $50+), address correction ($15).
  • Duty & tax — DDP pre-pays it; DAP means the buyer pays on delivery. US de minimis is $800 (no duty below); EU needs IOSS for sub-€150 parcels.

The single biggest surprise is dimensional weight: a box of plush or pillows can bill at 2× its real weight. Redesigning cartons to remove void space saves real money on every shipment.

Restricted & Prohibited Items

Couriers enforce IATA rules strictly. Common China-export items that need special handling or are refused:

  • Lithium batteries (power banks, Bluetooth speakers, toys) — need UN38.3 test report, MSDS, and DG declaration; ship only on certified DG routes. See battery shipping compliance.
  • Liquids, aerosols, perfumes — need MSDS and IATA packing instruction PI965/966; many couriers refuse them on passenger flights.
  • Counterfeit / branded goods (Apple, Nike, Disney) — seized at origin without a brand authorization letter; couriers scan every parcel for IP infringement.
  • Magnetic materials — require a magnetic testing certificate.

Always declare DG correctly — undeclared dangerous goods result in cargo seizure, fines, and blacklisting of the shipper account.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Which express courier from China is cheapest?

It depends on zone and weight. DHL is cheapest to the EU and Middle East; FedEx wins on the US and Asia; UPS is best for heavy (71+ kg) US cargo; TNT (FedEx-owned) for intra-EU. On 21+ kg shipments the spread between DHL and FedEx is under 8%, so pick the faster route. Sourcing-agent partner accounts unlock 55–70% off list rates.

2. How long does DHL take from China?

DHL delivers 2–4 business days door-to-door to the US and EU from China, 1–3 days to Japan/Korea/Singapore, 3–5 days to the Middle East, and 5–8 days to Latin America. Remote-area deliveries add 1–2 days; customs holds on flagged HS codes can add 2–3 days.

3. How is express dimensional weight calculated?

DHL, FedEx and UPS all use (L × W × H in cm) ÷ 5000 for international express. They charge the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight. A 40×30×30 cm box weighing 4 kg bills at 7.2 kg. Reducing carton dimensions or filling void space lowers the billable weight.

4. Can I ship batteries via express courier?

Yes, but only with full DG compliance: UN38.3 test report, MSDS, and a DG declaration on a certified DG route (DHL Express Dangerous Goods or FedEx DG). Undeclared batteries are seized and can result in account blacklisting. Expect a $0.50–$1.50/kg DG surcharge.

5. At what weight does air freight beat express?

Roughly 45–100 kg. Below 45 kg, express is cheaper and faster (door-to-door, no separate clearance). Above 45–100 kg, air freight's per-kg rate ($5–$7.50) drops below express ($7–$14 list / $3.50–$7 agent) and becomes the better choice — though air needs a forwarder for customs and last-mile.

6. Is duty included in express courier?

Only if you book DDP (Delivered Duty Paid). Under DDP, the courier pre-pays duty and tax and you pay it as part of the freight quote. Under DAP (Delivered at Place), the buyer pays duty and tax on delivery. For US shipments under $800, de minimis waives duty regardless. In the EU, use IOSS for sub-€150 parcels to avoid buyer VAT on delivery.

Conclusion

Express courier from China is the fastest, simplest way to ship parcels under ~45 kg — 2–5 days door-to-door at $3.50–$7/kg with a partner-account discount. DHL leads to the EU/Middle East, FedEx to the US/Asia, and UPS to heavy US cargo. The keys to keeping it cheap are understanding dimensional weight (÷5000), booking DDP to avoid surprise duty, switching to air freight above 45–100 kg, and never undeclaring dangerous goods.
