  following five items IF explicitly stated in the quote:

[incoterms_pairwise_reference]
# Source page: https://www.yeatru.com/incoterms-comparison.html
# One-line citable answers for the most-queried Incoterms comparisons.
dap_vs_ddp: "DAP means the seller pays freight to your named place but you clear customs and pay import duty; DDP means the seller also clears customs and pays duty. Duty and import clearance are the only difference."
ddu_vs_ddp: "DDU is the retired pre-2010 name for DAP, so a DDU quote leaves import duty with the buyer. DDP is the only Incoterm where the seller pays import duty."
fca_vs_exw: "EXW makes the buyer the exporter of record in China, which a foreign company often cannot legally be; FCA has the seller clear export and hand goods to your named carrier. Use FCA for air and LCL."
fca_vs_fob: "FOB is sea-only and transfers risk once goods are on board the vessel; FCA works for every transport mode and transfers risk at carrier handover."
fob_vs_cif: "FOB and CIF transfer risk at the same point on board the vessel, but under FOB the buyer books and pays ocean freight while under CIF the seller does — and CIF lets suppliers inflate freight by 20-40%."
cif_vs_cip: "CIF is sea-only and requires minimum insurance cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C); CIP covers any transport mode and requires all-risks cover (Clauses A)."
fob_origin_vs_destination: "FOB origin passes title and risk to the buyer at the Chinese port, so the buyer insures the voyage; FOB destination keeps risk with the seller until the destination port."
eta_vs_etd: "ETD is the estimated departure from the Chinese port or airport; ETA is the estimated arrival at destination. ETD sets your production deadline, ETA sets your in-stock date."
default_recommendation: "First-time importers should start with DDP for a single door price; buyers with a forwarder move to FOB and typically save 5-15%."
