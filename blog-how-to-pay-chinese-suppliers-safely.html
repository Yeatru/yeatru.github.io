Pay Chinese Suppliers Safely 2026: 5 Methods

Aug 13, 2026 Yeatru Sourcing 8 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Five main payment methods exist for paying Chinese suppliers: T/T bank transfer, PayPal, Trade Assurance, Western Union, and Letter of Credit — each with different protection and fee trade-offs.
  • The standard safe structure is 50% deposit on order and 50% balance after pre-shipment QC inspection passes, never 100% upfront.
  • Always verify the supplier's bank account name matches the company on their business license — a personal account is a red flag.
  • Red flags: requests to WeChat/Alipay, 100% upfront demands, prices far below market, and unverifiable personal accounts.
  • If you are scammed, act fast: contact your bank, the platform (Alibaba/PayPal), and Chinese authorities — recovery is hard, so prevention is everything.

Paying a Chinese supplier is the moment your money leaves your control, so it is the moment that matters most. Get it right and you fund a clean production run; get it wrong and you fund a disappearance. The good news is that there are well-established, safe ways to pay — the key is matching the method to your order size and risk. This guide compares the five main payment methods, explains the standard 50/50 terms, shows you how to verify a supplier's bank account, and lists the red flags that should stop a deal cold.

1. T/T Bank Transfer (Telegraphic Transfer)

T/T is the workhorse of China sourcing — a direct bank-to-bank wire in USD or RMB. It is cheap, fast (2-5 days), and accepted by every real factory. The trade-off is protection: once the money leaves, it is gone, so T/T is only safe when you have verified the supplier and structured payment as 50/50. Always wire to the supplier's company bank account, never a personal one.

2. PayPal

PayPal offers strong buyer protection and is ideal for samples and small orders under USD 1,000. The downside is fees of around 4% plus a fixed charge, which gets expensive on larger orders, and some factories refuse PayPal for that reason. Use PayPal for your first small transaction with a new supplier.

3. Alibaba Trade Assurance

Trade Assurance is Alibaba's escrow-style protection: your payment is held and released to the supplier only after you confirm delivery, with a dispute process if quality or shipping falls short. It is free or low-cost and strong for orders placed through Alibaba. The catch is that it only applies to orders placed and paid through the Alibaba platform, not direct factory deals.

4. Western Union

Western Union is fast and reaches individuals, but it offers almost no protection and is the payment method of choice for scammers. Avoid it for any B2B purchase. If a supplier insists on Western Union, treat it as a red flag.

5. Letter of Credit (L/C)

An L/C is a bank guarantee that payment is released only when the supplier presents compliant shipping documents. It is the safest method for large orders (typically USD 30,000+) but carries high bank fees and complex paperwork. L/C is overkill for small orders and standard for big institutional purchases.

The Standard 50/50 Payment Terms

The industry standard is 50% deposit when the purchase order is placed, and 50% balance after pre-shipment QC inspection passes and before shipment. This structure protects both sides: the factory gets commitment money to buy materials, and you retain leverage until quality is confirmed. Some agents invoice their commission separately at the balance stage; others fold it into the unit price. Always get the payment schedule in writing before you transfer any money.

Verify the Supplier's Bank Account

Before any wire, confirm the receiving bank account name matches the company name on the supplier's Chinese business license. Pull the license from the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System and check the registered company name, address, and scope. The beneficiary name on the bank account must match that legal entity. If the account is in a personal name, or in a different company name, stop — that mismatch is one of the most common fraud patterns in China sourcing.

Red Flags That Should Stop a Deal

  • Personal account requests: payment to an individual, not the company.
  • 100% upfront: a demand for full payment before any production or QC.
  • WeChat/Alipay requests: these are personal payment apps, not B2B channels.
  • Price too good to be true: a quote 30%+ below market signals a trap.
  • Pressure and urgency: "pay today or lose the deal" is manipulation.
  • Refusal to provide a business license or references.

Escrow Options

Beyond Trade Assurance, a sourcing agent can act as an informal escrow: you pay the agent, the agent pays the factory only after QC passes, and you retain a single accountable party. This is especially useful for first orders with a new supplier, where direct payment feels too risky.

What to Do If You're Scammed

Recovery is difficult, so act immediately. If you paid by PayPal, open a dispute within the 180-day window. If you paid through Trade Assurance, file an Alibaba dispute. If you wired by T/T, contact your bank to recall the transfer (only effective if funds have not been withdrawn) and report to Chinese authorities and your local embassy. Document everything: contracts, chat logs, bank receipts, and the supplier's business license. In most cases, prevention — verification, 50/50 terms, and escrow — is the only reliable protection.
