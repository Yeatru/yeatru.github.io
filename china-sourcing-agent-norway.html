What one-stop procurement covers

Most buyers arrive with a product idea and no supplier. The point of a procurement agency is that one team carries the order from the idea to the door, so you are not assembling a supplier, a forwarder, an inspector and a customs broker on your own.

1. Supplier search and vetting

We compare factories and Yiwu market stalls against your specification, check business licences and export records, and shortlist two or three that can actually hit the target price and the target quality.

2. Price negotiation and quotation

You get one table: unit price, decoration or label cost, domestic freight to our warehouse, our fee and the freight to Norway. The factory price is what the factory charges — our commission is the only line we add.

3. Sampling and customisation

Logo, label, packaging, dust bag, hangtag, colour ratio, size grading. Sample cost is quoted up front and is refundable on the first bulk order.

4. Production follow-up and QC

Production schedule tracked against the promised date; AQL 2.5 inspection at the factory with dated photos and video; rework agreed with the factory before the balance moves.

5. Consolidation

Cartons from several suppliers collected in Yiwu, checked in, stored free for 15 days, and packed into one shipment so you pay one freight bill instead of five.

6. Export documents and delivery

Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin where it helps, and DDP delivery with Norwegian import formalities completed before the truck reaches your address.

Norway: the three steps that decide whether the shipment clears

Norway applies EU product rules through the EEA Agreement, but it runs its own customs and VAT system. A shipment that clears in Sweden does not automatically clear at Svinesund.

Customs and MVA

Norway is outside the EU customs union, so goods are declared on import and 25% VAT (MVA) is charged. A Norwegian organisation number and VAT registration are what let a business deduct that import VAT; without them it becomes a cost. To deduct it you need the import declaration in your own name, which is why we confirm who the importer of record is before quoting.

Duty and B2C parcels

Norway has its own tariff schedule rather than the EU one. Most industrial and consumer goods enter at low or zero duty, but some product groups — apparel and certain textiles in particular — carry rates that do not exist inside the EU, so the HS code is checked before a price is fixed. For direct-to-consumer sales, the VOEC scheme covers consignments under NOK 3,000 per item.

EEA product rules

CE marking, EN 71 for toys, GPSR safety information and technical documentation all apply in Norway through the EEA Agreement, and consumer information has to be in Norwegian. Producer responsibility runs through Norwegian schemes — packaging (Grønt Punkt Norge), waste electricals (Norsirk or Eurovironment) and batteries (Batteriretur) — and registration sits with the producer, not the factory.

Registration obligations are the importer’s, not the agent’s. We flag them before production so nothing is discovered at the border — and we can point you to the scheme you need, but we do not register on your behalf.

Freight lanes to Norway

| | Lane || Typical planning range || Best for || How it is priced |

| Air express, door to door || 5–9 days || Trials, samples, restocks under about 100 kg || Chargeable weight — volumetric, not scale weight |

| Air freight to Oslo (OSL) + clearance || 8–14 days || 200–1,000 kg, time-critical || Per kg + terminal + clearance + MVA |

| Sea LCL to Oslo || 30–45 days || Bulky, low-value-per-kg goods || Per cubic metre + destination charges |

| Road groupage via Sweden (Svinesund) || 25–35 days || Consignments combined with an EU load || Per shipment, customs at the border |

Transit ranges are planning figures, not promises — the booked route, cut-off and vessel or flight are confirmed per shipment, and the DDP price is fixed at booking. A soft toy in a 55 × 40 × 50 cm carton can be charged on volume rather than weight, which is exactly why we quote freight separately from the EXW unit price.

Mixed SKUs are allowed. Fifty pieces of one model and fifty of another still count as a custom run, and they can leave in the same carton.

Frequently asked questions

Do you only source certain product categories?

No. Yeatru is a procurement agency, not a category specialist. The Norwegian order on this page was a plush toy because that is what the buyer sold. The same supplier search, quotation, inspection and consolidation process applies to hardware, textiles, ceramics, electronics, packaging or anything else a Norwegian business already trades in.

Is Norway part of the EU for customs purposes?

No. Norway is in the EEA, which carries EU product rules such as CE marking and GPSR, but it is outside the EU customs union. Goods are declared on import, 25% VAT applies, and Norway uses its own tariff schedule. That is why a Norwegian shipment is quoted separately from an EU shipment even when the product and the factory are identical.

Can you deliver DDP to Norway?

Yes. DDP is quoted per shipment because it depends on chargeable weight, the lane and the MVA handling. If you already have a Norwegian importer of record or your own forwarder, EXW or FCA is usually cheaper because you control the freight and the VAT deduction. We quote both and let you choose.

What is the minimum order?

50 pieces per SKU for custom and private-label work, and mixed SKUs from different factories can go in one shipment. Stock catalogue items can be bought from one piece. Orders below USD 1,000 carry a 10% low-order rate so the work is still viable on both sides.

What does it cost and how is the fee charged?

5–8% of order value for regular sourcing, 8–12% for OEM and private label, 5% for wigs. The fee is shown as its own line on the quotation, next to the factory price and the freight, so you can see what the product costs and what the service costs. There is no hidden margin inside the unit price.
