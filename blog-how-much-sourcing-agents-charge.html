China Sourcing Agent Fees 2026: 3–8% + Calculator

Aug 13 2026 Yeatru Sourcing 9 min read

Key Takeaways

  • China sourcing agents typically charge a 3% to 8% commission on the ex-factory order value or a fixed fee with a minimum order fee of about USD 50.
  • Percentage commission suits larger orders; flat or hybrid fees suit small or repeated orders. Always confirm whether the percentage is on goods only or the full invoice.
  • Sample fees run USD 30-80 per SKU (often refundable on bulk orders from USD 500) and QC inspection costs about USD 120 per man-day.
  • The standard payment structure is 50% deposit on order placement and 50% balance after pre-shipment QC passes.
  • Watch for hidden fees in shipping markups currency conversion and warehouse storage beyond the typical 60-day free window.

How Much Do China Sourcing Agents Charge?

China sourcing agent fees typically range from 3-8% of the total order value depending on service scope and order size. Order Management (placement + tracking only) costs 3-4% with a $150 minimum. Full Sourcing (supplier search + negotiation + QC + shipping) costs 5-8% with a $300 minimum — 7-8% for orders under $5,000 5-6% for $5,000-$20,000 and 3-4% above $20,000. Turnkey OEM services (product development + private label) command 8-15%. Most agents enforce a minimum per-order fee of $50-$100 to cover fixed labor costs regardless of order size.

If you are importing from China for the first time one of the first questions you ask is: "How much do sourcing agents charge in China?" The honest answer is that there is no single price because sourcing agents price their work using a few different models. This 2026 fee guide breaks down every common fee component shows you when each model makes sense and gives you a worked cost calculator example so you can budget a real order.

The Main Sourcing Agent Fee Models

Most China sourcing agents use one of three fee models and many blend them. Understanding the difference is the single biggest factor in predicting your total cost.

Fee Model Typical Range Best For Watch Out For
Percentage commission 3% – 8% of order value Medium to large orders where work scales with value Commission applied to shipping/tax not just goods
Flat fee per order ~USD 90 – 500 per order Small orders repeat buyers simple products Minimum order fee on tiny orders
Hybrid (small % + flat) Low % + flat service fee Buyers who want transparency on fixed work Double counting on inspection/consolidation

At Yeatru Sourcing for example the standard structure is a 3-4% Order Management fee or a 4-8% tiered Full Sourcing fee with a minimum of USD 50 per order. The percentage falls as your order value rises which rewards long-term buyers. A transparent agent will publish these bands; an agent who will not quote a fee until "after we see the product" is usually planning to load it into the unit price.

Every Fee Component Explained

Commission is only one line. A full-service sourcing engagement typically includes several discrete fees. Here is what each one covers and the 2026 ranges you should expect.

1. Sourcing commission (3-8%)

This is the core service fee for finding factories negotiating price managing the order and coordinating export. It is normally calculated on the ex-works or FOB value of the goods only excluding freight and taxes. Some agents apply it to the total invoice including inspection and consolidation so always confirm the base in writing.

2. Blind / dedicated sourcing fee (USD 150-400)

When you ask an agent to source a product from scratch with a full supplier shortlist and comparison report many charge a one-time blind sourcing fee in the USD 150-400 range. This covers the research factory outreach and quote comparison. Reputable agents often credit this fee against your first bulk order commission.

3. Sample fees (USD 30-80 per SKU)

Ordering samples is not free. Agents typically charge USD 30-80 per SKU to cover factory sample cost domestic courier to the agent's office and handling. Many agents refund the sample fee (or credit it) once you place a bulk order of USD 500 or more. See our sample ordering guide for the full process.

4. Quality control inspection (USD 120/man-day)

Pre-shipment QC under the AQL 2.5 standard is billed per inspector per day typically about USD 120 per man-day. A small order needs one man-day; a multi-SKU container may need two or three. The fee buys an on-site inspector a written report with photos and a pass/fail decision before you pay the balance.

5. Warehousing (free 60 days then storage)

Most agents include free consolidation storage for around 60 days while you collect goods from multiple suppliers. Beyond that window daily or monthly storage kicks in. Confirm the free period and the over-run rate up front because slow buyers are where some agents quietly make margin.

6. Shipping and freight (passed through)

Freight is normally passed through at cost-plus a small handling fee. The red flag here is hidden markup on the freight rate or an unfavorable currency conversion. Always ask for the forwarder's original quote.

Payment Terms: The 50/50 Standard

The industry-standard payment structure is 50% deposit when the purchase order is placed and 50% balance after pre-shipment QC inspection passes and before shipment. This 50/50 split protects both sides: the factory gets commitment money to buy materials and you retain leverage until quality is confirmed. The agent's commission is usually invoiced alongside the balance or folded into the unit price for simplicity. For more on safe payment read our guide on how to pay Chinese suppliers safely.

What's Included vs. What's Not

A professional sourcing fee should include supplier search price negotiation order follow-up pre-shipment inspection and export coordination. It typically does not include product certifications (CE/FCC/FDA testing) custom tooling for OEM destination-country customs duty and VAT or storage beyond the free window. If a "full-service" quote sounds too cheap the agent is almost certainly making it up in shipping markup or a hidden factory kickback.

When Percentage Is Better vs. Flat Fee

Use a percentage commission when your order is large enough that the percentage fee buys real leverage on price QC and consolidation — typically orders above USD 5,000. Use a flat fee when you place many small repeat orders of the same product because the fixed work per order does not scale with value and a percentage would overcharge you. A hybrid model fits buyers who want one long-term partner handling everything from samples to freight.

Hidden Fees to Watch For

  • Shipping markup: the agent quotes a freight rate well above the forwarder's actual cost.
  • Currency conversion: an unfavorable RMB/USD rate applied at payment.
  • Factory kickbacks: the agent earns a hidden rebate from the factory inflating your unit price.
  • Re-inspection charges: surprise fees when a failed batch is re-checked.
  • Storage over-run: a short free window that pushes you into daily storage.

Negotiation Tips

Always get the full fee structure in writing before your first order. Ask whether commission is on goods only or the full invoice. Negotiate a sliding scale so the percentage drops as your annual volume grows. Request that the forwarder's original quote be attached to every freight invoice. And run a small trial order first — a trial reveals more about an agent's true fee behavior than any sales pitch.
