China Warehousing & Consolidation: Multi-Supplier LCL

July 4, 2026

What Is China Warehousing & Consolidation?

China warehousing & consolidation is a logistics service that collects cargo from multiple Chinese suppliers into a single warehouse (usually in Yiwu, Shenzhen, or Guangzhou), inspects it, repalletizes it to maximize container utilization, and ships it as one LCL or FCL shipment with one Bill of Lading. Instead of paying 3–10 separate freight, documentation and customs bills, you pay one. This is the single biggest freight-saving lever for buyers who source from more than one factory — which is nearly every importer.

Why Consolidate? The 4 Benefits

  • Lower per-unit freight — one 12 CBM LCL shipment costs less than four 3 CBM LCL shipments (one consolidation fee, one doc fee, one destination THC). Above 15 CBM, a consolidated FCL 20GP is cheaper per CBM than LCL.
  • Fewer customs entries — one BL = one import declaration, one duty calculation, one broker fee. This cuts admin and reduces the chance of a customs hold on one delayed supplier’s cargo.
  • Less damage — cargo is handled once (at the consolidator) instead of 3–10 times (each supplier → port → destination). Fewer handlings = fewer crushed cartons and broken goods.
  • Quality control checkpoint — the consolidator’s warehouse is the ideal place to run an AQL 2.5 inspection before the goods are sealed in a container. Catching defects in China costs a fraction of returning goods after delivery.

Types of Warehousing in China

| | Warehouse Type || Location || Use Case || Free Storage |

| Supplier’s warehouse || Factory floor || Single-supplier orders, no consolidation || Included in EXW |

| 3PL warehouse (Yiwu/Shenzhen) || Yiwu, Shenzhen, Guangzhou || Multi-supplier consolidation, FBA prep || 15 – 30 days |

| Bonded warehouse || FTZ / port zone || Export consolidation, duty deferral, re-export || 30 – 90 days (bonded) |

| Amazon FBA prep warehouse || Yiwu/Shenzhen || FNSKU labeling, polybagging, palletization || 15 – 30 days |

Bonded warehouses are useful when you want to defer duty or re-export goods without them entering China’s domestic market. For most importers, a standard 3PL warehouse in Yiwu or Shenzhen is the most cost-effective choice.

Many sourcing agents (including Yeatru) bundle storage, handling and consolidation into a single flat fee or waive them for clients using their full sourcing service. Always ask whether storage is truly free or rolled into a per-shipment fee.

The Consolidation Process: 5 Steps

  • Collect from suppliers — each factory delivers to the consolidator’s warehouse (or the consolidator picks up). Cargo is logged by PO number, supplier, CBM and weight.
  • Inspect on arrival — run an AQL 2.5 inspection per supplier lot, or a consolidated final inspection. Photograph and document every lot. Reject defective units before they ship.
  • Repalletize & pack — remove suppliers’ individual outer cartons where possible, nest products, repalletize to maximize CBM usage, and load the container (20GP/40GP/40HQ).
  • Issue one BL & ship — a single Bill of Lading covers the whole container. You pay one set of docs, one customs entry, one destination THC.
  • Track & clear — track the vessel/container in real time and clear destination customs within the free port days to avoid demurrage. See shipment tracking.

Worked Example: 3 Suppliers × 1 CBM — Consolidate vs Ship Direct

A buyer sources 1 CBM each from 3 Yiwu suppliers (3 CBM total) to the US West Coast. Compare shipping 3 separate LCL shipments vs consolidating into 1 LCL.

| | Line Item || 3 Separate LCL Shipments || 1 Consolidated LCL Shipment |

| Ocean freight (3 × 1 CBM vs 1 × 3 CBM) || 3 × $70 = $210 || 3 × $55 = $165 |

| Consolidation & handling (per shipment) || 3 × $180 = $540 || 1 × $220 = $220 |

| Documentation & BL || 3 × $60 = $180 || 1 × $60 = $60 |

| Destination THC + customs || 3 × $150 = $450 || 1 × $180 = $180 |

| Last-mile delivery || 3 × $120 = $360 || 1 × $200 = $200 |

| Warehousing (12 days, within free window) || $0 || $0 |

| Total freight + fees || $1,740 || $825 |

| Savings || $915 (52.6% of freight cost) |

| Transit (door-to-door) || 30 – 40 days (staggered) || 30 – 40 days (one shipment) |

Consolidating 3 small shipments into one saves $915 — more than half the freight cost — with no increase in transit time. The savings come from eliminating duplicate consolidation, documentation, customs and last-mile fees. And because cargo is handled once instead of three times, damage risk drops too.

Consolidation for Amazon FBA

Consolidation is the default shipping strategy for Amazon FBA sellers sourcing from China. The consolidator:

  • Collects from 2–10 factories (different SKUs from different suppliers)
  • Applies FNSKU labels and FBA prep (polybagging, bubble wrap, boxing) — see FBA prep service
  • Palletizes to Amazon’s specs (pallet height < 72 in, weight < 1,500 lb, 4-way entry)
  • Ships one container to the Amazon FBA warehouse under DDP

This is far cheaper and lower-risk than shipping each supplier’s order separately, and it ensures all SKUs arrive at the FBA center in one shipment (avoiding split-warehouse routing).

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is shipment consolidation in China?

Consolidation collects cargo from multiple Chinese suppliers into one warehouse, inspects and repalletizes it, then ships it as a single LCL or FCL shipment with one Bill of Lading. This cuts per-unit freight, reduces customs entries, and lowers damage risk from multiple handlings.

2. Does consolidation really save money?

Yes. Three 1 CBM LCL shipments cost more than one 3 CBM LCL shipment because you pay consolidation, documentation and destination fees three times. Above ~15 CBM, a consolidated 20GP FCL is cheaper per CBM than LCL. Typical savings: 15–40% on freight alone. The crossover point and per-CBM math are detailed in our LCL vs FCL comparison.

3. How long is free storage in a China warehouse?

Most China 3PLs and sourcing agents offer 15–30 days of free storage while you gather orders from multiple suppliers. After that, storage runs $0.50–$1.00 per CBM per day. Plan your shipments to ship within the free window.

4. Can I consolidate Amazon FBA orders?

Yes. Consolidation is ideal for FBA: collect from multiple factories, apply FNSKU labels and FBA prep in the China warehouse, palletize to Amazon specs, and ship one container to the FBA warehouse. This is cheaper and lower-risk than shipping each supplier’s order separately.

5. What is a bonded warehouse in China?

A bonded warehouse is a customs-supervised facility where goods can be stored without paying import duties and taxes. In China, bonded warehouses are used for export consolidation and re-export; goods entering the domestic market still require customs clearance and duty payment.

6. Consolidation vs 3PL — what is the difference?

Consolidation specifically means combining multiple supplier shipments into one export container to cut freight and fees. A 3PL (third-party logistics) provider offers a broader range of services: warehousing, consolidation, fulfillment, returns, and last-mile delivery. Consolidation is one service a 3PL provides.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Accepting a flat DDP rate without line items. A real DDP quote breaks out freight, duty, VAT, clearance, and last-mile. A single per-kg number hides costs that surface as surprise invoices or low-quality service.
  • Choosing CIF for control of freight. Under CIF the supplier picks the carrier and inflates freight by 15–25%. Choose FOB (you control the line) or DDP (all-in, your forwarder) — never CIF unless you have no other option.
  • No transit-time buffer for FBA restocks. Sea freight from China runs 25–40 days + 5–7 days FBA receiving. Order 45–60 days before you need stock in FBA, not 30. Running out of stock kills your Amazon ranking.
  • Ignoring ISPM-15 for wooden pallets. EU and US customs require heat-treated pallets with the IPPC stamp. Missing stamps = container detained, €400–$600 fumigation, 3–7 day delay.
  • No cargo insurance. Freight forwarders’ liability is limited to ~$2/kg by convention. For high-value cargo, buy separate all-risks insurance (0.3–0.5% of value) — it costs $50 and pays out $10,000.
