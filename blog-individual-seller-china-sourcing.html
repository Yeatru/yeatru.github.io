Individuals Importing From China 2026

Aug 13, 2026 Yeatru Sourcing 9 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Yes — an individual can import from China without a registered company, especially for small orders under your country's customs de minimis threshold.
  • You need basic ID (passport/ID), a delivery address, and sometimes a tax ID — no business registration required for small personal imports.
  • A realistic minimum order starts around USD 50-500; a sourcing agent handles the language, payment, and consolidation you cannot do alone.
  • Use safe individual payment methods like PayPal and Alibaba Trade Assurance — never wire to an unverifiable personal account.
  • Know your customs threshold: US $800 de minimis, EU €150 IOSS, UK £135 — under these, duty/VAT is simplified or zero.

Can Individuals Import Products from China?

Individual importing refers to personal importation of goods from China without a registered company. In most countries, individuals can import goods valued under $800 (US Section 321) or €150 (EU) duty-free. For larger quantities, individuals typically use a sourcing agent who handles import documentation, or register a sole proprietorship to obtain an import license. A sourcing agent makes individual importing viable by consolidating small orders and handling customs clearance.

A common myth stops many would-be importers before they start: "You need a company to buy from China." That is false. An individual can buy directly from China factories — no LLC, no EIN, no business license required for small orders. The real barriers are language, payment, and logistics, all of which a sourcing agent removes. This guide explains exactly what an individual needs, the minimum realistic order size, safe payment methods, customs thresholds by country, and a step-by-step path from finding a product to receiving it at your door.

Yes, Individuals Can Import from China

There is no legal requirement that you form a company to import goods for personal use or small resale. Chinese factories and platforms sell to whoever pays — individual or company. The constraints are practical, not legal: you need to communicate with the seller, pay them in a way they accept, receive the goods at an address, and clear customs in your country. For small orders, all of this is straightforward. For larger or commercial orders, a company structure helps with duty, tax, and scale, but it is not a prerequisite to start.

What Documents You Need

For a small personal import, the document list is short:

  • ID or passport: for identity when receiving goods and for customs in some countries.
  • A delivery address: your home, a friend's address, or a 3PL warehouse.
  • Tax ID (some countries): the EU and UK may require a tax ID for import; the US generally does not for personal imports under the de minimis threshold.
  • No business registration: not required for small personal or trial orders.

How to Start

The easiest entry point is to send a sourcing agent a product photo, a 1688 link, or a competitor listing. The agent confirms the product, quotes a unit price and MOQ, and tells you the minimum realistic order. You do not need to speak Mandarin, open a Chinese bank account, or figure out export paperwork — the agent handles all of it and invoices you in your currency.

Minimum Realistic Order Size

For an individual, the practical floor is about USD 50-500 per order. Below USD 50, shipping and the agent's minimum order fee (about USD 50) eat any savings. The sweet spot for a first trial is USD 200-500, which is enough to get a real sample batch, test the product, and share shipping across a few SKUs. See our low MOQ guide for how agents reach small quantities.

Safe Payment Methods for Individuals

  • PayPal: strong buyer protection, good for samples and small orders under USD 1,000; fees are higher.
  • Alibaba Trade Assurance: escrow-style protection on Alibaba orders; money is released to the supplier only after you confirm delivery.
  • Bank transfer (T/T): standard for larger orders, but only to a verified company account — never a personal one.

Never pay by WeChat, Alipay, or wire to a personal account for a first order. For full detail, read our guide on how to pay Chinese suppliers safely.

Step-by-Step: From Finding a Product to Receiving at Home

  1. Find a product. Browse 1688, Alibaba, or a competitor store. Save a photo or link.
  2. Send it to a sourcing agent. Get a quote with unit price, MOQ, and shipping estimate.
  3. Order a sample (USD 30-80 per SKU) to verify quality before committing.
  4. Place a small trial order. Pay by PayPal or Trade Assurance, often 50% deposit and 50% after QC.
  5. Agent inspects and consolidates. AQL 2.5 QC removes defective units; goods are packed for export.
  6. Ship by express or air. Express (USD 5-13/kg) is best for small parcels; the agent handles export.
  7. Receive and clear customs. Under the de minimis threshold, your parcel usually arrives duty-free at your door.
