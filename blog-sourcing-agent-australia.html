China Sourcing Agent for Australia & New Zealand

China sourcing agent for Australian buyers: ABF customs, GST 10%, ACL consumer guarantees, RCM mark for electronics, biosecurity ISPM 15, Sydney/Melbourne routing, and an AUD $7.15/unit toy landed example.

Key Takeaways

  • Australia applies GST of 10% on all imports — collected at the border for goods over AUD 1,000 and at point of sale for low-value goods since 2020. GST-registered buyers can claim it back on their BAS.
  • Electronics need the RCM (Regulatory Compliance Mark) covering ACMA EMC plus EESS electrical safety; toys, bicycles, sunglasses, and baby products have mandatory safety standards under the ACL.
  • Biosecurity is enforced at the border: all timber packaging must be ISPM 15 treated and marked; timber and plant products need BICON permits and treatment.
  • ABF customs duty is typically 0–5% by HS code; low-value goods under AUD 1,000 enter duty-free (but still attract GST).
  • Route via Sydney, Melbourne, or Brisbane to match your warehouse. Compare with US sourcing and UK sourcing.

China Sourcing for Australian Buyers

China sourcing for Australian buyers means buying goods from Chinese factories and clearing them through the Australian Border Force (ABF) — a process shaped by GST of 10% on all imports, Australian Consumer Law (ACL) mandatory safety standards, the RCM (Regulatory Compliance Mark) for electronics, and strict biosecurity rules (ISPM 15 wood packaging, BICON permits for timber and plants). An Australia-aware China sourcing agent handles ABN guidance, compliance verification, GST modeling, biosecurity checks, DDP/DAP logistics, and port routing to Sydney, Melbourne, or Brisbane.

An Australian buyer imports 500 children's toys. EXW China price is $3.00/unit, DDP freight is $2.00/unit, the ABF duty is 5%, and GST is 10%.

A toy that costs $3.00 at the factory lands at $7.15/unit in Australia — a 138% uplift driven mostly by the high per-unit freight (low-volume 500 units) and GST. Consolidating to a full 20' container would roughly halve the freight per unit. Toys also need the mandatory AS/NZS 8124 safety test before sale.

Biosecurity, ISPM 15, and ACL

  • ISPM 15 wood packaging — all timber pallets, crates, and dunnage must be heat-treated (HT) or methyl-bromide fumigated (MB) and stamped with the ISPM 15 mark. ABF inspects; untreated wood is fumigated (AUD 100–300) or destroyed.
  • BICON permits — timber, bamboo, and plant-based products need a Biosecurity Import Conditions (BICON) permit and may require treatment (fumigation, irradiation) before or on arrival.
  • ACL consumer guarantees — all goods sold in Australia carry automatic guarantees of acceptable quality and fitness for purpose; defects can force refunds or recalls. Mandatory safety standards apply to toys, bicycles, sunglasses, and baby products.

Port Routing: Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane

Match the port to your warehouse to minimize inland trucking:

  • Sydney (Botany) — for NSW and the Australian Capital Territory; 18–25 days sea from Shanghai/Ningbo.
  • Melbourne — for Victoria and South Australia; 20–27 days sea.
  • Brisbane — for Queensland; 18–24 days sea.

Fremantle (Perth) handles Western Australia but has fewer direct sailings. For DDP to Australia details, see DDP shipping from China.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does an Australia sourcing agent handle?

An Australia-aware China sourcing agent handles supplier discovery, HS-code classification for the ABF tariff, ACL and RCM compliance verification, GST 10% modeling, biosecurity and ISPM 15 checks, DDP shipping to Sydney, Melbourne or Brisbane, and AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC. They turn a factory EXW price into a fully cleared landed cost per unit.

2. How does Australian GST work on imports?

GST of 10% applies to all imported goods. For goods valued over AUD 1,000, GST is collected at the border by ABF; for low-value goods under AUD 1,000, GST has been collected at the point of sale since 2020. GST-registered importers can claim the import GST back as a credit on their BAS.

3. What is the RCM mark?

The Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) is Australia's compliance mark for electrical and electronic devices. It covers both ACMA EMC (electromagnetic compatibility) and EESS (Electrical Equipment Safety System) requirements, which are state-based. Electronics sold in Australia must carry the RCM with a compliant supplier number.

4. What is ISPM 15 and why does it matter?

ISPM 15 is the international standard for wood packaging material. All timber pallets, crates, and dunnage used in shipping to Australia must be heat-treated or fumigated and marked with the ISPM 15 stamp. Untreated wood packaging is detained, fumigated at the importer's cost, or destroyed by ABF biosecurity.

5. What is the Australian Consumer Law (ACL)?

The ACL is Australia's national consumer protection law. It imposes mandatory consumer guarantees (acceptable quality, fitness for purpose, repairs and refunds) on all goods sold in Australia, and mandatory safety standards for specific products such as toys, bicycles, sunglasses, and baby products. Non-compliant goods can be recalled and fines apply.

6. What is Australia's de minimis threshold?

Australia's customs duty de minimis is AUD 1,000 — shipments below this enter duty-free. However, GST of 10% applies to all imported goods regardless of value (collected at the border for over AUD 1,000, and at point of sale for low-value goods since 2020). For commercial imports, GST is the more consistent cost line.
