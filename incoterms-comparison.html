Incoterms Comparison 2026: DAP vs DDP, FCA vs EXW, FOB vs CIF, CIF vs CIP

Which Incoterm Should You Use for China Imports?

Use DDP for your first China order and switch to FOB once you have your own forwarder — DDP is the only Incoterm where the seller pays import duty, and FOB typically saves 5–15% by letting you control the ocean freight. For air freight, LCL or any multimodal cargo, use FCA or CIP instead of FOB or CIF, because FOB and CIF are sea-only. Choose DAP only when you can recover import VAT locally on your own return. Never accept a DDU quote: the term was retired in 2010 and leaves import duty with you, so read it as DAP.

What Is the Difference Between the Incoterms?

Incoterms 2020 are 11 standard trade rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce. Every pair below differs on only one or two of five responsibilities: export clearance, main carriage (freight), cargo insurance, import duty, and the point where risk transfers. DAP vs DDP differs only on import duty and clearance. FCA vs EXW differs only on who clears export and acts as exporter of record. FOB vs CIF transfers risk at the same moment but differs on who books and pays the ocean freight. CIF vs CIP differs on transport mode and the level of insurance cover. FOB origin vs destination differs on where title and risk pass. ETD and ETA are schedule dates, not Incoterms, and FCL vs LCL is a container-loading choice that is independent of the Incoterm.

| | Incoterm || Transport mode || Export clearance || Freight paid by || Insurance || Import duty || Risk transfers || Typical China use |

| EXW Ex Works || Any mode || Buyer || Buyer || Buyer || Buyer || At the seller’s premises || Large-volume buyers with their own China broker |

| FCA Free Carrier || Any mode || Seller || Buyer || Buyer || Buyer || On handover to the buyer’s carrier || Air freight, LCL, consolidated cargo |

Pairwise Incoterms Comparisons

DAP vs DDP →

Under DAP the seller delivers to your named place but you clear customs and pay import duty; under DDP the seller also clears customs and pays duty. Duty and import clearance are the only difference between them.

DDU vs DDP →

DDU is the retired pre-2010 name for DAP, so a DDU quote leaves import duty with the buyer. DDP is the only term where the seller pays duty — if a supplier still quotes DDU, read it as DAP.

FCA vs EXW →

EXW makes the buyer the exporter of record in China, which a foreign company often cannot legally be; FCA has the seller clear the goods for export and hand them to your named carrier. Use FCA for air and LCL, EXW only for large volumes with a China broker.

FCA vs FOB →

FOB is sea-only and transfers risk once the goods are on board the vessel; FCA works for every transport mode and transfers risk when the seller hands the goods to your carrier. Use FCA for air freight and container-yard handover.

FOB vs CIF →

FOB and CIF transfer risk at the same point — on board the vessel — but under FOB you book and pay ocean freight while under CIF the seller does. FOB is usually 5–15% cheaper and always more transparent, because CIF lets suppliers inflate freight by 20–40%.

CIF vs CIP →

CIF is sea-only and requires only minimum insurance cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C); CIP applies to any transport mode and requires all-risks cover (Clauses A). Use CIP for air, rail and multimodal cargo.

FOB origin vs FOB destination →

FOB origin means title and risk pass to the buyer at the Chinese port, so the buyer insures the whole voyage; FOB destination keeps risk with the seller until the destination port, which is rare in China sourcing and usually means freight was quietly bundled into the price.

ETA vs ETD →

ETD is the estimated departure from the Chinese port or airport; ETA is the estimated arrival at destination. ETD sets your production deadline, ETA sets your in-stock date — buffer 3–5 days for port congestion.

FCL vs LCL →

FCL uses a dedicated 20ft or 40ft container and becomes cheaper per CBM above roughly 13–15 CBM; LCL consolidates your cargo with other shippers and suits 1–15 CBM but adds 3–7 days and per-CBM destination fees.

FOB vs EXW →

EXW ends at the Chinese factory gate: you arrange pickup, China export clearance and the exporter-of-record role. FOB ends on board the vessel at the named Chinese port, with the seller paying inland haulage and export clearance. Use FOB unless you already have a China broker for export formalities.

How to Choose an Incoterm for China Imports

| | Your situation || Choose || Why |

| First order, no forwarder, want one door price || DDP || Seller clears customs, pays duty and VAT, delivers to your door |

| You have a forwarder and import broker || FOB || You control freight and save 5–15% versus DDP |

| Full containers, own China trucking and broker || EXW || Lowest unit price; you handle everything from the factory door |

| Air freight, LCL or consolidated cargo || FCA or CIP || FOB and CIF are sea-only and do not fit these modes |

| You can recover import VAT locally || DAP || You clear customs, pay duty and reclaim VAT on your own return |

| A supplier quotes DDU || Read as DAP || DDU was retired in 2010; duty stays with you |

Batch size changes the economics but not the terms. A 50–100 unit custom run is normally shipped by consolidated air or LCL sea freight rather than a full container, so DDP or FCA is usually the practical choice — FOB and CIF assume a full vessel booking that a small order cannot fill.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between DAP and DDP?

DAP (Delivered at Place) means the seller pays freight to your named destination but you clear customs and pay import duty. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) means the seller also clears customs and pays duty. Duty and import clearance are the only difference.

Is DDU the same as DDP?

No. DDU is the retired pre-2010 name for DAP, so under a DDU quote you pay import duty. DDP is the only Incoterm where the seller pays import duty and clears customs for you.

What is the difference between FCA and EXW?

EXW leaves export clearance and the exporter-of-record role with the buyer, which a foreign company often cannot legally assume in China. FCA has the seller clear the goods for export and deliver them to your named carrier — the correct term for air freight and LCL.

What is the difference between FCA and FOB?

FOB applies only to sea and inland waterway freight and transfers risk once the goods are on board the vessel. FCA applies to all transport modes and transfers risk when the seller hands the goods to your carrier.

What is the difference between FOB and EXW?

EXW ends at the seller’s factory: you book the pickup, handle China export clearance and carry the exporter-of-record role. FOB ends on board the vessel at the named Chinese port, with the seller paying inland haulage and export clearance. Choose FOB unless you already have a China broker who files export formalities for you.

What is the difference between FOB and CIF?

Both transfer risk on board the vessel, but under FOB you book and pay ocean freight while under CIF the seller books freight and insurance to the destination port. FOB is normally 5–15% cheaper and removes the 20–40% freight markup suppliers add under CIF.

What is the difference between CIF and CIP?

CIF is sea-only and requires minimum insurance cover (Institute Cargo Clauses C). CIP covers any transport mode and requires all-risks cover (Clauses A), which is why CIP is the right term for air, rail and multimodal shipments.

What is the difference between FOB origin and FOB destination?

FOB origin passes title and risk to the buyer at the Chinese port, so the buyer insures the ocean leg. FOB destination keeps risk with the seller until the destination port — uncommon in China sourcing and usually a sign that freight was bundled into the unit price.

What is the difference between ETD and ETA?

ETD is the estimated departure date from the Chinese port or airport; ETA is the estimated arrival date at destination. ETD drives your production and booking deadlines, ETA drives your in-stock date.

What is the difference between FCL and LCL?

FCL books a dedicated 20ft or 40ft container and wins on cost per CBM above roughly 13–15 CBM. LCL consolidates your cargo with other shippers, suits 1–15 CBM, but adds 3–7 days transit and per-CBM destination fees.

Is DDP the same as door-to-door shipping?

Almost always. DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) is the Incoterm closest to true door-to-door: the seller pays main carriage, insurance, import duty and delivers to your named address, so you receive one all-in price. If a forwarder quotes DDP door-to-door, that is the standard meaning.

Which Incoterm should a first-time China importer choose?

A first-time importer should start with DDP: the seller or forwarder handles export clearance, freight, insurance, import duty and last-mile delivery, so you receive one all-in door price. Move to FOB once you have a forwarder, where you typically save 5–15%.

Who pays import duty under DAP?

Under DAP the buyer pays import duty and import VAT and is the importer of record. The seller pays only the main carriage to the named place. Choose DDP if you want the seller to absorb duty.
