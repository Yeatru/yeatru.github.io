China Quality Control Inspection: AQL 2.5 & $80-500 Costs

June 23, 2026

What Is Quality Control Inspection in China?

A quality control (QC) inspection is an on-site check of manufactured goods by an independent inspector. The inspector samples a batch against the buyer’s approved specification and the AQL 2.5 standard (ISO 2859-1 / ANSI Z1.4), then issues a report with a Pass / Conditional / Fail decision. QC is the buyer’s main defense against defects, short-shipments, wrong labeling, and safety hazards — it is how you verify the goods before paying the balance and before they leave the factory.

Four Types of QC Inspection (and When to Use Each)

There is no single “QC inspection” — there are four, timed to the production stage. Combining the right ones for your order catches defects early, when rework is cheap, instead of after the lot is packed and on a ship.

| | Inspection Type || When Done || What’s Checked || Cost (man-day) || Best For |

| Pre-Production (IPC) || Before mass production, at raw-material / first-article stage || Raw material spec, components, mold, first article vs golden sample || $80 – $300 || New product, new supplier, high material-cost product |

| During-Production (DUPRO) || 20% – 50% of units made || Workmanship, in-line defects, production rate, spec adherence || $80 – $300 || New supplier, tight deadline, high defect-rate product |

| Pre-Shipment (PSI) || 100% produced, 80%+ packed || Quantity, packaging, labeling, appearance, dimensions, function, safety || $80 – $500 || Every order — the mandatory final gate |

| Loading Supervision || At container loading || Carton count, condition, sealing, container integrity, loading method || $100 – $300 || Full containers, high-value goods, fragile goods |

For a typical order, do PSI every time, add DUPRO for new suppliers or new products, and add IPC only when the material cost or tooling risk is high. See our dedicated DUPRO guide and PSI guide for deep dives.

Third-Party vs Agent vs In-House QC

Who runs the inspection matters for cost, independence, and report credibility. There are three tiers:

| | Provider || Cost / man-day || Report Credibility || Lead Time || Best For |

| Big-4 TPI (SGS, BV, TÜV, Intertek) || $250 – $500 || Globally recognized || 2 – 4 days || LC-required inspection, retailer compliance, certification audits |

| Mid-tier inspection firms || $150 – $300 || Good, standardized || 1 – 3 days || Buyers wanting independent reports without big-4 pricing |

| Sourcing-agent QC || $80 – $200 || Good, product-specific || 1 – 2 days || Day-to-day order QC, repeat suppliers, flexible scheduling |

| In-house QC || Salary + travel || Low (not independent) || n/a || Only if you have a China-based team; not recommended as sole gate |

What an Inspection Report Includes

A usable inspection report has eight sections. If any are missing, ask for a re-issue:

  • Order & supplier info — PO number, factory name/address, lot size, inspection date.
  • Quantity check — total cartons counted vs PI; inner-box quantities verified.
  • Packaging & labeling — carton condition, shipping marks, barcodes, language labels, country of origin.
  • Appearance & workmanship — photos of finish, print, stitching, assembly.
  • Dimensions & weight — measured against spec tolerances (e.g. ±1 mm, ±5 g).
  • Function & safety tests — button/battery/voltage test, drop test, torque test for toys, etc.
  • Defect classification — each defect tagged critical/major/minor with photo and location (see defect classification).
  • AQL summary & decision — sample size n, Ac/Re per defect class, counts, and Pass/Conditional/Fail.

Worked Example: 5,000-Unit Order Inspection Timeline & Cost

A UK wholesale buyer orders 5,000 stainless-steel water bottles at $2.80/unit ($14,000 total) from a new Shenzhen supplier. They run IPC, DUPRO, and PSI through Yeatru’s agent QC at $120/man-day.

| | Stage || Timing || Defect Found || Action || Cost |

| IPC || Before mass production || Wrong grade of steel (201 vs 304 spec) || Supplier swaps material before cutting || $120 |

| DUPRO || 50% produced (~2,500 units) || 3% logo misprint (major) || Reprint remaining 50% with corrected film || $120 |

| PSI || 100% produced, 90% packed || 1 critical (leaking seal), 6 major || Rework 60 units, re-inspection passes || $120 + $0 re-inspection |

Total QC cost: $360 (2.6% of order value). Catching the wrong steel at IPC alone avoided a full-lot rework that would have cost $4,000+ in material and labor — the IPC paid for itself 33× over.

Common Defects Found in China Inspections

  • Appearance — scratches, color mismatch, poor print registration, glue stains, uneven stitching.
  • Functional — non-working buttons/switches, leaking seals, weak battery contacts, incorrect voltage.
  • Dimensions — parts out of spec tolerance, missing components, wrong assembly.
  • Packaging & labeling — wrong barcodes, missing country-of-origin label, language errors, weak cartons.
  • Safety (critical) — sharp edges, small detachable parts on toys, live electrical contacts, toxic paint.

How to Handle a Failed Inspection

  • Don’t panic — review the report with the defect photos and counts. Confirm the defect classification matches your defect sheet.
  • Require rework at supplier cost — this is the standard contractual remedy. The supplier reworks defective units and the re-inspection is billed to them.
  • Hold the balance payment — release it only after a Pass re-inspection report. This is your main leverage.
  • For critical defects, reject rather than discount — a safety defect can trigger a recall; never accept critical defects with a price reduction.
  • Air-freight the rework if time-critical — if the supplier reworks only part of the lot and you need the good units urgently, ship the passed portion by air and rework the rest for the next shipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a quality control inspection in China?

A QC inspection is an on-site check of manufactured goods by an independent inspector, sampling a batch against the buyer’s specification and the AQL 2.5 standard (ISO 2859-1). It covers quantity, appearance, dimensions, function, packaging, labeling, and safety. The four main types are pre-production (IPC), during-production (DUPRO), pre-shipment (PSI), and loading supervision.

2. How much does a QC inspection in China cost?

Big-4 third-party inspectors (SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV, Intertek) charge $250–$500/man-day. Mid-tier firms charge $150–$300/man-day. Sourcing-agent QC costs $80–$200/man-day. A standard single-lot PSI takes 1 man-day, so it costs $80–$300 depending on the provider.

3. SGS / BV vs agent QC — which should I choose?

Use SGS/BV/TÜV when a buyer, letter of credit, or retailer requires a named third-party report, or for compliance audits. Use a sourcing agent’s QC team for day-to-day order inspection — it’s 40–70% cheaper, more flexible on scheduling, and the inspector already knows your product and defect list. Agent QC is not a substitute for a third-party audit when certification is required.

4. When should I do a pre-shipment inspection (PSI)?

Book a PSI when production is 100% complete and at least 80% of goods are packed into export cartons. Inspect 1–3 days before cargo-ready, before you pay the balance and before the container loads. PSI should be done on every order over $2,000 from a new or unverified supplier.

5. What happens if a QC inspection fails?

If defect counts hit the Re (reject) number for any class, the lot fails. Standard handling: supplier reworks at their cost and passes a re-inspection. Hold the balance payment until a Pass report. For critical (safety) defects, reject the lot rather than accept with discount. The re-inspection fee is normally borne by the supplier.

6. Can I skip QC inspection to save money?

You can, but you shouldn’t — skipping QC means paying the balance on goods you’ve never seen. A single failed lot that reaches your warehouse costs 5–20× the inspection fee in returns, rework, and lost selling time. At minimum, do a PSI on the first order from every supplier and on every order over $2,000.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Skipping pre-shipment inspection. Once the container is sealed and at sea, defects cannot be returned to the factory. A $150 AQL 2.5 inspection catches $1,000+ in defects before they cross the Pacific.
  • Using the wrong AQL level. AQL 2.5/4.0 is standard for consumer goods; 0.65 for medical/safety products, 4.0 for low-value commodities. The wrong level either over-inspects (wasting money) or under-inspects (missing defects).
  • Inspecting at the factory without photo/video evidence. A verbal ‘pass’ report is worthless when you receive 200 broken units. Demand dated photos of every defect category and a sealed sample retained at the factory.
  • No defect rework timeline in the contract. Specify that defects must be reworked within 3–5 days at the factory’s cost, with a re-inspection before shipment. Without this, factories delay rework indefinitely.
  • Confusing ‘passed QC’ with ‘guaranteed quality’. AQL sampling statistically estimates defect rate — it does not guarantee zero defects. Budget 1–3% for post-shipment defects and include a defect-credit clause in the contract.

Conclusion

QC inspection is the buyer’s last line of defense before goods leave China, and the four-stage pipeline (IPC → DUPRO → PSI → loading) catches defects at the cheapest point to fix them. Run every inspection against AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor / 0 critical, require photo evidence for every defect, and hold the balance payment until a Pass report.
