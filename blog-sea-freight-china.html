Sea Freight from China: FCL vs LCL Costs & Transit (2026)

Ocean freight rates, FCL vs LCL, transit times, ports.

Key Takeaways

  • Sea freight from China costs $40–$80/CBM (LCL) or $1,800–$3,800 per container (FCL) in 2026 — roughly $0.50–$1.50/kg, vs $4–$9/kg for air freight.
  • Transit runs 12–18 days to the US West Coast, 28–38 days to the US East Coast and EU, and 32–42 days to the UK; door-to-door adds 3–7 days for inland haulage and customs.
  • Pick FCL once your cargo exceeds ~15 CBM — LCL's per-CBM rate includes $30–$60/CBM of consolidation, THC and documentation fees that disappear with a full container.
  • Main loading ports are Shanghai, Shenzhen/Yantian, Ningbo, Qingdao and Guangzhou; Yiwu buyers truck to Ningbo (280 km) to save $200–$400 vs Shanghai. Pre-clear via China customs clearance.
  • Peak season (Aug–Oct) adds PSS ($300–$800/container) and GRI; book 2–3 weeks ahead and always quote DDP against FOB/CIF via DDP shipping from China.

What Is Sea Freight from China?

Sea freight (ocean freight) moves cargo in sealed steel containers aboard container ships from Chinese ports to destinations worldwide. It is priced by container (FCL) or by cubic meter (LCL), not by weight like air freight. The five main export gateways are Shanghai, Shenzhen/Yantian, Ningbo, Qingdao and Guangzhou. Standard container types are the 20GP, 40GP and 40HQ; refrigerated (reefer), open-top and flat-rack variants exist for special cargo.

LCL vs FCL: Which Should You Book?

Your decision hinges on volume (CBM) and weight. LCL consolidates your cargo with other shippers' in one container; FCL gives you the whole container.

FactorLCL (Less than Container Load)FCL (Full Container Load)
Pricing unitPer CBM (min 1 CBM)Per container (flat rate)
Ideal volume1 – 15 CBM15+ CBM
Per-CBM cost$40 – $160 + feesLower once volume > ~15 CBM
Transit3 – 7 days slower (consolidation)Direct, fastest sea option

Minimum weight rule1 CBM = 1,000 kg (whichever greater)Container payload limit applies

Rule of thumb: below 1 CBM, express courier or air is usually cheaper; 1–15 CBM = LCL; above 15 CBM, price out both — FCL often wins on a per-unit basis and reduces damage. Many buyers use China warehousing & consolidation to combine multiple supplier orders into one FCL.

Note the payload ceiling: a 20GP holds 33 CBM but only ~21.7 tons, so dense goods like ceramic tiles hit the weight limit long before the volume limit. Light, bulky goods fill a 40HQ's 76 CBM before its weight cap.

Incoterms for Sea Freight: FOB, CIF, DDP

The Incoterm decides who pays freight, insurance and duty, and where risk transfers.

  • FOB (Free On Board) — supplier delivers the container onto the vessel at the China port; you pay ocean freight, insurance, duty and delivery. Most common for experienced buyers; you control the freight and can shop rates.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) — supplier pays freight and insurance to the destination port. Simpler but suppliers often mark up freight 10–25%; you still handle destination customs and duty.
  • DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) — forwarder handles everything to your door, including duty and tax. Simplest for small buyers but the import is filed under the forwarder's EORI/account, so VAT is not recoverable in the EU unless you provide your own EORI. See FOB vs EXW vs DDP.

Peak Season Surcharges & Demurrage

Two cost areas surprise new importers:

  • Peak Season Surcharge (PSS) — shipping lines add $300–$800 per container roughly August through October, plus General Rate Increases (GRI). Book 2–3 weeks ahead and ship before mid-August to lock lower rates.
  • Demurrage & Detention (D&D) — the port gives 3–7 free days after discharge. After that, demurrage runs $100–$250/day. Detention applies once the container leaves the port but isn't returned empty on time. Clear customs and return the container within the free window.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How much does sea freight from China cost?

In 2026, LCL from China to the US West Coast costs $40–$80/CBM, to the EU $50–$90/CBM. FCL 20GP to LA runs $1,800–$3,200, to Rotterdam $2,200–$3,800. DDP door-to-door adds $300–$900 for customs, duty and last-mile depending on HS code and destination.

2. How long does sea freight from China take?

Port-to-port: US West Coast 12–18 days, US East Coast 28–38 days, EU 28–38 days, UK 32–42 days, Australia 18–25 days. Door-to-door adds 3–7 days for inland + customs, so plan 18–45 days total.

3. LCL or FCL — which should I choose?

LCL is priced per CBM and works for 1–15 CBM. Once cargo exceeds ~15 CBM, an FCL 20GP (33 CBM capacity) is usually cheaper because LCL adds consolidation, handling and documentation fees of $30–$60/CBM. Above 67 CBM, book a 40HQ.

4. FOB vs CIF vs DDP — what's the difference?

FOB: supplier delivers to the China port, you pay freight + duty + delivery. CIF: supplier pays freight + insurance but often marks it up 10–25%. DDP: the forwarder handles everything to your door including duty and tax — simplest for small buyers but VAT is not recoverable in the EU unless you provide your own EORI. Compare all three via FOB vs EXW vs DDP.

5. Do sea freight rates rise in peak season?

Yes. Peak season surcharges (PSS) of $300–$800 per container and General Rate Increases (GRI) apply roughly August through October as retailers stock for the holidays. Booking 2–3 weeks ahead and shipping before mid-August avoids most PSS.

6. What are demurrage and detention?

Demurrage is charged by the shipping line when the container sits at the port beyond the free days (usually 3–7), at $100–$250/day. Detention is charged when you keep the container past the free return window after delivery. Clear customs fast and return the empty on time to avoid them.

Conclusion

Sea freight from China is the cheapest way to move bulk cargo — $0.50–$1.50/kg vs $4–$9/kg by air — but it takes 18–45 days door-to-door. The key to keeping it cheap is choosing the right mode (LCL under 15 CBM, FCL above), shipping from the nearest port, booking before the August peak season, and clearing customs fast to avoid demurrage. Always compare DDP, FOB and CIF quotes on total landed cost, not the headline freight number.
