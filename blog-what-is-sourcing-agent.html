What Is a Sourcing Agent? Roles, Fees & Services

Aug 12, 2026 Neil Liu 45 min read

Key Takeaways

  • A sourcing agent is an independent professional who works for you — the buyer — not for the Chinese supplier. Their job is to find factories, negotiate prices, run quality control, and handle export logistics.
  • Typical fees range from 3% to 8% commission on the order value, or a fixed fee of $90–$500 per order. Avoid agents who want more than 8% or who charge hidden fees.
  • A sourcing agent differs from a trading company: agents do not markup prices and always reveal the actual factory name and location.
  • Using a local agent in China saves you 20–40% on communication time and gives you on-the-ground factory access that Alibaba cannot match.
  • Always verify your agent's business license and ask for 3+ client references in your product category before signing.

What Is a Sourcing Agent?

A China sourcing agent is a professional intermediary who helps international buyers source products from China efficiently. Think of them as your trusted local partner — they live in China, speak the language, know the factories, and work exclusively for your interests. Unlike trading companiesLearn more in our Alibaba vs sourcing agent comparison. that buy and resell goods at a markup, a sourcing agent is paid a transparent service fee to manage the entire sourcing process on your behalf.

What Does a Sourcing Agent Do?

A full-service sourcing agent typically handles ten core services:

  1. Supplier discovery — searching factories on Alibaba, 1688, Yiwu markets (see our Yiwu market guide for details), and Canton Fair
  2. Factory verification — confirming the factory exists, has a valid business license, and can meet your MOQ
  3. Price negotiation — using volume commitments and local market knowledge to drive down ex-factory prices
  4. Quality control inspection — pre-shipment QC using AQL 2.5 standard, photo and video evidence
  5. OEM / private label — custom molds, packaging design, and brand printing coordination
  6. Sample sourcing — arranging sample production, fast shipping, and defect identification
  7. Production follow-up — daily or weekly updates on manufacturing progress
  8. Export documentation — commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin
  9. International logistics — arranging sea freight, air freight, or DDP door-to-door shipping
  10. After-sales support — handling defects, replacements, or rework if quality issues arise

How Much Does a Sourcing Agent Cost?

Agent fees are one of the most common questions we receive. Here is a clear breakdown:

Commission-Based Fees (Most Common)

  • Budget buyers ($1,000–$5,000): 6–8% commission, minimum $100
  • Growing businesses ($5,000–$20,000): 4–6% commission
  • Wholesale / bulk ($20,000+): 3–4% commission, negotiable

Worked Example

Suppose you order 5,000 silicone kitchen utensils at $2.50 each = $12,500 total ex-factory:

  • At 5% commission: agent fee = $625
  • At 8% commission: agent fee = $1,000
  • Good agents will negotiate the unit price down by 10–20% — easily covering their fee

Hidden costs to avoid: setup fees, per-supplier discovery fees, additional charges for factory audits, or "express processing" surcharges. Your agent should quote one all-inclusive fee upfront.

When to Choose a Sourcing Agent vs Buying Direct

You can buy directly from suppliers on Alibaba. But consider these factors:

Factory verificationAgent visits factory in personYou rely on supplier's online profile
Quality controlLocal inspector on-sitePhotos/videos from supplier (may be misleading)
Problem resolutionAgent mediates locallyYou compete on Alibaba's complaint process
Cost3–8% fee on order valueNo agent fee but higher risk of overpaying

If your order is under $500 and you are testing one simple product, buying direct may be fine. For anything larger — or if compliance, quality, and consistency matter — an agent is the safer and more cost-effective choice.

How to Choose the Right Sourcing Agent (7 Steps)

  1. Define your product clearly — know your MOQ, target price range, quality standards, and shipping timeline
  2. Check their legal entity — ask for a business license (统一社会信用代码) and verify it on the Chinese government business registry
  3. Confirm on-the-ground presence — they should have an office or team in Yiwu, Guangzhou, or Shenzhen — not just a mailbox
  4. Ask for client references — 3 recent clients in your product category, with contact details
  5. Compare fee structures — get written quotes from 2–3 agents and compare all-inclusive costs
  6. Test communication — email, message on WhatsApp or WeChat, and call them. If they take 3 days to reply, they will be slow during production
  7. Run a trial order — start small ($500–$2,000) before committing to larger volumes

Red Flags to Avoid

  • Agent refuses to show their business license or physical office address
  • Fees exceed 10% or are structured with multiple hidden charges
  • They cannot provide references in your product category
  • Communication is only through a messaging app — no email, no phone, no video call
  • They push you toward one specific supplier immediately without showing alternatives
  • They claim to be a "direct factory" but also offer sourcing agency services — decide whether you need a factory or an agent
  • Payment requested in full upfront before any supplier work begins

Sourcing Agent for TikTok Shop / Shopify / Wholesale

Different business models have different sourcing needs:

  • TikTok Shop / Shopify sellers typically need low MOQ sourcing (50–500 pieces), fast turnaround (10–20 days), and private label packaging with barcodes. An agent with e-commerce experience understands these requirements.
  • Wholesale buyers / distributors need volume pricing (10,000+ pieces), container-level shipping, and consistent quality across batches. An agent with strong factory relationships can secure better tier pricing.
  • Amazon FBA sellers need FBA-compliant packaging, product photography, and DDP shipping that delivers directly to Amazon warehouses. Look for an agent that offers these services as a standard package.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is a sourcing agent the same as a freight forwarder?

No. A sourcing agent manages the product sourcing side — finding factories, negotiation, QC, OEM. A freight forwarder manages the shipping side — booking containers, customs clearance, last-mile delivery. Some agents like Yeatru offer both services under one roof for convenience.

2. How much does a sourcing agent save me?

Good agents typically negotiate 15–30% lower ex-factory prices than what you would get messaging suppliers directly. The savings usually exceed their fee many times over.

3. Do I need a sourcing agent if I already found a supplier on Alibaba?

Yes, an agent can still help. They can verify the supplier's legitimacy (via our supplier verification process), negotiate a better price, run pre-shipment QC, and handle export documentation. Many buyers use agents even when they already have a supplier contact.

4. Can a sourcing agent work with me remotely?

Absolutely. Most agent relationships are conducted entirely online — email, WhatsApp, WeChat, and video calls. The key benefit is that the agent is physically in China and can visit factories on your behalf.

5. What if my order is very small ($200–$500)?

Some agents charge a minimum fee ($50–$150) for small orders. For very small test orders, you may prefer to buy direct first. But if you plan to reorder, establishing a relationship with an agent early is worthwhile.

6. How long does the sourcing process take?

For an existing product you want to import: 5–15 days from RFQ to confirmed supplier. For custom/OEM products: 20–40 days including sample approval. Production itself takes 15–60 days depending on quantity and complexity.

7. Do sourcing agents handle customs and import duties?

Many agents offer DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) shipping, which means they handle export clearance in China and pay import duties and taxes on your behalf. You pay one landed price and receive the goods at your door.

8. Can I trust a sourcing agent I found online?

As with any service provider, do your due diligence. Check their business license, ask for references, and start with a small trial order — also read our low-MOQ sourcing guide. Established agents like Yeatru have been in business since 2013 and have public client testimonials.

9. What payment methods do sourcing agents use?

Most accept bank transfer (T/T), sometimes Western Union or XTransfer. Reputable agents will provide a formal invoice and never ask for payments to personal accounts.

10. Do I still need to visit China if I use a sourcing agent?

No. A good agent eliminates the need to travel. But if your order exceeds $50,000 or you are developing a new custom product and OEM development, a 3–5 day visit to meet the factory in person is still valuable.

Conclusion

A China sourcing agent is your local expert who navigates the complexities of importing for you. They save you time, money, and risk. The investment — typically 3–8% of your order value — is recouped through better pricing, verified factories, and consistent quality.
