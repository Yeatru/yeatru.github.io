To understand why the role exists, it helps to look at how ground-level manufacturing acquisition from China actually works for a foreign acquiring-party client. A typical local production plant in Yiwu or Shenzhen does not list its products on an English-language storefront with transparent pricing. It lists on Chinese platforms, attends domestic trade fairs, and responds to Mandarin-language inquiries. Its sales staff may have limited English, its quotes are often informal WeChat messages, and its willingness to negotiate depends heavily on whether the buyer is perceived as a serious long-term customer. An overseas importer principal who contacts such a manufacturing workshop directly receives a higher quoted price, slower responses, and almost no leverage. The role also exists because importing from China is regulated, documented, and logistically complex.
