What one-stop procurement covers

The reason to use a procurement agency instead of a trading company is that you keep the specification and the price but lose the coordination work: supplier vetting, sampling, inspection, consolidation and export paperwork all sit with one team.

1. Supplier search and vetting

Factories and market stalls compared against your spec; licences and export history checked before a deposit is discussed.

2. Negotiation and one quotation

Unit price, logo or label cost, domestic freight, our fee and the freight to Australia — separated, in one table.

3. Sampling and private label

Brand labels, packaging, barcodes, hangtags and colour or size ratios, quoted per unit so the customisation cost is visible.

4. Production follow-up and QC

Schedule tracked against the promised date, AQL 2.5 inspection at the factory, defects agreed with the factory before balance.

5. Consolidation across suppliers

Several factories, one shipment. Cartons checked in at Yiwu, stored free for 15 days, packed for the sea or air lane you choose.

6. Export documents and delivery

Invoice, packing list, FTA certificate of origin where it applies, fumigation or treatment certificates where biosecurity requires them.

Australia: three checks that decide the landed cost

Australia is not a harder market than the EU, but it fails in different places. These three decide whether the container clears, and all three are checked before the factory is booked.

GST, duty and the FTA

10% GST applies to most imports. Duty depends on the HS code, and under the China–Australia FTA many goods enter at 0% if a valid certificate of origin is issued with the shipment — a document that has to be arranged before export, not after arrival. Consignments at or below AUD 1,000 follow the low-value imported goods rules instead.

Biosecurity and natural materials

Seagrass, water hyacinth, coconut shell, rattan, bamboo, dried plant material, artificial plants and timber are all biosecurity-sensitive. DAFF inspects on arrival, some lines need an import permit, and timber packaging must meet ISPM 15. This is the single most common reason a natural-fibre shipment is held.

Product compliance

Electrical goods need RCM marking and the relevant AS/NZS standards; toys are tested to AS/NZS ISO 8124 and can fall under ACCC mandatory standards; clothing and textiles carry care-labelling requirements; cosmetics and skincare have their own ingredient rules. The test route is confirmed before the quotation is fixed.

Biosecurity and product compliance are the importer's legal responsibility. What we do is identify the requirement early, get the treatment or test done at the factory, and put the certificate in the document pack — so the shipment is not held while paperwork is found.

How an order runs, start to door

  1. Brief. Product link, photo or spec, target quantity, target landed cost. If there is no spec yet, we write one.
  2. Sourcing and quotation. Two or three suppliers compared; product, freight and fee shown separately.
  3. Sample or trial. Approved in your hands, including label and packaging.
  4. Production and inspection. AQL 2.5 at the factory, dated evidence, biosecurity and test documents collected before export.
  5. Consolidation and delivery. Cartons combined in Yiwu, FTA certificate issued where eligible, DDP delivery with GST and duty handled.
