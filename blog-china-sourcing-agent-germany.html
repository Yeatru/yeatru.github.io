China Sourcing Agent for Germany & DACH Buyers

Sourcing for German/DACH buyers, CE, REACH, DDP to DE.

Key Takeaways

  • Germany has one of the EU's strictest import regimes: CE marking, REACH SVHC, WEEE, and EPR (VerpackG packaging, ElektroG electronics, BattG batteries) are mandatory, plus GPSR 2024 requires an EU responsible person.
  • German VAT is 19% on the customs value (EXW + freight + insurance) plus import duty. For B2C parcels under €150 use IOSS; for B2B, the importer pays VAT at the border and reclaims it on the German VAT return.
  • Sea freight from China to Hamburg takes 30–40 days + 2–5 days clearance + 1–3 days trucking. Bremerhaven is the alternative for southern Germany (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg) via rail.
  • A China sourcing agent for Germany handles CE/REACH/EPR compliance, EUR invoicing, and Hamburg routing — the compliance mistakes alone can cost you €200,000+ in fines plus seized shipments.
  • German consumer protection: 14-day Widerrufsrecht (withdrawal right), Produkthaftung (strict liability), and German-language labeling. Safety-critical products often need the voluntary GS mark (TÜV). See also sourcing for the whole EU and CE certification.

What Is China Sourcing for German & DACH Buyers?

China sourcing for German, Austrian, and Swiss (DACH) buyers is the process of finding, qualifying, and importing products from China while complying with EU and German-specific regulations. Unlike sourcing for the US, the DACH market adds layers of compliance: mandatory CE marking, REACH chemical restrictions, WEEE and EPR registration, and since 2024 the EU General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR). The biggest cost for German buyers is not the unit price — it is getting the compliance paperwork wrong and having a container seized at Hamburg.

Worked Example: Electronics Imported to Germany

A German Amazon.de seller imports 5,000 Bluetooth speakers from Shenzhen.

  • EXW unit price: $4.00
  • Sea freight + insurance: $0.80/unit
  • Customs value: $4.80/unit → duty at 4.2% (HS 8518) = $0.20/unit
  • VAT base: $4.80 + $0.20 = $5.00 → 19% VAT = $0.95/unit
  • CE (LVD+EMC+RED) + REACH testing: $2,200 total = $0.44/unit
  • EPR (ElektroG + VerpackG): $0.10/unit
  • Total landed: $4.00 + $0.80 + $0.20 + $0.95 + $0.44 + $0.10 = $6.49/unit ≈ €8.50/unit

The seller lists at €24.99 on Amazon.de — the €8.50 landed cost leaves healthy margin after Amazon fees. Note that skipping the CE test would save $2,200 upfront but risks having all 5,000 units seized at Hamburg, a far bigger loss.

Shipping & Customs to Germany

  • Hamburg — Germany's largest container port, handles 80%+ of China container volume. 30–40 days from Shanghai/Shenzhen, 2–5 days clearance.
  • Bremerhaven — second-largest, closer to northern industrial regions and better rail connections to Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg.
  • Frankfurt / Munich air — for urgent or high-value cargo; 5–8 days door-to-door, $4–$7/kg.
  • EORI number — required for any business importing into the EU. Apply through your local German customs office (BZO).
  • DDP shipping — a China forwarder handles duty + 19% VAT + clearance, delivering to your door. Compare DDP vs EXW+own-freight; DDP is usually simpler for small importers. See DDP shipping from China.

German Consumer Protection & Product Safety

German B2C law is stricter than the EU average. Key rules:

  • Widerrufsrecht (14-day withdrawal right) — consumers can return most goods within 14 days for any reason.
  • Produkthaftung (strict product liability) — the seller is liable for damage caused by a defective product, even without fault.
  • AGB (General Terms of Business) — your terms must be fair and properly disclosed; unfair clauses are void.
  • GPSR 2024 — all consumer products need an EU responsible person, traceability (batch numbers), and an accident-reporting process.
  • German labeling — safety warnings, instructions, and the CE mark must be in German.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Why use a China sourcing agent for Germany?

Germany has one of the strictest compliance regimes in the EU. A sourcing agent fluent in CE, REACH, WEEE, EPR (VerpackG/ElektroG/BattG) and GPSR 2024 ensures your product clears customs without being held or destroyed. An agent also handles EUR invoicing, Hamburg/Bremerhaven routing, and German-speaking support, saving you the cost of learning German import rules the hard way.

2. Is CE marking mandatory for Germany?

Yes. CE marking is mandatory for most products sold in Germany and the EU (electronics, toys, machinery, PPE, construction products). You need a test report from an accredited lab, a Declaration of Conformity (DoC) in your name, and the CE mark on the product and packaging. Without it, customs can seize the shipment and German retailers will refuse to list it.

3. What is EPR VerpackG and do I need it?

VerpackG is Germany's packaging EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility) law. Anyone who puts packaged goods on the German market must register with the LUCID register and pay a license fee to a dual system (e.g. Der Grüne Punkt) based on packaging weight and material. Typical cost: €50–500/year for small sellers. Non-registration can block your listing on Amazon.de and bring fines up to €200,000.

4. How much is German VAT on imports from China?

Germany's standard VAT rate is 19% (7% for some food and books). It is calculated on the customs value (EXW + freight + insurance) plus import duty. For B2C parcels under €150, use IOSS to collect 19% VAT at checkout and avoid clearance delays. For B2B commercial shipments, the importer pays 19% VAT at the border and reclaims it on their German VAT return.

5. How long does sea shipping take to Hamburg from China?

Sea freight from Shanghai/Shenzhen to Hamburg takes 30–40 days port-to-port, plus 2–5 days for customs clearance and 1–3 days for last-mile trucking. Total door-to-door: 35–50 days. Bremerhaven adds 1–2 days but often faster rail to southern Germany (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg). For urgent orders use air freight (5–8 days to Frankfurt/Munich).

6. What German product safety rules apply?

Germany enforces EU GPSR (General Product Safety Regulation, 2024), the Produktsicherheitsgesetz (ProdSG), and Produkthaftung (strict product liability). Safety-critical products (tools, toys, children's items) often need the voluntary GS mark (issued by TÜV) to be accepted by German retailers. You must also label the product in German, show the EU responsible person, and respect the 14-day Widerrufsrecht (withdrawal right) for B2C sales.

Sourcing from China for Germany means managing one of the strictest compliance stacks in the world — CE, REACH, WEEE, EPR (VerpackG/ElektroG/BattG), GPSR 2024, 19% VAT, and German consumer-protection law — on top of the usual sourcing, QC, and shipping.
