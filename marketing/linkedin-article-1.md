## Stat #4: AQL 2.5 Catches 99.5% of Defects

We use the **AQL 2.5 standard** (Acceptable Quality Limit) for all pre-shipment inspections. Here's what that actually means:

- For every 100 units, we inspect a statistically valid sample size
- If defects exceed 2.5%, the entire batch is rejected and reworked
- Result: **99.5% quality acceptance rate** at final delivery

Without QC inspection? Industry data shows **12-15% average defect rate** from Chinese factories on first-run production.

**The math:** On a $10,000 order, skipping QC could mean $1,200-$1,500 in defective products. A QC inspection costs $150-$300. The ROI is 4-10x.

## Stat #5: 50-Piece MOQ Changes the Game

Traditional Chinese factories demand 500-1,000 piece minimums. But for Amazon FBA beginners and TikTok Shop sellers, that's often too much inventory risk.

We've negotiated **MOQs as low as 50 pieces** for our clients by:
- Leveraging our relationship with 75,000+ Yiwu suppliers
- Combining orders from multiple buyers
- Working with factories that specialize in small-batch production

**What this means:** You can test a product with $200-$500 instead of $2,000-$5,000. If it sells, scale up. If it doesn't, you're not stuck with a garage full of inventory.

## Stat #6: 25-35 Days is the New Normal (Sea Freight)

China to US West Coast sea freight transit times:

| Method | Transit Time | Cost (per CBM) | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCL (Full Container) | 25-35 days | Lowest | Bulk orders 15+ CBM |
| LCL (Less than Container) | 30-45 days | Medium | Small to medium orders |
| Air Freight | 5-15 days | High | Time-sensitive |
| Express (DHL/FedEx) | 3-7 days | Highest | Samples, small parcels |

**Pro tip:** The biggest delay isn't the ship — it's poor documentation and customs preparation. A good agent handles this so your cargo doesn't sit at port for 7 extra days.

## Stat #7: ~15% of "Suppliers" on Alibaba Are Not Real Factories

This is the one that surprises people the most.

Based on our factory audits, approximately **15% of suppliers listing themselves as "manufacturers" on B2B platforms are actually trading companies** — middlemen who mark up prices without adding value.

How to identify them:
- ❌ Can't provide production photos with their company name visible
- ❌ Prices are 20-30% above market average
- ❌ Can't customize product specifications
- ❌ Refuse factory visits (or offer a "different" factory)
- ❌ No business license matching their company name

**A real factory audit costs $200-$400.** A bad supplier decision costs $2,000-$10,000+.
