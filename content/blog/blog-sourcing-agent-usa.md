## China Sourcing for US Buyers

China sourcing for US buyers means buying goods from Chinese factories and clearing them through US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) — a process shaped by **Section 301 tariffs** (7.5–25% on top of MFN duty), **US import fees** (Merchandise Processing Fee 0.3464%, Harbor Maintenance Fee 0.125%), and **US product compliance** (CPSC, FCC, FDA). A US-aware China sourcing agent translates factory capability into US-compliant, customs-cleared shipments, handling HS-code classification, compliance verification, Amazon FBA prep, and DDP delivery to US warehouses or FBA centers.

## US Import Cost Components

Landed cost for a US shipment stacks well beyond the EXW factory price. Below are the components for a typical China-to-US import:

 | | Component || Basis / Rate |

 | EXW unit price || Factory price |

 | China inland + export clearance || $80–$300 per shipment |

 | Sea/air freight to US port || $0.50–$9.00/kg by mode |

 | MFN duty || HS-code rate, 0–10% typical |

 | Section 301 tariff || 7.5–25% on goods value (List 1–4) |

 | MPF (Merchandise Processing Fee) || 0.3464% of customs value (min $31.67, max $614.35) |

 | HMF (Harbor Maintenance Fee) || 0.125% of customs value (sea only) |

 | Customs broker / entry || $50–$200 per entry |

 | Inland delivery to warehouse / FBA || $150–$1,200 by zone |

Section 301 tariffs apply to China-origin goods regardless of which port they enter through. For the latest 2026 List 1–4 rates, see our Section 301 tariffs guide.

## US Compliance by Product Category

US product safety is regulated by multiple agencies, and non-compliance leads to seizure, destruction, or CPSC/CBP recalls. The key regimes:

 | | Product || US Regulator || Key Requirement |

 | Toys & children's products || CPSC || CPSIA testing (lead, phthalates), tracking label, Children's Product Certificate |

 | Electronics (RF) || FCC || FCC ID for intentional radiators; SDOC for unintentional radiators |

 | Food contact / cosmetics || FDA || FDA facility registration, Prior Notice, GRAS / cosmetic ingredient review |

 | Medical devices || FDA || 510(k) or Q-Submission, Establishment Registration |

 | Apparel & textiles || CBP / FTC || Country-of-origin label, fiber content, care instructions |

 | Battery / electronics safety || CPSC / UL || UL/ETL listing where required; UN38.3 for shipping |

For electronics, FCC testing must be done by an accredited lab; a factory self-declaration is not enough. See FCC certification from China for the full process.

A US Amazon FBA seller imports 2,000 units of a consumer electronic gadget. EXW China price is **$4.00/unit**, DDP freight to the US is **$1.50/unit**, and the HS code carries a **Section 301 tariff of 25%**.

 | | Cost line || Per unit (USD) || Total (2,000 units) |

 | DDP freight China → US || $1.50 || $3,000 |

 | MFN duty (assume 0%) || $0.00 || $0 |

 | MPF (0.3464% on goods+freight+duty) || $0.02 || $38 |

A product that costs **$4.00** at the factory lands at **$6.80/unit** in the US — a 70% uplift driven almost entirely by the 25% Section 301 tariff and DDP freight. Model this before setting your Amazon price; under-pricing against Section 301 is the most common US launch mistake.

## De Minimis, Importer of Record, and Customs Clearance

 - **De minimis ($800) eliminated for China in 2025** — every China-origin shipment now enters formal customs with duty, MPF, and HMF. Low-value express shipments that previously cleared duty-free are now fully dutiable.
 - **US importer of record (IOR)** — the entity legally responsible for the import declaration, duty payment, and compliance. This can be you (the US buyer) or your agent's US entity. Being IOR means liability for misclassification; choose an agent with a US IOR option for first imports.
 - **DDP vs DAP** — DDP means the shipper (agent) pays duty, MPF, HMF, and clearance to your door; simplest for first imports and Amazon FBA. DAP means you clear and pay yourself, enabling better cost control but requiring a US broker and IOR setup.
 - **USMCA note** — USMCA rules of origin do not apply to China-origin goods; they enter the US under MFN plus Section 301 rates regardless of any USMCA routing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What does a US sourcing agent from China handle?

A US-aware China sourcing agent handles supplier discovery, HS-code classification, Section 301 tariff modeling, CPSC/FCC/FDA compliance verification, US importer-of-record arrangement, DDP shipping to US warehouses or Amazon FBA centers, and AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC. ### 2. How do Section 301 tariffs affect my China imports?

Section 301 tariffs add **7.5–25%** on top of MFN duty for China-origin goods, depending on the HS code's List (1–4). They apply regardless of entry port and cannot be avoided by routing through a third country (transshipment is illegal). See Section 301 tariffs 2026 for current list rates.

### 3. What happened to the $800 de minimis threshold in 2025?

The **$800 de minimis exemption for China-origin goods was eliminated in 2025**. Previously, shipments under $800 entered duty-free with minimal paperwork; now every China-origin shipment is subject to duty, MPF, and HMF through formal customs entry.

### 4. How does US customs clearance work for China imports?

US customs clearance is filed through the **ACE** (Automated Commercial Environment) system. The entry declares HS code, customs value, origin, and applicable duty (MFN plus Section 301), plus MPF (0.3464%) and HMF (0.125% for sea). A licensed US customs broker files the entry; duty and fees must be paid before CBP releases the cargo.

### 5. Can I ship DDP directly to Amazon FBA from China?

Yes. A US-aware agent handles FBA prep (FNSKU label, polybag, case pack), pays Section 301 duty plus MPF and HMF under DDP, and delivers directly to the Amazon fulfillment center. This skips a US middleman warehouse and cuts 3–5 days versus self-routing. See Amazon FBA sourcing.

### 6. Who is the US importer of record?

The US importer of record (IOR) is the legal entity responsible for the customs declaration, duty payment, and compliance — typically the US buyer or the agent's US entity. Being IOR carries liability for misclassification; first-time buyers often use their agent's US IOR option until they set up their own broker and continuous bond.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Accepting a flat DDP rate without line items.** A real DDP quote breaks out freight, duty, VAT, clearance, and last-mile. A single per-kg number hides costs that surface as surprise invoices or low-quality service.
- **Choosing CIF for control of freight.** Under CIF the supplier picks the carrier and inflates freight by 15–25%. Choose FOB (you control the line) or DDP (all-in, your forwarder) — never CIF unless you have no other option.
- **No transit-time buffer for FBA restocks.** Sea freight from China runs 25–40 days + 5–7 days FBA receiving. Order 45–60 days before you need stock in FBA, not 30. Running out of stock kills your Amazon ranking.
- **Ignoring ISPM-15 for wooden pallets.** EU and US customs require heat-treated pallets with the IPPC stamp. Missing stamps = container detained, €400–$600 fumigation, 3–7 day delay.
- **No cargo insurance.** Freight forwarders' liability is limited to ~$2/kg by convention. For high-value cargo, buy separate all-risks insurance (0.3–0.5% of value) — it costs $50 and pays out $10,000.
