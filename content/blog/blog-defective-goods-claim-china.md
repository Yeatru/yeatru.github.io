## What Is a Defective-Goods Claim Against a Chinese Supplier?

A **defective-goods claim** is a formal, documented demand that a Chinese supplier remedy goods that fail your agreed specification after delivery.
It is built on three things: evidence taken before the goods are sorted or resold, a defect rate measured against the contract's **AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor / 0 critical** standard, and a written remedy (rework, replacement, refund or price credit) within the contract's claim window.
A claim is not a complaint — it is a contract action with a number attached.

## The 7-Step Defect Claim Process

Work the claim in this order.
Each step protects the next; skipping one is how a legitimate claim turns into a goodwill negotiation you lose.

 - **Freeze the evidence.** Photograph and count every defect, keep the original cartons and postal labels, and pull the pre-shipment inspection report and AQL card.
Quarantine the defective units — do not sort, repair or resell them before the claim is filed.
Evidence captured before the goods move is what wins; evidence captured later lets the supplier argue the damage happened after arrival.
 - **Notify the supplier in writing.** Send one dated email within 7 days that states the PO number, the measured defect rate, the photo set and the specific remedy you want.
Most contracts set a **7–14 day** window from delivery — late notice can void the claim even at a 12% defect rate.
 - **Classify the defects and fix responsibility.** Sort into critical, major and minor (see [defect classification](blog-defect-classification-qc.html)), compare the rate against AQL 2.5 / 4.0, and separate factory-process faults from transit damage and buyer-caused design faults so responsibility is unambiguous.
 - **Propose a remedy.** Match one of the four remedies to the defect class (see the comparison table below).
This is where you set your anchor, before any negotiation.
 - **Negotiate against the number.** Quote your measured defect rate and cost, not a round percentage.
Suppliers respond to arithmetic, not adjectives.
 - **Escalate.** If the sales rep stalls, move the claim to the factory GM, the owner, or through your sourcing agent — and mention the platform or bank-channel dispute route only as a last resort.
 - **Line up a backup supplier.** Keep an approved second factory in reserve so the negotiation cannot stall your business.
Suppliers negotiate differently when they know you can move.

## Rework vs Replacement vs Refund vs Discount

Most buyers ask for the wrong remedy.
The right choice depends on the defect class, whether the factory can re-run the lot, and how much of the sell season is left.

 |
| Remedy |
| Best When |
| Typical Cost to Supplier |
| Timeline |

| **Rework** |
| Major defects that are repairable (reseam, reprint, re-test); partial lot |
| Labour + parts; usually 5–15% of the defective units' value |
| 3–10 days, then re-inspect |

| **Replacement** |
| Factory can re-run the affected units; season still allows re-production |
| Material + labour for re-run units; factory absorbs freight |
| 10–25 days production + transit |

| **Refund** |
| Critical or unsellable defects; factory cannot re-run in time |
| Full unit value of the defective share, minus recoverable scrap |
| Paid against next PO or bank |

| **Discount / credit** |
| Recoverable cosmetic or minor defects you can still sell |
| 5–25% credit on the defective share |
| Offset against the same or next invoice |

Use a **price credit only for recoverable minor or cosmetic defects**. For critical (safety) defects, insist on refund or replacement, because a discount on a recall-risk item is a discount on a future recall.
When in doubt, see [return & defect policy](blog-sourcing-return-defect-policy.html).

## How to Calculate Your Claim

A fair claim is a sum, not a feeling.
Use this formula:

**Claim = (defective units × unit cost) + rework labour + freight paid on the defective share + re-inspection fee − recoverable scrap value.**

Worked example: a $9,500 order of 5,000 units at **$1.90/unit** with a **12% defect rate**. Defective units = 600.
Base claim = 600 × $1.90 = **$1,140**. Add rework labour (600 units × $0.20) = **$120**, plus your inbound freight share on the defective units (roughly **$180**), plus a re-inspection day (**$160**).
Documented claim value ≈ **$1,600** before negotiation — and you anchor there, not at a round "give me 10%".

Compare that to physically returning the goods: sea-returning 600 units to China adds freight, export re-import duty and clearance that typically **exceed the $1,140 in goods value**. That is why the practical remedies are credits, rework, replacement, or in-market disposal — not a container sailing back.

## The 6 Clauses That Must Be in Every Email and Contract

Your leverage in a defect claim is written down long before the dispute.
Put these six clauses in the contract and refer to them in the claim email:

 - **Acceptance standard:** AQL **2.5 major / 4.0 minor / 0 critical** (ISO 2859-1), Level II sampling, against a signed golden sample.
 - **Claim window:** **7–14 days** from delivery for visible defects, up to 30 days for latent defects, with photo evidence required to open a claim.
 - **Rework at supplier cost:** the factory repairs or replaces defective units at its own cost, and the **re-inspection fee is billed to the supplier**.
 - **Defect-credit table:** a fixed credit per defect class so the remedy is arithmetic, not a negotiation (for example 3% major, 1% minor on the defective share).
 - **Balance hold-back:** the balance is released only after a **Pass pre-shipment inspection**;
no Pass, no payment.
 - **Offset / chargeback right:** you may offset the credit against the next PO invoice, and dispute through Trade Assurance or the card/PayPal channel if the supplier refuses — see [how to pay Chinese suppliers safely](blog-how-to-pay-chinese-suppliers-safely.html).

## When to Walk Away: 6 Escalation Triggers

A claim is worth fighting while the supplier engages in good faith. Move the volume when these six triggers appear.

 - The supplier refuses to acknowledge a documented defect rate at all.
 - The defect recurs after a remedy was already applied to the same process.
 - The supplier asks you to accept a discount on a critical/safety defect.
 - The factory cannot reproduce or explain the defect on the line.
 - The supplier misses your claim window or answer deadlines twice.
 - The defect cost exceeds 15% of order value and the supplier will not offset it.
 - The supplier substitutes a cheaper material or component without approval.
 - Communication stops entirely for more than 5 business days.

## What to Write in the First Claim Email

Keep the first email factual and short. Include these seven items so the supplier cannot claim confusion or delay.

 - PO number, product and the date the goods arrived.
 - The measured defect rate (for example 12% = 600 of 5,000 units).
 - A short table of defects by class with unit counts.
 - Photo evidence attached, named by defect class.
 - The AQL 2.5 / 4.0 standard that the rate breaches.
 - The remedy you are requesting (rework, replacement, refund or credit).
 - A reply deadline (for example 5 business days) and the balance-hold consequence.

## Common Mistakes That Kill a China Defect Claim

 - **Sorting or reselling goods before documenting.** Once the cartons are broken and units sold, you cannot prove the defect rate.
 - **Filing after the claim window.** A 12% defect rate filed on day 25 of a 14-day contract window can be refused outright.
 - **Asking for a round discount.** "Give me 15%" invites a counter at 3%. Quote your measured rate and cost.
 - **Accepting a discount on a critical defect.** A safety defect discounted is a recall you will pay for yourself.
 - **No pre-shipment inspection to begin with.** Without a Pass report you cannot separate factory faults from transit damage — see [PSI in China](blog-pre-shipment-inspection-china.html).
 - **Negotiating only with the sales rep.** The rep has no authority;
escalate to the GM with your number in writing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1.
How long do I have to claim defective goods from a Chinese supplier?

Most China supply contracts set a claim window of **7–14 days from delivery**, or 30 days for latent defects that only appear in use.
Send a written notice within **7 days** of arrival to be safe, and inspect within 48 hours.
A claim filed after the window closes can be refused even when the defect rate is **12%**.

### 2.
Should I ask for a refund or a replacement for defective goods from China?

Ask for a **refund** when the defects are critical or unsellable and the factory cannot re-run the lot; ask for **replacement** when the factory can re-produce the affected units and the season still allows it; ask for **rework** when only a fraction is repairable; and accept a **price credit** only for recoverable cosmetic or minor defects.
Never take a discount for a safety (critical) defect.

### 3.
How do I calculate a fair claim for a defective shipment?

**Claim = (defective units × unit cost) + rework labour + freight you already paid on the defective share + re-inspection fee.** Example: a **$9,500** shipment at $1.90/unit (5,000 units) with a **12% defect rate** (600 units) gives a base claim of 600 × $1.90 = **$1,140**, plus labour and freight, before negotiation.
Anchor to this number, not to a round discount.

### 4.
Who pays return freight for defective goods from China?

The supplier pays according to the contract, but in practice returning goods to China is uneconomic.
For a **$9,500 order**, returning 600 defective units by sea costs more than the goods are worth once freight, duty and re-import clearance are added.
That is why credits, rework or in-market disposal are usually cheaper than a physical return.

### 5.
Can I charge back a Chinese supplier through my payment method?

It depends on how you paid.
Card and PayPal disputes can be filed within **120 days** and **180 days** respectively, but card networks rarely side with the buyer on a quality dispute for physical goods.
**Trade Assurance** covers Alibaba orders up to the order value when you file within the platform window.
Bank transfer (T/T) has no chargeback, which is why the contract and QC report carry the whole burden of the claim.

### 6.
What clauses should be in the contract to support a defect claim?

Six clauses: (1) **AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor / 0 critical** acceptance standard; (2) a **7–14 day** claim window with photo evidence required; (3) rework at supplier cost with the re-inspection fee billed to the supplier; (4) a defect-credit table per class; (5) hold-back of the balance until a Pass pre-shipment inspection; (6) a chargeback or offset right against the next PO.

## Conclusion

Recovering money from a defective shipment from China is a process, not a plea.
Document the defects before you touch the goods, file within the **7–14 day** window, classify against **AQL 2.5**, quote the measured defect rate and cost, then choose the right remedy — rework, replacement, refund or credit — and hold the balance until the fix is verified.
A $9,500 order that could have settled at a 5% discount recovered **18% plus free replacement** because the buyer brought numbers and a deadline, not emotion.
