## What Is an HS Code and Why It Matters

The HS (Harmonized System) code is a 6-digit international classification maintained by the World Customs Organization (WCO) and used by 200+ countries to classify traded goods. China applies the full 10-digit HS code on exports, while the US extends it to a 10-digit HTS (Harmonized Tariff Schedule) code for imports. Getting the right code directly determines your duty rate, your customs clearance speed, and whether your shipment clears the border at all. For importers sourcing from China, the HS code is the single most important number on your commercial invoice — it drives the tariff, the Section 301 surcharge, the MPF fee, and your eligibility for duty drawback. The WTO HS database and the USITC HTS tool are the two authoritative references buyers rely on in 2026.

## Step-by-Step: How to Look Up Your HS Code

Follow these 5 steps to find your HS code in under 5 minutes:

- **Identify your product and material.** Note exact material, function, and end use. A plastic kitchen spoon is classified differently from a stainless steel kitchen spoon.- **Search hts.usitc.gov or the WTO HS database by keyword.** Type "LED light," "cotton T-shirt," or "plastic kitchenware" to find candidate codes.- **Confirm the 6-digit international HS code.** This part is global and recognized by both China customs and US CBP.- **Extend to the 10-digit US HTS code on hts.usitc.gov.** The last 4 digits determine your exact US duty rate and Section 301 treatment.- **Cross-check with your supplier and customs broker.** If your supplier's commercial invoice code differs from your broker's, request written clarification before shipment.

## How to Find Your US Import Tariff Rate

Once you have your 10-digit HTS code, finding the total US import tariff from China takes 4 steps:

- **Look up the MFN duty rate** at hts.usitc.gov for your 10-digit HTS code — this is the base rate.- **Check for the Section 301 additional tariff.** Search the USTR Section 301 list to see if your HTS code is covered, with rates of 7.5%, 15%, or 25%.- **Add CBP MPF at 0.3464%** (ad valorem, min $31.67, max $614.35 per formal entry).- **Calculate total:** Duty = (Customs Value × (MFN% + Section 301%)) + MPF.

In 2026, de minimis treatment for China goods is eliminated — every shipment now requires formal entry, regardless of value. Use the WTO HS database (hs.wcoomt.org) to verify the international 6-digit code and hts.usitc.gov for the US 10-digit rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How do I find my HS code?

Find your HS code by searching the WTO HS database for the 6-digit international code or hts.usitc.gov for the 10-digit US HTS code by keyword. Verify the code against your product's material and function. A sourcing agent can confirm the correct classification within 24 hours.

### 2. What is the difference between HS code and HTS code?

The HS code is the international 6-digit harmonized system used by 200+ countries. The HTS code extends to 10 digits for US imports and adds country-specific tariff lines. For China sourcing, the first 6 digits match globally; the last 4 determine your exact US duty rate.

### 3. How much is the US import tariff from China?

In 2026, US import tariffs on China combine the MFN rate (0% to 32% by product) plus Section 301 tariffs of 7.5% to 25% by HTS code, plus CBP MPF at 0.3464% (min $31.67). Apparel can reach 32% while some toys are 0%. Total duty depends on your HS classification.

### 4. What is Section 301 tariff?

Section 301 tariffs are additional duties the US imposes on Chinese imports since 2018 over intellectual property and technology transfer disputes. In 2026, rates range from 7.5% to 25% by HTS code, applied on top of normal MFN duties. Some categories like certain toys are excluded.

### 5. Can my sourcing agent find my HS code?

### 6. What happens if I use the wrong HS code?

Using the wrong HS code triggers CBP penalties, duty back-payments with interest, shipment delays, and possible seizure. Misclassification, even unintentional, can cost 2 to 4 times the original duty under 19 USC 1592. Always verify with a customs broker or sourcing agent before filing entry.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Accepting a flat DDP rate without line items.** A real DDP quote breaks out freight, duty, VAT, clearance, and last-mile. A single per-kg number hides costs that surface as surprise invoices or low-quality service.
- **Choosing CIF for control of freight.** Under CIF the supplier picks the carrier and inflates freight by 15–25%. Choose FOB (you control the line) or DDP (all-in, your forwarder) — never CIF unless you have no other option.
- **No transit-time buffer for FBA restocks.** Sea freight from China runs 25–40 days + 5–7 days FBA receiving. Order 45–60 days before you need stock in FBA, not 30. Running out of stock kills your Amazon ranking.
- **Ignoring ISPM-15 for wooden pallets.** EU and US customs require heat-treated pallets with the IPPC stamp. Missing stamps = container detained, €400–$600 fumigation, 3–7 day delay.
- **No cargo insurance.** Freight forwarders' liability is limited to ~$2/kg by convention. For high-value cargo, buy separate all-risks insurance (0.3–0.5% of value) — it costs $50 and pays out $10,000.
