## What Is PayPal for China Sourcing?

PayPal for China sourcing means using a PayPal cross-border payment to send money from your PayPal account to a Chinese supplier's PayPal account, usually in **USD**. It is the most buyer-protected payment method available for small China orders, thanks to PayPal's **Purchase Protection** (goods not received / not as described) and an instant transfer. The trade-off is cost — roughly **5.4% all-in** — and the fact that many Chinese factories either cannot accept large PayPal amounts or will not, because of chargeback risk. PayPal is therefore best treated as a **samples-and-small-orders tool**, not a production payment method.

## PayPal Fee Structure for China Payments

When you pay a Chinese supplier via PayPal, three separate charges can apply:

 | | Fee Component || Rate || Notes |

 | Cross-border transaction fee || 2.9% + $0.30 || Standard PayPal fee for receiving goods/services payments. |

 | Currency conversion || ~2.5% above interbank || Applied when your funding currency differs from the supplier's receiving currency (e.g. USD → RMB). |

 | Supplier withdrawal to bank || Free (above threshold) or small flat fee || Chinese suppliers withdraw RMB to their bank; small fees may apply below minimums. |

 | **Total typical cost to supplier** || **~5.4%** || Most suppliers mark up the unit price by 4–6% to absorb this. |

## When to Use PayPal (and When Not To)

**Use PayPal for:**

 - **Samples ($50–$500)** — fast, protected, and the fee is small in absolute terms.
 - **First-time supplier trust-building** — the 180-day protection de-risks a first small order.
 - **Small orders under $3,000** where the $5–$30 fee difference vs T/T is worth the protection.

**Do not use PayPal for:**

 - **Production orders over $3,000** — the ~5.4% fee becomes hundreds of dollars, and most factories refuse.
 - **100% upfront on custom orders** — even with protection, recovery is slow; use [50/50 T/T](blog-50-50-deposit-china.html) with a QC gate instead.
 - **Suppliers with unverified or new PayPal accounts** — fund holds and limits can delay the order.

## PayPal Buyer Protection for China Orders

PayPal's Purchase Protection covers two scenarios: **goods not received** and **goods significantly not as described**. Key rules:

 - Claim window: **180 days** from the date of payment.
 - You must have paid via **Goods and Services** (not Friends & Family).
 - Evidence needed: photos of the received goods vs the agreed spec, the PI/invoice, tracking number, and communication records.
 - Typical resolution time: **10–14 days** after you escalate to a claim.

Protection does not cover ordinary commercial disputes about minor quality variances — you need clear photographic proof of a material discrepancy.

## Worked Example: $800 Sample Payment via PayPal

You order **$800** worth of pre-production samples from a new Shenzhen supplier, payable via PayPal Goods & Services in USD:

 - Transaction fee: **2.9% × $800 + $0.30 = $23.50**.
 - Currency conversion: you pay in USD, supplier's account receives USD — no conversion fee in this case.
 - Supplier receives: **$800 − $23.50 = $776.50**.
 - If the supplier converts to RMB at PayPal's rate, they lose another ~2.5% (~$19.40) on conversion, netting ~**$757**.

For this reason suppliers often quote samples at **$840–$850** (a 5–6% markup) so they net close to $800. Factor this markup into your sample budget.

## Red Flags When Paying by PayPal

 - Supplier asks for payment via **Friends & Family** — this removes your buyer protection entirely.
 - Supplier's PayPal email is a **free personal account** (gmail/qq) not tied to the company domain.
 - Supplier wants to split a large order into multiple PayPal payments to dodge limits.
 - Supplier refuses to provide a PI with the PayPal account name matching the company name.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What are the PayPal fees for paying Chinese suppliers?

Cross-border PayPal fees are **2.9% + $0.30** per transaction plus a currency conversion fee of **~2.5%** (PayPal's spread over interbank), for a total of roughly **5.4%**. On an $800 sample payment the fee is about $23.50. Most Chinese suppliers pass this on by adding 4–6% to the quoted price.

### 2. Does PayPal buyer protection cover China orders?

Yes — PayPal's Purchase Protection covers goods not received or significantly not as described, with a **180-day** claim window from payment. You must pay via "Goods and Services" (not Friends & Family) and provide evidence (photos, PI, tracking). Resolution usually takes 10–14 days.

### 3. PayPal vs T/T — which should I use?

Use PayPal for samples ($50–$500) and small orders under **$3,000** where buyer protection matters more than fees. Use T/T for production orders: it costs only $35–$100 per wire (vs. ~5.4% for PayPal), is universally accepted, and does not expose the supplier to chargeback risk.

### 4. Are there limits on Chinese supplier PayPal accounts?

Yes. Unverified or newly-registered Chinese PayPal accounts can be limited to **$10,000–$60,000** per transaction and may hold funds for 21 days. Many factories refuse large PayPal payments because of chargeback risk and account limitations. Always confirm the supplier's account can handle your amount before paying.

### 5. Can I use PayPal for large production orders?

Not recommended. On a $25,000 production order, PayPal fees alone would be **~$1,350**, and most Chinese suppliers will refuse or add the fee to the unit price. Use T/T with 50/50 terms and AQL 2.5 QC for production; keep PayPal for samples and small trust-building orders.

### 6. Can a supplier get a PayPal refund after I pay?

Once you pay, the supplier cannot refund themselves — only the buyer can open a dispute or the supplier can issue a refund from their PayPal dashboard. If you need your money back, open a dispute within 180 days. If the supplier's account has insufficient funds, PayPal may not be able to recover the full amount.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Wiring to a personal account instead of the company account.** Always confirm the PI beneficiary name matches the supplier's business license. A personal account = red flag for fraud or tax evasion, and you lose all payment protection.
- **No bank-detail re-verification before the balance transfer.** Hackers infiltrate supplier email and send a 'new account' notice mid-order. Re-confirm bank details by phone or WeChat call before every wire over $5,000.
- **Paying 100% upfront without a QC gate.** Structure 50/50 with an AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection before the balance. Once the full amount is paid, you have zero leverage over defects.
- **Ignoring SWIFT cost-sharing (SHA vs OUR).** SHA splits fees ($25–$45 total) but the supplier receives $15–$25 less. OUR costs you ~$55–$60 upfront but guarantees the full amount arrives. For deposits over $10k, OUR avoids short-payment disputes.
- **No written payment terms in the contract.** Specify due dates, currency, bank charges, and late-payment penalties. Verbal agreements are unenforceable in Chinese courts.
