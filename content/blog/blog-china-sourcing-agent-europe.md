## China Sourcing for EU Buyers

China sourcing for European buyers means buying goods from Chinese factories and clearing them into the EU's single market — a process shaped by **EU product compliance** (CE marking, REACH, RoHS, WEEE, EPR, GPSR), **EU customs** (EORI, HS-code MFN duty, member-state VAT of 19-23%), and **port routing** (Rotterdam, Hamburg, Felixstowe, Genoa). An EU-aware China sourcing agent translates factory capability into EU-compliant shipments, handling test reports, EORI registration, DDP/DAP logistics, and VAT recovery so goods clear EU customs on the first attempt.

## EU Compliance: CE, REACH, RoHS, WEEE, EPR, GPSR

EU product law is strict and customs enforces it at the border. The regulations that touch most China imports:

 - **CE marking** — required for electronics, toys, machinery, PPE, and medical devices; needs a technical file and EU Declaration of Conformity. See CE certification from China.
 - **REACH** — chemical safety; SVHC above 0.1% must be disclosed. Common for textiles, plastics, jewelry, electronics. See REACH regulation.
 - **RoHS** — restricts hazardous substances in electrical/electronic equipment.
 - **WEEE** — waste electronics recycling scheme; registration and reporting per member state.
 - **EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility)** — eco-fees on packaging, electronics, and batteries (EUR 0.05-0.80/unit).
 - **GPSR (General Product Safety Regulation, 2024)** — broad safety duty on all consumer products with an EU-based responsible person.
 - **Textile labelling & wood EUTR** — fibre-content labels and FLEGT timber due diligence.

## DDP vs DAP, EORI, and IOSS

 - **EORI** — mandatory for B2B EU imports; one number works EU-wide. Apply in your home member state.
 - **DDP** — shipper delivers duty and VAT paid; simplest for first imports, but the shipper's VAT may be unrecoverable.
 - **DAP** — you self-clear and pay duty/VAT, enabling VAT recovery; needs a broker and EORI.
 - **IOSS** — for B2C distance sales under EUR 150; VAT collected at checkout and remitted via one EU portal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. Do EU imports from China need CE marking?

Most electronics, toys, machinery, PPE, and medical devices sold in the EU need **CE marking** with a valid technical file and EU Declaration of Conformity. Goods without it are detained or destroyed at EU customs. See CE certification from China.

### 2. What is REACH and does it apply to my product?

REACH regulates chemicals in all goods sold in the EU. Any product containing substances of very high concern above 0.1% needs disclosure. Textiles, plastics, jewelry, and electronics commonly require REACH test reports from the factory. See REACH regulation.

### 3. What is EPR and who pays it?

Extended Producer Responsibility makes the importer pay for end-of-life recycling of packaging, electronics, and batteries. You register per EU country, report volumes, and pay eco-fees — typically **EUR 0.05-0.80 per unit** depending on category.

### 4. Do I need an EORI number to import to the EU?

Yes for B2B imports. An EORI (Economic Operators Registration and Identification) number is required for EU customs clearance. Apply in your home member state; one EORI works across the whole EU.

### 5. DDP vs DAP shipping from China to the EU — which?

DDP means the shipper delivers duty and VAT paid to your door — simplest but the shipper's VAT can be unrecoverable. DAP means you clear and pay duty/VAT yourself, letting you recover VAT. Choose DDP for first imports, DAP once you have a broker and VAT recovery set up.

### 6. Which EU port should I route my China shipment through?

Rotterdam for Benelux and pan-EU distribution; Hamburg for Germany/DACH; Felixstowe for the UK post-Brexit; Genoa or Barcelona for Italy/Spain. Route by your warehouse country to minimize inland trucking.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Using the supplier's test report without verification.** Many factories provide fake or expired CE/FCC reports. Verify the report number on the certification body's website before placing a bulk order.
- **Applying the wrong certification for the destination market.** CE for EU, FCC/CPC for US, UKCA for UK, RCM for Australia. Shipping CE-marked goods to the US = customs seizure and $10k+ fines.
- **No test report for the exact product variant.** A CE report for a red 220V version does not cover a blue 110V version. Each variant needs its own test or a report covering all variants.
- **Forgetting ongoing compliance.** Regulations change (e.g., EU GPSR replacing GPSD, US CPSC updates). A cert valid today may be invalid in 12 months. Track renewal dates and budget for re-testing.
- **Under-declaring product category to skip certification.** Listing a children's toy as 'home decoration' to avoid CPC is customs fraud. The buyer of record (you) carries the liability.
