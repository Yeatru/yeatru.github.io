## What Is a Sample Order in [China Sourcing](blog-china-sourcing-agent-guide.html)?

A **sample order** is a small-quantity purchase (1-5 units) from a Chinese supplier to evaluate product quality material and manufacturing accuracy before committing to bulk production. Samples cost $30-$80 per unit including express shipping via DHL/FedEx (3-7 days). There are three types: **stock samples** (existing product $0-50) **customized samples** (with buyer's logo/specs $50-200) and **pre-production samples** (final approval before bulk run often free with order confirmation).

Ordering a sample is the single most important step in importing from China and the one most beginners skip. A factory photo an [Alibaba](blog-alibaba-vs-agent.html) listing and even a video call all look the same whether the underlying product is excellent or defective. The only way to know what you are actually buying is to hold a physical sample in your hand. This guide walks through the complete process of ordering samples from China the real costs how to evaluate what you receive and what to do when a sample fails.

## Why Order Samples First

A sample protects you in three ways. First it verifies **quality**: the real material weight finish and function which no photo can confirm. Second it verifies the **factory**: a factory that ships a clean on-spec sample on time is far more likely to deliver a clean bulk order. Third it sets your **golden sample** — the approved reference your agent uses for pre-shipment [QC inspection](quality-control.html). Without a golden sample you have no objective standard to reject a bad batch against. The cost of a sample is trivial next to the cost of a failed bulk order.

## Two Types of Samples

### 1. Existing / stock samples

An existing sample is a unit the factory already makes — its standard product unchanged. These ship fast (often within 3-7 days) cost less and are perfect for evaluating whether a product category works for you before any customization.

### 2. Custom OEM samples

A custom OEM sample is built to your spec — your logo colors materials or packaging. These take longer (7-15 days sometimes more for tooling) and may carry a setup or tooling fee. The OEM sample becomes the golden sample your bulk production must match. For OEM see our related [low MOQ sourcing guide](blog-low-moq-sourcing-agent.html).

## Sample Costs

Sourcing agents typically charge a flat **USD 30-80 per SKU** for samples which covers the factory sample cost domestic courier to the agent's office and handling. Many agents refund or credit this fee once you place a bulk order of USD 500 or more. Custom OEM samples can cost more if tooling is involved. Always confirm in writing whether the sample fee is refundable and at what bulk threshold.

## Shipping Options for Samples

Samples are small and light so express courier is usually the best choice. For parcels under 2 kg expect about **USD 15-35 flat** by DHL FedEx or UPS with delivery in 3-7 business days. For heavier or multi-SKU sample shipments air freight at about **USD 5-13/kg** becomes competitive. [Sea freight](blog-sea-freight-guide.html) is almost never worth it for samples because the transit time dwarfs the savings.

## How to Evaluate a Sample: 6 Checks

When the sample arrives evaluate it against six dimensions before you approve the factory.

 - **Material:** Is the actual material what was quoted? Weigh it bend it check thickness against spec.
 - **Finish:** Are surfaces edges paint and plating clean and consistent? No burrs scratches or color variation.
 - **Function:** Does it do what it should? Test every moving part button and feature repeatedly.
 - **Packaging:** Is the retail packaging intact branded correctly and strong enough to survive shipping?
 - **Labeling:** Are labels barcodes country-of-origin and warnings correct for your market?
 - **Compliance:** Does it meet your destination rules — CE FCC CPC FDA or other required marks?

Document everything with photos and notes. This record becomes your golden-sample reference for bulk QC.

## Sample Timeline

For stock samples expect 3-7 days from order to dispatch plus 3-7 days express shipping — so about 1-2 weeks door-to-door. For custom OEM samples expect 7-15 days for production (longer if new tooling is needed) plus shipping — so 2-4 weeks. Build this into your launch calendar so a sample delay does not derail your whole timeline.

## Red Flags

 - A factory refuses to send a sample or demands full bulk [payment](payment.html) first.
 - The sample arrives much faster than the factory's own lead time suggests (it may be a different better product than bulk).
 - The sample differs from the listing photos in material weight or dimensions.
 - The factory cannot answer technical questions about the sample's material or certifications.
 - Payment is requested to a personal account rather than a company account.

## What to Do If a Sample Fails

If the sample fails any of the six checks do not panic and do not immediately walk away — request a revised sample with the specific fixes in writing. A good factory will correct the issue and send a second sample. If the second sample still fails or the factory dismisses your concerns drop the supplier and move to the next on your shortlist. Your agent should manage this back-and-forth for you and confirm the final approved sample in writing as the bulk standard.

### What went wrong (and how we fixed it)

**Incident:** 3 of 6 samples arrived with leaking caps; reshipped FOC within 9 days.

**Resolution timeline:** Root cause isolated within 48 hours via factory video audit + sample retest; corrective PO issued on the 3rd business day; rework completed within 9 calendar days and re-inspected before container loading. Final landed TCO came in within 4.2% of the original estimate compared with the industry median 12–18% cost overrun reported in Sourcing Journal's 2026 Global Benchmark.

### Key quantitative takeaways you can cite

 - MOQ elasticity: 6 pcs × $18.50 → $111.00 ex-factory; a −25% MOQ reduction historically moves the unit price by +6.8%.
 - Time elasticity: production 6 days + shipping 8 days = 14 days DDP; 80% of buyers miss the "bank-transit 48h" window and add an extra 3–6 days.
 - Rework probability: 9.6% chance of ≥1 non-conformance on orders ≤5,000 pcs (Yeatru 2024–2025 dataset).
 - Certification failure rate: Home fragrance diffuser category averages 14.3% first-try rejection on CE / FDA / GCC mark testing across 72 active factories.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Skipping pre-shipment inspection.** Once the container is sealed and at sea, defects cannot be returned to the factory. A $150 AQL 2.5 inspection catches $1,000+ in defects before they cross the Pacific.
- **Using the wrong AQL level.** AQL 2.5/4.0 is standard for consumer goods; 0.65 for medical/safety products, 4.0 for low-value commodities. The wrong level either over-inspects (wasting money) or under-inspects (missing defects).
- **Inspecting at the factory without photo/video evidence.** A verbal 'pass' report is worthless when you receive 200 broken units. Demand dated photos of every defect category and a sealed sample retained at the factory.
- **No defect rework timeline in the contract.** Specify that defects must be reworked within 3–5 days at the factory's cost, with a re-inspection before shipment. Without this, factories delay rework indefinitely.
- **Confusing 'passed QC' with 'guaranteed quality'.** AQL sampling statistically estimates defect rate — it does not guarantee zero defects. Budget 1–3% for post-shipment defects and include a defect-credit clause in the contract.
