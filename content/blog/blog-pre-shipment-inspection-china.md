## What Is Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)?

**Pre-shipment inspection (PSI)** is the final on-site quality check performed when production is **100% complete** and at least **80%** of the goods are packed into export cartons. An independent inspector draws a statistically sized sample (AQL) and checks it against the buyer's approved specification, then issues a report with a Pass, Conditional, or Fail decision. PSI is the most common inspection type in China sourcing and the mandatory final gate before the balance payment is released and the container is loaded.

## What PSI Checks: The 10-Item Checklist

A thorough PSI covers ten check areas, each with a clear pass/fail criterion. If any area fails its AQL threshold, the lot fails inspection.

 | | Check Area || What's Inspected || Pass / Fail Criteria |

 | **1. Quantity** || Total cartons and inner-box count vs PI || Count matches ±0%; short-shipment = Fail |

 | **2. Packaging** || Carton condition, sealing, inner packing, polybags || No crushed/damaged cartons; correct packing method |

 | **3. Labeling & marks** || Shipping marks, barcodes, country of origin, language labels || 100% match approved artwork; any wrong label = major defect |

 | **4. Appearance** || Finish, color, print, stitching, cleanliness || Within AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor tolerance |

 | **5. Dimensions & weight** || Measured vs spec tolerances (e.g. ±1 mm, ±5 g) || Within spec tolerance; out-of-tolerance = major |

 | **6. Function test** || Buttons, switches, battery, voltage, moving parts || 100% of sampled units must pass function test |

 | **7. Safety test** || Sharp edges, small parts (toys), torque, drop, leakage || Any critical safety defect = Fail (AQL 0) |

 | **8. Assembly & components** || All parts present, correctly assembled, no missing pieces || Missing component = major defect |

 | **9. Product spec & material** || Material grade, color code, logo, artwork vs golden sample || Matches approved sample; deviation = major |

 | **10. Carton drop test** || Drop from defined height (e.g. 80 cm) on each face || Product and carton survive without damage |

## AQL 2.5 Sampling in PSI

PSI uses the **AQL (Acceptable Quality Limit)** method under ISO 2859-1, almost always with **AQL 0 for critical defects, AQL 2.5 for major, and AQL 4.0 for minor**, under General Inspection Level II. The sample size depends on lot size. For example:

 - Lot 500 units → sample **50**, AQL 2.5 major Ac=3 / Re=4
 - Lot 2,000 units → sample **125**, AQL 2.5 major Ac=7 / Re=8
 - Lot 5,000 units → sample **200**, AQL 2.5 major Ac=10 / Re=11

## How to Book a PSI: 4 Steps

 - **Confirm readiness** — ask the supplier for photos of packed cartons and the total carton count. PSI requires 100% produced and 80%+ packed.
 - **Book 1–3 days ahead** — contact your inspector (third-party or agent). Book 5–7 days ahead in peak season (Oct–Dec) when inspectors are fully scheduled.
 - **Send the spec pack** — proforma invoice (PI), approved golden sample, product spec sheet, defect list, and packaging/labeling artwork. Without these, the inspector can't judge pass/fail.
 - **Confirm the appointment** — inspector name, factory address, date, and meeting point are confirmed 1 day before.

## Inspection Day Procedure

A standard PSI day runs like this:

 - **Arrival & carton count** (0.5–1 hr) — inspector arrives at the factory, counts total cartons vs PI, selects random cartons for sampling.
 - **Sample drawing** (0.5 hr) — inspector randomly selects the AQL sample size from across the batch (not just the front cartons).
 - **Inspection** (4–6 hrs) — checks all 10 areas, classifies each defect critical/major/minor, and takes photos of every defect.
 - **On-site summary** (0.5 hr) — inspector verbally briefs the factory QC manager on findings.
 - **Same-day report** (end of day) — written report with photos, defect counts, AQL comparison, and Pass/Conditional/Fail decision sent to the buyer.

## PSI Report Sections

A usable PSI report has these sections:

 - Order & supplier info, lot size, inspection date, sample size
 - Quantity check result (cartons counted vs PI)
 - Packaging & labeling check with photos
 - Appearance, dimensions, and function test results
 - Defect list with photos, each classified critical/major/minor
 - AQL summary table: Ac/Re per defect class vs actual counts
 - Final decision: **Pass** (all classes ≤ Ac), **Conditional** (minor issues, buyer decides), **Fail** (any class ≥ Re)

## Worked Example: $20k Order PSI — Failure, Rework, Pass

A US Amazon FBA seller orders **5,000 wireless earbuds** at $4.00/unit ($20,000 total), FOB Shenzhen. Payment terms are [50% deposit / 50% balance after PSI pass](blog-50-50-deposit-china.html). The PSI finds **8 major defects** (Bluetooth pairing failure) out of 200 samples. AQL 2.5 major for lot 5,000 (n=200) is Ac=10 / Re=11 — wait, that passes major. But the function test requires 100% pass, and 8 units failed pairing, so it's a **Fail** on function test criteria.

 | | Step || Action || Time || Cost |

 | PSI 1 || 8 major defects (BT pairing fail) → Fail || Day 0 || $180 (agent QC) |

 | Rework || Supplier replaces defective BT modules in affected units || 2 days || $0 (supplier cost) |

 | Re-inspection || 0 major defects → Pass || Day 3 || $0 (billed to supplier) |

 | Balance payment || Released on Pass report; goods shipped || Day 4 || — |

The 2-day rework delay cost the seller a potential Amazon stockout, but catching the pairing failure before shipping avoided a wave of returns and negative reviews that would have cost $10,000+ in lost rank and refunds.

## Why PSI Matters

 - **Catch defects before paying the balance** — with 50/50 terms, the deposit is already paid; PSI is your leverage to force rework before releasing the rest.
 - **Catch defects before shipping** — reworking in China costs a fraction of reworking or returning goods after they arrive at your warehouse.
 - **Photo evidence for disputes** — a PSI report with defect photos is the documentation you need if the supplier disputes the defect claim.
 - **Compliance & safety** — PSI catches wrong labeling, missing certification marks, and safety defects that could trigger customs seizure or product recalls.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is a pre-shipment inspection (PSI)?

A PSI is the final on-site quality check done when production is **100% complete** and at least **80%** of goods are packed. An independent inspector draws an AQL sample, checks quantity, packaging, labeling, appearance, dimensions, function, and safety, then issues a Pass/Conditional/Fail report before the balance payment and shipping.

### 2. When should I do a pre-shipment inspection?

Do PSI when the supplier confirms production is 100% finished and 80%+ of cartons are packed. Book **1–3 working days** before the cargo-ready date, before you pay the balance and before the container loads. PSI should be done on every order over $2,000 and the first order from any new supplier.

### 4. Who pays for the pre-shipment inspection?

The buyer pays for the initial PSI. If it fails, the **re-inspection after rework is normally paid by the supplier** — this should be in the contract. Some suppliers offer "free PSI," but the report is only credible if run by an independent inspector, not the supplier's own staff.

### 5. What is AQL 2.5 in a pre-shipment inspection?

AQL 2.5 allows up to **2.5% major defects** under ISO 2859-1. For lot 2,000 units under Level II, sample size is 125 and Ac=7 / Re=8 for major defects. Critical defects use AQL 0 (one fails the lot); minor defects use AQL 4.0.

### 6. What happens if a pre-shipment inspection fails?

A failed PSI means defect counts reached Re for any class. Standard handling: supplier reworks defective units at their cost and passes a re-inspection. Hold the balance payment until a Pass report. For critical (safety) defects, reject the lot rather than accept with a discount.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Skipping pre-shipment inspection.** Once the container is sealed and at sea, defects cannot be returned to the factory. A $150 AQL 2.5 inspection catches $1,000+ in defects before they cross the Pacific.
- **Using the wrong AQL level.** AQL 2.5/4.0 is standard for consumer goods; 0.65 for medical/safety products, 4.0 for low-value commodities. The wrong level either over-inspects (wasting money) or under-inspects (missing defects).
- **Inspecting at the factory without photo/video evidence.** A verbal 'pass' report is worthless when you receive 200 broken units. Demand dated photos of every defect category and a sealed sample retained at the factory.
- **No defect rework timeline in the contract.** Specify that defects must be reworked within 3–5 days at the factory's cost, with a re-inspection before shipment. Without this, factories delay rework indefinitely.
- **Confusing 'passed QC' with 'guaranteed quality'.** AQL sampling statistically estimates defect rate — it does not guarantee zero defects. Budget 1–3% for post-shipment defects and include a defect-credit clause in the contract.
