## What Is Business License Verification?

Business license verification is the process of checking a Chinese supplier's **营业执照** (business license) against the official government registry at **gsxt.gov.cn** (National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, run by the State Administration for Market Regulation, SAMR). The license image the supplier sends you is just a piece of paper — it can be Photoshopped, borrowed from another company, or revoked since issuance. The gsxt.gov.cn record is the live truth: current registration status, abnormal-operations flags, administrative penalties, and annual report filings. This is the single cheapest, highest-leverage due-diligence step before any deposit; pair it with a [factory audit](blog-china-factory-audit.html) and the [50/50 deposit rule](blog-50-50-deposit-china.html) for full protection.

## The 9 Business License Fields to Check

A Chinese 营业执照 has a fixed set of fields. Below are the 9 you must verify, what to look for, and the red flag in each.

 | | # || Field (CN / EN) || What to Check || Red Flag |

 | 1 || **统一社会信用代码** (Unified Social Credit Code) || 18-character alphanumeric; first digit = registration authority; search it on gsxt.gov.cn || Doesn't resolve on gsxt.gov.cn, or resolves to a different company name |

 | 2 || **注册资本** (Registered Capital) || Real amount & paid-in status; compare to your order size — a $5k order from a ¥10,000-capital "factory" is a red flag || Registered capital under ¥500,000 for a manufacturer, or 实缴 far below 认缴 |

 | 3 || **经营范围** (Business Scope) || List must explicitly cover manufacturing of your product category, not just "trade/consulting" || Scope is "domestic trade" only, or omits your product class (e.g. no "manufacture of plastics" for a plastics supplier) |

 | 4 || **成立日期** (Establishment Date) || Older than 2 years is safer; under 1 year is higher risk for a large order || Company registered in the last 3-6 months while claiming "10 years experience" |

 | 5 || **法定代表人** (Legal Representative) || Name must match the person signing your contract; check they're not on court-dishonesty lists (失信被执行人) || Legal rep is a paid straw-man; many quick-registered shells use proxies |

 | 6 || **登记状态** (Registration Status) || Must be **存续/在营** (active). Walk away if 吊销 (revoked), 注销 (cancelled), or 迁出 (moved out) || Any status other than 存续/在营 — even a 吊销 from 5 years ago means the company cannot legally trade |

 | 7 || **年度报告** (Annual Report Filings) || Filed for each year since establishment on gsxt.gov.cn; missing years = neglect or fraud || No annual report filed for the last 1-2 years |

 | 8 || **行政处罚** (Administrative Penalties) || Clean record preferred; minor consumer-protection fines are common, repeated quality/IP penalties are not || Multiple penalties for false advertising, product quality, or trademark infringement |

 | 9 || **经营异常名录** (Abnormal Operations List) || Must NOT be on this list — being listed means SAMR flagged the company for false address, hidden annual report, or false disclosure || Currently on 经营异常名录 — strong "do not pay" signal |

Fields 1, 6, and 9 are the dealbreakers — if any fails, do not wire money. The rest adjust your risk and negotiation leverage.

## How to Verify a License on gsxt.gov.cn (Step by Step)

 - **Get the license image + unified social credit code.** Ask the supplier to send a clear photo or scan of the 营业执照 (both sides if folded). If they refuse or send a blurry image, treat it as a red flag — see [China sourcing scams](blog-china-sourcing-scams.html).
 - **Go to gsxt.gov.cn.** The site is in Chinese; use Chrome's auto-translate. Enter the company's full Chinese name OR the 18-character 统一社会信用代码 in the search box.
 - **Match the company name exactly.** The gsxt.gov.cn result page shows the registered name — it must match the license image character-for-character, including 注册地 (registered address).
 - **Check 登记状态.** Look for "存续" or "在营" (active). If it says 吊销 (revoked), 注销 (cancelled), or 停业 (suspended), the company cannot legally operate.
 - **Check 经营异常名录.** Click into the company's detail page; the "经营异常" tab shows whether they're listed. A current listing is a hard stop.
 - **Verify the bank account beneficiary.** Cross-check that the bank account the supplier asks you to wire to has a **beneficiary name matching the licensed company name** — the core defense in fake-invoice fraud.

## Worked Example: Catching a Fake Yiwu "Supplier"

A buyer finds a Yiwu "manufacturer" of silicone kitchen tools offering 5,000 custom spatulas at $0.85/pc — 40% below market. The "factory" sends a license image showing:

 - Company name: 义乌市XX硅胶制品有限公司 (Yiwu XX Silicone Products Co., Ltd.)
 - 统一社会信用代码: 91330782MA2XXXXXX9X
 - 注册资本: ¥500,000
 - 经营范围: "塑料制品、五金的销售" (sale of plastics & hardware — note: "sale", not "manufacture")
 - 登记状态 on the image: 存续

**gsxt.gov.cn lookup reveals four hard red flags:**

 - 登记状态 actually shows **吊销, 未注销** (revoked, not yet cancelled) — business license revoked 14 months ago for a false annual report. The image sent to the buyer was an old scan from before revocation.
 - 经营异常名录: **列入** (listed) — flagged twice for "通过登记的住所无法联系" (unreachable at registered address).
 - 经营范围 on the official record is **"销售"** (sales) only — the company is a **trading company, not a manufacturer**. It has no production facility and will outsource to whoever is cheapest.
 - The bank account beneficiary name is **张XX个人** (a personal name), not the licensed company.

Had the buyer wired the 50% deposit of $2,125, recovery would be near-zero. Correct action: decline, find a factory-audited supplier whose gsxt.gov.cn record shows 存续 + 经营范围 covers 制造 (manufacturing) + bank account in the company name, then apply the 50/50 deposit rule.

## Red Flags That Should Stop Payment

 - **License image looks Photoshopped** — fonts inconsistent, seals misaligned, or the 统一社会信用代码 area is blurred. Demand a fresh phone-camera scan of the physical original.
 - **gsxt.gov.cn shows 吊销 or 注销** — the company is no longer legally entitled to trade; the "license" is stale.
 - **On the 经营异常名录** — SAMR flagged them for false address, hidden reports, or false disclosure.
 - **注册资本 under ¥500,000 while quoting a $50,000 order** — shells are often registered at the minimum ¥100k-¥500k to limit liability.
 - **经营范围 says 销售/trade, not 制造** — a trading company masquerading as a factory; expect markup and zero production control.
 - **Bank account in a personal name** — the #1 fake-invoice signature in fake-invoice scams.
 - **Company registered in the last 3-6 months** but claiming "10+ years experience" — a freshly-registered shell.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is the unified social credit code (统一社会信用代码)?

It's an **18-character alphanumeric ID** assigned to every registered Chinese company by SAMR. The first digit identifies the registering authority, the next 8 the region/organization, then a 9-digit body and a check digit. Search it on gsxt.gov.cn to pull the live company record — it's the Chinese equivalent of a tax ID plus a company registry number.

### 2. Is gsxt.gov.cn free to use?

Yes — **gsxt.gov.cn is free and publicly accessible** at no charge, run by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). No account is required to search company records or view registration status, annual reports, abnormal-operations listings, and administrative penalties.

### 3. What if the license image is blurry or won't download?

Treat it as a red flag. Ask for a fresh phone-camera scan of the physical license (both sides, all corners visible). If the supplier refuses or keeps sending a low-res image, they may be hiding a revoked/edited license. Verify the **统一社会信用代码** directly on gsxt.gov.cn regardless of the image — the official record is the truth, the image is just a copy.

### 4. Can a trading company pretend to be a factory?

Yes — and it's extremely common. Check the **经营范围** on gsxt.gov.cn: if it says "销售" (sales) or "贸易" (trade) but not "制造" or "生产" (manufacturing/production) of your product category, the company is a trading company, not a manufacturer. They'll outsource to whichever back-alley workshop is cheapest, with no production control. A factory audit confirms whether the address is actually a working line.

### 5. What does 经营异常名录 (abnormal operations list) mean?

It's a public list SAMR maintains for companies flagged for: (a) being unreachable at their registered address, (b) failing to file annual reports on time, or (c) disclosing false information. A company on this list is restricted from large-scale contracts, bank loans, and government procurement. **If your supplier is currently listed, do not wire a deposit** — it's a strong fraud-adjacent signal.

### 6. How often should I re-verify a supplier?

Re-verify at least **once every 12 months** for repeat suppliers, and immediately before any new large order. License status can flip to 吊销 or 经营异常 between orders, and bank accounts can change. For long-term partners, re-running the gsxt.gov.cn check takes 5 minutes and catches revocations before you wire the next deposit.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Wiring to a personal account instead of the company account.** Always confirm the PI beneficiary name matches the supplier's business license. A personal account = red flag for fraud or tax evasion, and you lose all payment protection.
- **No bank-detail re-verification before the balance transfer.** Hackers infiltrate supplier email and send a 'new account' notice mid-order. Re-confirm bank details by phone or WeChat call before every wire over $5,000.
- **Paying 100% upfront without a QC gate.** Structure 50/50 with an AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection before the balance. Once the full amount is paid, you have zero leverage over defects.
- **Ignoring SWIFT cost-sharing (SHA vs OUR).** SHA splits fees ($25–$45 total) but the supplier receives $15–$25 less. OUR costs you ~$55–$60 upfront but guarantees the full amount arrives. For deposits over $10k, OUR avoids short-payment disputes.
- **No written payment terms in the contract.** Specify due dates, currency, bank charges, and late-payment penalties. Verbal agreements are unenforceable in Chinese courts.
