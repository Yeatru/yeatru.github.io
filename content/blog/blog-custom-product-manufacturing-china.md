## What Is Custom Product Manufacturing?

**Custom product manufacturing** is the full journey of taking a product idea and turning it into mass-produced goods in China. Unlike private label (where you brand an existing product), custom manufacturing means your product is genuinely new — a new shape, new function, new material combination, or all three. The process spans **concept validation, design & engineering (CAD), prototyping, tooling, pilot run, mass production, and QC**. It's more expensive and slower than private label, but it gives you a defensible, patentable product that competitors can't copy overnight.

## The 7 Stages of Custom Manufacturing

- **Concept & market validation** — confirm there's demand, define the target price point, and write a one-page product spec (function, dimensions, materials, differentiator).
- **Design & engineering (CAD + DFM)** — create a 3D CAD model and run **DFM (design for manufacturing)** with the factory to remove un-moldable features. See [product design in China](blog-product-design-china.html).
- **Prototyping** — make 1–50 pcs via 3D printing, CNC, or silicone mold to test fit, function, and aesthetics. Iterate 1–3 rounds.
- **Tooling** — cut the production tool: injection mold for plastic, die-cast for metal, stamping for sheet metal.
- **Pilot run** — produce 50–200 pcs with the final tool and assembly line to validate everything before mass.
- **Mass production** — 15–45 days depending on quantity and complexity.
- **QC & shipping** — run an [AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection](blog-pre-shipment-inspection-china.html) with photo/video, then ship DDP.

## Worked Example: Custom Kitchen Gadget

Let's walk through a custom silicone + plastic kitchen gadget from idea to 5,000 pcs mass production:

- **Concept**: 1 week, $0 (internal) → product spec sheet
- **CAD & DFM**: 3 weeks, $1,500 → 3D model + factory DFM report
- **3D print prototype**: 5 days, **$300** → 3 working samples
- **Silicone mold prototype (2nd round)**: 14 days, $800 → 20 pcs for fit & function test
- **Injection mold (2-part, P20 steel)**: 30 days, **$8,000** → production tool (see mold cost guide)
- **Pilot run**: 10 days, 100 pcs × $8 = **$800** → validate mold & assembly
- **Mass production**: 30 days, 5,000 pcs × $3.50 = **$17,500**
- **QC & shipping (DDP US)**: 7 days, $1,200 inspection + $2,500 freight = **$3,700**
- **Total: ~$32,100** for 5,000 pcs → **$6.42/pc landed**
- **Total timeline: ~6 months**

At a $24.99 retail, that's a ~74% gross margin. The mold ($8,000) is the biggest one-time cost; at 10,000 pcs the per-pc landed cost drops to **$5.62**.

## The Importance of DFM

**DFM (Design for Manufacturing)** is the review of your CAD design against the factory's manufacturing capabilities — specifically injection molding constraints. The goal is to remove or modify features that are expensive or impossible to mold:

- **Undercuts** — require slides or lifters in the mold, adding $500–$3,000 per undercut. Redesign to a straight pull if possible.
- **Wall thickness** — keep it uniform (2–3 mm typical); thick sections cause sink marks and long cycle times.
- **Draft angle** — add 1–2° draft so the part releases from the mold; zero draft means scuffing and stuck parts.
- **Sharp corners** — cause stress concentration and mold cracking; radius all internal corners to ≥0.5 mm.

Why DFM matters: a design change costs **~$50** before the mold is cut. Modifying a cut mold to fix the same issue costs **$5,000–$15,000** and delays 2–4 weeks. DFM is the single highest-ROI step in custom manufacturing.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Skipping DFM** — the #1 cause of mold modifications and 6-week delays.
- **Going straight to mass from prototype** — a 3D-printed prototype behaves nothing like an injection-molded part. Always run a pilot.
- **No mold ownership contract** — you pay for the mold but the factory owns it; switching means re-tooling. See mold cost guide.
- **Under-budgeting tooling** — a $3,000 quote for a complex part usually means cheap P20 steel and short mold life. Budget realistically.
- **Ignoring IP** — file a design patent and sign an NDA before sharing CAD. See [IP protection](blog-ip-protection-china-sourcing.html).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. How do I get a custom product made in China?

The full journey has 7 stages: (1) concept and market validation, (2) design and engineering with DFM, (3) prototyping (3D print / CNC / silicone mold), (4) production tooling (injection mold, die-cast, stamping), (5) 50–200 pc pilot run, (6) mass production, and (7) QC and shipping. Total timeline is **4–12 months** depending on complexity.

### 2. How much does a prototype cost in China?

Prototypes range from **$50 to $2,000+** depending on method and complexity. 3D printing (SLA/SLS) costs $50–$500 for 1–5 pcs in 3–7 days. CNC machining costs $200–$2,000 for 1–10 pcs in 7–14 days. Silicone mold vacuum casting costs $300–$1,500 for 10–50 pcs in 10–20 days and gives production-like plastic parts.

### 3. How much does production tooling (mold) cost?

Injection molds cost **$1,000–$50,000+** depending on part size, complexity and cavitation. Simple parts: $1,000–$3,000. Medium consumer products: $3,000–$12,000. Complex multi-cavity tools: $12,000–$50,000+. Die-cast and stamping tools are in similar ranges. The mold is a one-time cost amortized over production volume. See our injection mold cost guide.

### 4. What is DFM and why is it important?

**DFM (Design for Manufacturing)** is the process of reviewing your CAD design with the factory before tooling to remove features that are expensive or impossible to mold (undercuts, thin walls, sharp corners). A design change costs ~$50 before the mold is cut but $5,000+ to modify the mold afterward. DFM is the single highest-ROI step in custom manufacturing.

### 5. What is a pilot run and do I need one?

A pilot run is a small production batch of **50–200 pcs** made with the final mold and assembly line, before mass production. It validates mold quality, cycle time, defect rate and assembly process. Skipping it risks discovering mold defects in a 5,000-pc mass run, which can cost $20,000+ and delay the launch by 6–8 weeks.

### 6. How long does custom product manufacturing take from idea to shipping?

Total timeline is **4–12 months**: concept 1–2 weeks, design & DFM 2–4 weeks, prototyping 2–6 weeks (1–3 rounds), tooling 15–45 days, pilot run 1–2 weeks, mass production 15–45 days, QC & shipping 5–10 days. Simple products take 4–6 months; complex electronics or multi-part assemblies take 9–12 months.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **No written mold/drawing ownership contract.** By default Chinese law treats the factory as the mold owner. Your contract must state you own the mold, drawings, and tooling upon full payment, with retrieval rights.
- **Signing no NDA before sharing designs.** A factory can legally sell your custom design to competitors if there's no NDA. Sign before sending any CAD files, samples, or spec sheets.
- **Approving production without a signed golden sample.** A verbal 'looks good' leads to 5,000 units that don't match your expectation. Sign and retain a golden sample; production must match it exactly.
- **No MOQ flexibility in the contract.** A 5,000-unit MOQ for your first order is risky. Negotiate a 1,000–2,000 trial MOQ at a slightly higher unit price, then scale up after quality is confirmed.
- **Skipping IP registration in China.** Register your trademark and design patent in China (not just your home country). Chinese trademark law is first-to-file — a factory or competitor can squat your brand if you don't register first.
