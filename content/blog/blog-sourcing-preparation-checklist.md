## What Is Sourcing Preparation?

**Sourcing preparation** is the work you do *before* sending the first message to a Chinese supplier: defining the product, budget, volume, compliance, and logistics so the supplier can quote accurately. Suppliers receive 20-50 RFQs per day; vague requests get a generic high price or no reply, while a complete, professional RFQ gets fast, accurate quotes and signals you are a serious buyer. Preparation also protects you from the most common China-sourcing losses — wrong product, wrong MOQ, missing certifications, and surprise landed costs.

## The 10-Item Preparation Checklist

Complete all 10 items before sending your RFQ. Each one directly affects quote accuracy and your ability to negotiate.

 | | # || Item to prepare || What to include || Why it matters |

 | 1 || Product spec sheet || Dimensions, material, tolerance, color (Pantone), labeling, weight || Eliminates wrong-product quotes |

 | 2 || Target price + landed budget || Max EXW unit price + freight/duty/fee budget || Locks the right volume tier; avoids overpaying |

 | 3 || MOQ target || 10-100 stock / 300-1,000 OEM per SKU || Sets pricing and tooling expectations |

 | 4 || Reference samples/photos || 3-6 photos or a physical sample || Gives the factory a visual anchor |

 | 5 || Certifications || CE/UKCA, FCC/FDA, RoHS, CPSIA by destination || Prevents non-compliant shipments |

 | 6 || Packaging requirements || Retail box, polybag, inserts, barcode, FBA prep || Avoids repackaging costs at destination |

 | 7 || Target delivery date || Must-arrive date + buffer (7-14 days) || Determines production + shipping mode |

 | 8 || Annual volume estimate || Realistic monthly/annual units || Gets volume pricing and priority |

 | 9 || Shipping destination + incoterm || Country/port + FOB/EXW/DDP || Lets supplier quote freight accurately |

 | 10 || Payment terms you accept || 50/50 T/T, PayPal for samples, XTransfer || Sets expectations; avoids 100% upfront |

For incoterms, see [FOB vs EXW vs DDP](blog-fob-vs-exw-vs-ddp.html); for safe payment, [how to pay Chinese suppliers](blog-how-to-pay-chinese-suppliers-safely.html).

## RFQ Prep Template

Copy this structure into your supplier message. A complete RFQ gets a real quote in 24-48 hours; a vague one gets ignored or inflated.

**RFQ template:**

Hi [factory name],

 We are sourcing [product] for [market]. Please quote based on:

 - Product: [name], dimensions [LxWxH], material [type], color [Pantone], [certifications]

 - Quantity: [MOQ] pcs first order, annual [volume] pcs

 - Target EXW price: $[unit] (budget $[total])

 - Packaging: [retail box / polybag], with [barcode/FNSKU]

 - Destination: [country], incoterm [FOB/EXW/DDP], target delivery [date]

 - Payment: T/T 50/50 after QC

 Please send PI, lead time, and sample cost. Thank you.

## Worked Example: Prepared vs Vague Request

Two buyers contact the same Yiwu silicone-kitchen-tool factory on the same day.

 | | Outcome || Prepared buyer (full spec sheet) || Vague buyer ("send me price") |

 | Quote received || 18 hours || 4 days (and 3 follow-ups) |

 | Unit price quoted || $2.10 (correct volume tier) || $3.40 (first-timer/inflated) |

 | Product correctness || Matches spec exactly || Wrong material (generic TPE instead of food-grade silicone) |

 | Certifications || FDA + LFGB test reports attached || No certs mentioned |

 | MOQ || 500 pcs (negotiated from 1,000) || 1,000 pcs (no flexibility) |

 | Sample cost || $25 + free shipping || $80 + shipping |

The prepared buyer's **$2.10** quote on 500 pcs = $1,050; the vague buyer's **$3.40** on 1,000 pcs = $3,400 — and the vague buyer also received the wrong material. Preparation here saved **69% per unit** and avoided a non-compliant, non-saleable shipment.

## Common Preparation Mistakes

 - **Vague specs** — "a silicone spoon" vs "food-grade LFGB silicone spoon, 22 cm, Pantone 286C blue, FDA-compliant". Vague specs = wrong product or inflated price.
 - **No target price** — without a target, suppliers quote their highest tier and you have no benchmark to negotiate down.
 - **No reference sample** — a photo or physical sample cuts interpretation errors dramatically. "Like this photo" works better than a paragraph of text.
 - **Wrong MOQ** — asking for 100 pcs of a custom-mold product wastes time; the real OEM MOQ is 500-1,000. Know your product's MOQ range first.
 - **Ignoring certifications** — discovering your electronics lacks FCC after it lands in the US means seizure, re-testing ($300-2,000), or destruction.
 - **Forgetting incoterm** — an EXW quote looks cheap until you add $1-3/kg freight and duty. Always specify FOB or DDP for apples-to-apples comparison.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What should I prepare before contacting Chinese suppliers?

Prepare 10 items: product spec sheet (dimensions, material, tolerance, color, labeling, certification), target price and landed-cost budget, MOQ target, reference photos/samples, required certifications (CE/FCC/RoHS/FDA), packaging requirements, target delivery date, annual volume estimate, shipping destination with incoterm, and payment terms you can accept.

### 2. What goes in a product spec sheet?

Include product name, dimensions with tolerance, material and grade, color (Pantone code), weight, labeling/logo requirements, certifications needed, and 3-6 reference photos. A 1-2 page spec sheet with photos is enough; a full 3D drawing is ideal for custom parts.

### 3. How do I set a target price for China sourcing?

Work backward from your sell price: sell price minus your target margin, minus freight, duty, Amazon/payment fees, and returns allowance = max EXW unit price. Share this target with suppliers so they quote the right tier instead of guessing high.

### 4. What MOQ should I target for a first order?

For stock/RTS goods, MOQ is **10-100 pieces**. For OEM private label, expect **300-1,000 pieces** per SKU. A first-time buyer should target 100-500 pieces to test demand before committing to a larger MOQ.

### 5. What certifications do I need to prepare?

By destination: CE (EU general), UKCA (UK), FCC (US electronics), FDA (US food contact/medical), RoHS (electronics EU/US), CPSIA (US children's products), CPC (US children's). List these in your RFQ and require test reports before production.

### 6. How do I write an RFQ that gets good quotes?

Send the spec sheet, target price, MOQ, annual volume, certifications, packaging, destination incoterm, and target delivery date. Suppliers respond faster and quote more accurately when the RFQ is complete — vague requests get inflated or wrong quotes.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Using the supplier's test report without verification.** Many factories provide fake or expired CE/FCC reports. Verify the report number on the certification body's website before placing a bulk order.
- **Applying the wrong certification for the destination market.** CE for EU, FCC/CPC for US, UKCA for UK, RCM for Australia. Shipping CE-marked goods to the US = customs seizure and $10k+ fines.
- **No test report for the exact product variant.** A CE report for a red 220V version does not cover a blue 110V version. Each variant needs its own test or a report covering all variants.
- **Forgetting ongoing compliance.** Regulations change (e.g., EU GPSR replacing GPSD, US CPSC updates). A cert valid today may be invalid in 12 months. Track renewal dates and budget for re-testing.
- **Under-declaring product category to skip certification.** Listing a children's toy as 'home decoration' to avoid CPC is customs fraud. The buyer of record (you) carries the liability.
