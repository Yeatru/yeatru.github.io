## Why Belgium is harder than it looks

Belgium is one market with three languages and a battery regime that catches most first-time importers. A shipment that is fine in the Netherlands can be non-compliant in Belgium for language or battery-registration reasons alone.

### Three languages

Consumer information has to be in the language of the region where the product is sold: Dutch in Flanders, French in Wallonia and Brussels, German in Ostbelgien. One label with all three is the safe default for a national launch.

### Battery and WEEE EPR

Rechargeable devices and anything with a battery need separate producer registration — Bebat for batteries and Recupel for waste electrical equipment. This applies to a USB wudu washer or a Bluetooth ring just as much as to a power tool.

### Radio and electrical rules

A Bluetooth ring is radio equipment under the RED directive; a USB-powered device falls under EMC and low-voltage rules. CE marking, a test report in your own name and a declaration of conformity travel with the goods.

### Why is the service fee 10% and not 5–8%?

   The published rate is 5–8% for regular sourcing and 8–12% for OEM and private-label work, and orders under USD 1,000 carry a 10% low-order rate. This order fell under that threshold, so 10% applied with no separate handling surcharge added on top.

### Do rechargeable products need extra registration in Belgium?

   Yes. Batteries fall under the Bebat producer-responsibility scheme and electrical and electronic equipment under Recupel. A Bluetooth product is also radio equipment under the RED directive, so CE marking with a test report in your name and a declaration of conformity are required. These obligations sit with the importer, not the Chinese factory.

### Is sea freight cheaper than air for a 120-piece order?

   Almost always, in cost per kilogram, and it is what we recommend when the launch date allows it. Air is chosen when the buyer needs stock on shelf within days rather than weeks. For a 31 kg basket, sea LCL plus destination charges usually beats air express on cost but adds roughly five to seven weeks door to door.
