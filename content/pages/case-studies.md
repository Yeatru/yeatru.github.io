## US client: first product for an Amazon Europe brand

A US-based seller launching a new brand on Amazon Europe needed one product taken from project set-up to a shipped batch before widening the range.

### Brief

- Product: entry doormat, first SKU of a new Amazon Europe brand
- Scope: project set-up, specification alignment, factory coordination, pre-shipment inspection and export shipment
- Requirement: a batch that could be inspected on the factory floor before the balance payment

### What Yeatru delivered

- **Project set-up:** product brief, target specification and inspection points agreed in writing before sampling.
- **Factory coordination:** specification and artwork checked against the factory's actual capability, not against a catalogue photo.
- **On-site inspection:** production run inspected at the doormat factory with dated photographs and a written report. The inspection gallery is published on the [quality control page](quality-control.html#inspection-photos).
- **Shipment:** export documents prepared and the batch shipped to the client.

### Verified order data

- Product: microfibre entrance mat, model PC00010, 60 × 90 cm, 10 mm thick, sand mélange (colour matched to the approved sample)
- Quantity: **500 pieces**
- EXW unit price: **USD 4.00**
- Line items on the proforma invoice (DF260812, dated 12 Aug 2026): product USD 2,000.00 · 500 custom wash labels USD 25.00 · delivery to Guangdong export hub USD 135.00
- Proforma total: **USD 2,160.00**
- Payment term: balance settled before shipment (after the pre-shipment inspection was approved)

### Outcome

The first SKU was produced, inspected and shipped — giving the brand a real product to launch with before committing to a larger range.

## Lithuania brand owner: developing the brand's first product, from spec sheet to shipment

A Lithuanian founder building his own brand from zero needed a first product he could actually manufacture. Yeatru developed that first product with him — specification, label artwork, production follow-up and shipment — not a private-label rebadge of an existing catalogue item.

### Brief

- Client type: brand owner / founder, not a distributor
- Product: a multi-item set sold under the client's new own brand — the brand's first product
- Scope: product specification, label artwork, production follow-up and shipment
- Requirement: one document set that the factory, the printer and the founder could all work from

### What Yeatru delivered

- **Spec sheet:** materials, dimensions, quantity per set and packing written by our team so every supplier quoted the same product.
- **Label design:** label artwork prepared in-house for the client's brand, then checked against the packaging before printing.
- **Production follow-up:** factory liaison through production and packing.
- **Shipment:** the finished set shipped to the client.

### Verified order data

- Set series: **YCS-LTW-902A** — a 7-component set for the client's own brand, all components in black
- Components specified by Yeatru (component code / description): YCS-LTW-902A-001 stainless-steel piece (58 × 2 pcs, 1.2 mm) · YCS-LTW-902A-002-1 reusable DIY FRESH seal bags · YCS-LTW-902A-002-P 1200 ml stand-up pouch · YCS-LTW-902A-003 pack of 20 · YCS-LTW-902A-003-P 210D small thin drop bag · YCS-LTW-902A-004-P 210D large bag (30 × 35 cm) · YCS-LTW-902A-005 organizer pouch
- Spec sheet delivered: table with component, image, description, specification, colour and per-set quantity — the single document the factory, the printer and the buyer all worked from
- Label artwork: contents list plus a safety warning block. The warning states the set is not a toy, activated-charcoal pouches must not be opened or ingested, and the product must be kept away from fire

### Outcome

The brand's first product was produced and shipped with Yeatru's own specification and label documents, which remain the reference for the founder's repeat orders.

## Norway client: sloth-shaped plush toy that hangs around the neck

A Norwegian buyer wanted a sloth plush designed to be worn around the neck, with the weight built into the arms so it hangs correctly. The brief was a full specification, not a catalogue pick. The order has been produced and shipped.

### Verified order data

- Unit price: **USD 7.26 per piece, EXW** — freight excluded, based on the buyer's full specification including the weight in the arms and the dimensions
- Packaging: standard vacuum packing in cartons of 55 × 40 × 50 cm; the buyer asked for a simple dust bag, no logo
- Destination: Son, Norway — a DDP price was quoted alongside the EXW price, calculated separately because the shipment is charged on volumetric weight
- Status: produced and shipped

### What has to be right before production

- EN 71-1/-2/-3 test reports and CE marking: the toy is aimed at children, so the small-parts and seam-strength tests matter most on a neck-worn item
- GPSR safety information and warnings, supplied in Norwegian for the EEA market
- Norway is in the EEA but outside the EU customs union, so DDP is quoted as its own leg with 25% Norwegian import VAT — not the same calculation as an EU shipment

### Why this case matters

A plush toy is not a commodity order when it has to hang on a body: arm weighting, seam strength and the strap or loop attachment all change the specification and the unit price. The EXW quote is fixed; the DDP freight estimate is calculated separately because the shipment is charged on volumetric weight, not actual weight.

## Australia client: one sourcing channel across a hair-extension salon and a garden-décor wholesale business

An Australian client runs two businesses — a hair-extension salon and a garden-décor wholesale operation — and in September 2026 sent three separate quotation lists through the same account. The lines do not resemble each other at all: salon consumables, décor, ceramics, planters, even instruments.

### What has already shipped under this account

- **Salon side:** wigs, hair accessories (hair ties, extension-tying rings, clips, durags), jewellery and necklace lines, hair tools and salon equipment such as the hair-dressing trolley
- **Wholesale side:** outdoor and indoor home décor — faux cowhide rugs, lanterns, woven baskets and wall hangings, coconut-shell and acacia tableware, ceramic urns and vases, travertine and magnesium-oxide planters, display risers
- Two unrelated inventories, one supplier channel, one consolidation point

### Verified order data — three proforma invoices

- **PI YCS260908A2** (8 September 2026), salon side: hair ties and hair-extension tying rings in 10 cm / 11 cm, pearl necklaces, durags, 14 cm hair clips, and a hair-dressing trolley (40 × 28 × 86 cm, 15 kg). Goods USD 880.85 + 7% service fee USD 61.66 = USD 942.51.
- **PI YCS260923A2** (23 September 2026), décor side: faux cowhide rugs in eight sizes from 80 × 120 cm to 200 × 300 cm, a rechargeable antique-bronze lantern, conga drum sets (acacia, Toona wood and rubberwood, 10" + 11", some with REMO heads and stands), water-hyacinth and seagrass baskets, a 3.5 m artificial coconut tree and a faux sago palm.
- **PI YCS260928A2** (28 September 2026), expanded list: the same décor lines plus seagrass trivets in seven diameters, coconut-shell bowls, an acacia cake stand, a full ceramic range (urns, vases, planters, pastry stands, decorative teapots and a trophy), travertine outdoor pots by weight, magnesium-oxide and fibreglass planters up to 90 cm, foldable display risers for exhibitions, woven-grass wall hangings and wooden storage boxes.

### Why this account is the point

- **The categories do not share a compliance profile.** Salon consumables, glazed ceramics, stone planters and a wooden drum cannot share one label, one test report or one HS code.
- **They do not share freight economics either.** Hair accessories are light and high-value; travertine pots are quoted by the tonne and priced on weight; artificial trees and display risers are volumetric.
- **One account still consolidates.** Supplier vetting, quotation, QC booking and the Yiwu consolidation warehouse are the same process for all three lists — which is exactly why a sourcing agent is not a category specialist.

### Why this case matters

This is the clearest answer to "what products do you source?": whatever the buyer's business already sells. Yeatru did not choose hair or garden décor as a specialty — the client's two businesses did, and the same sourcing process covered both.

## Case 1: Amazon FBA Small-Batch Sourcing

Buyer profile: First-time Amazon seller, US-based, sourcing a home & kitchen product with no existing China supplier relationship.

### What the buyer needed

- Identify 3–5 real manufacturers (not trading companies) for a kitchen gadget
- Confirm MOQ under 500 pieces for first test order
- Pre-shipment QC before goods leave China
- FBA-ready packaging with FNSKU labels applied
- DDP shipping to an Amazon US fulfillment center

### Yeatru process

- **Sourcing:** Market visit in Yiwu + online factory outreach. Collected 8 quotes, filtered to 4 factories with valid business licenses (SAMR verified).
- **Sample:** Ordered samples from 3 factories. Tested for material, function, and packaging quality.
- **Negotiation:** Compared quotes; negotiated MOQ and unit price with the selected factory.
- **Production follow-up:** Scheduled in-line check and pre-shipment inspection at the factory.
- **QC:** AQL 2.5 inspection — checked product function, labeling, packaging, and count. Defects documented with photos.
- **FBA prep:** Applied FNSKU labels, bubble-wrapped fragile items, palletized per Amazon requirements.
- **Shipping:** DDP sea freight to Amazon US warehouse with customs clearance and duty prepaid.

### Outcome

Buyer received FBA-ready inventory. Inspection report and shipping documents provided. Follow-up reorder placed after successful Amazon listing launch.

## Case 2: Pre-Shipment Quality Issue Resolution

Buyer profile: DTC brand owner who had already paid a deposit to a factory before engaging Yeatru for QC only.

### The problem

The buyer ordered a branded consumer product. Before final payment and shipment, they requested a third-party pre-shipment inspection.

### Inspection findings

- AQL 2.5 sampling at the factory warehouse
- Defects found: inconsistent logo printing (offset), color mismatch vs approved sample, and 5% of units missing accessories
- Defect rate exceeded AQL 2.5 acceptance threshold

### Resolution

- Detailed QC report with photos sent to buyer and factory
- Negotiated with factory: sorting of affected batch, re-printing of defective units, replacement of missing accessories
- Re-inspection after factory rework
- Release for shipment only after second inspection passed

### Outcome

Buyer avoided receiving defective inventory. The cost of two inspection days was a fraction of what a return, rework, or Amazon suspension would have cost. Buyer now includes pre-shipment QC in every PO.

## Case 3: Multi-Supplier Consolidation Shipping

Buyer profile: Wholesale importer building a mixed-SKU order from 6 different Yiwu suppliers.

### The challenge

Sourcing from multiple suppliers means multiple shipments, higher per-unit freight cost, and customs complexity for each separate container or LCL shipment.

### Yeatru solution

- Coordinated 6 suppliers to deliver goods to Yeatru's Yiwu consolidation warehouse
- 15-day free warehousing while all suppliers delivered
- Checked each supplier's goods against PO upon receipt (quantity, packaging, labels)
- Consolidated all goods into one LCL shipment
- Single customs declaration and DDP delivery to buyer's warehouse

### Result

Buyer received one consolidated shipment with one set of documents instead of six separate shipments. Reduced shipping paperwork, customs fees, and last-mile delivery complexity.
