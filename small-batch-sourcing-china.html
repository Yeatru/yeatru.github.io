Where small batches go wrong — and what we check

Most first-order losses are not price problems. They are specification, labelling and compliance problems.

Compliance route

Before production we confirm which rules apply to the destination: CE marking for the relevant directives, REACH restricted substances, EN71 for toys, UKCA for Great Britain and GPSR for the EU. GPSR, in force since 13 December 2024, also requires an EU-based responsible person and traceability details on the product or packaging — a requirement many first-time sellers discover at customs.

Label and artwork

Symbols, warnings, material composition, country of origin, batch code and importer details have to fit on a small label without losing legibility. We review the artwork before the print run, because reprinting labels costs more than the labels.

Material and safety

A factory's standard material is not always the material your market accepts. We flag food-contact, children's-product and chemical restrictions early and coordinate the lab tests the factory must supply — the importer of record still holds legal responsibility.

Tolerances and packaging

Colour drift against an approved sample, size tolerance, carton drop strength and net weight are checked against the spec sheet, not against memory. On a 50-unit run one bad carton is 2% of your stock.
