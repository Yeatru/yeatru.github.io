China Sourcing Agent for Canadian Buyers

China sourcing agent for Canadian buyers: CBSA customs, GST/HST 5-15%, SOR/2016-174, Health Canada, ISED electronics, Vancouver/Montreal routing, and a CAD $8.20/unit landed example.

Key Takeaways

  • Canada imports need a Business Number (BN) with import/export RM account and clearance through CBSA (Canada Border Services Agency) — without a BN, the shipment is held at the port.
  • Canada's GST is 5% federally, with HST 13–15% in Ontario and Atlantic provinces; GST/HST applies to the duty-paid value (goods + freight + duty). GST-registered buyers can recover it as input tax credits.
  • Consumer products must meet SOR/2016-174 (Canada Consumer Product Safety Regulations); electronics need ISED certification (Canada's FCC-equivalent); cosmetics and medical need Health Canada approval.
  • Canada's de minimis is only CAD $20 for duty and CAD $20 for tax — almost all commercial China shipments exceed it and are fully dutiable.
  • Route via Vancouver (West), Montreal (East/Quebec), or Toronto (Ontario, by rail) to match your warehouse. Compare with US sourcing and EU sourcing.

China Sourcing for Canadian Buyers

China sourcing for Canadian buyers means buying goods from Chinese factories and clearing them through the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) — a process shaped by Canadian customs (BN import account, HS-code MFN duty), GST/HST (5% federal plus provincial HST of 13–15% in participating provinces), and Canada product compliance (SOR/2016-174 consumer product safety, Health Canada, ISED for electronics). A Canada-aware China sourcing agent handles BN guidance, compliance verification, GST/HST modeling, DDP/DAP logistics, and port routing to Vancouver, Montreal, or Toronto.

A product that costs $5.00 at the factory lands at $8.20/unit in Canada — a 64% uplift from freight, duty, GST, and inland delivery. In Ontario, the 13% HST would push the landed cost to roughly $8.64/unit. GST-registered buyers can recover the GST/HST as input tax credits, improving cash flow.

GST/HST, BN, and DDP vs DAP

  • Business Number (BN) — mandatory for every CBSA import declaration. Apply through the CRA; add the RM (import/export) program account before your first shipment.
  • GST/HST — 5% GST federally, 13% HST in Ontario, 15% in Atlantic Canada; Alberta and territories use 5% GST plus provincial sales tax. GST-registered importers self-assess and recover it as an input tax credit.
  • DDP — the shipper (agent) pays duty, GST/HST, and clearance to your door; simplest for first imports.
  • DAP — you clear with your own BN and broker, paying duty and GST/HST yourself; enables input tax credit recovery but adds paperwork.

Port Routing: Vancouver, Montreal, Toronto

Match the port to your warehouse to minimize inland trucking (which can add CAD 300–1,500 per shipment):

  • Vancouver — default for British Columbia and Western Canada; 14–18 days sea from Shanghai/Ningbo; Prince Rupert is a congestion overflow.
  • Montreal — for Quebec and Eastern Canada; 25–32 days (often via Halifax trans-shipment).
  • Toronto — for Ontario; reached by rail from Vancouver or Montreal, adding 3–7 days of inland transit.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does a Canada sourcing agent handle?

A Canada-aware China sourcing agent handles supplier discovery, HS-code classification for the Canada Customs Tariff, SOR/2016-174 and Health Canada/ISED compliance verification, GST/HST modeling, DDP or DAP shipping to Vancouver, Montreal or Toronto, and AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC. They turn a factory EXW price into a fully cleared landed cost per unit.

2. How does GST/HST work on Canada imports from China?

GST is 5% federally, and HST ranges 13–15% in participating provinces (Ontario 13%, Atlantic 15%), while Alberta and the territories use 5% GST plus provincial sales tax. GST/HST is levied on the duty-paid value (goods plus freight plus duty). GST-registered importers can claim input tax credits to recover it.

3. How does CBSA customs clearance work?

CBSA clearance is filed on Form B3 using the importer's BN. The entry declares HS code, customs value (transaction value), origin, and applicable duty under the Canada Customs Tariff. Duty plus GST/HST must be paid before release; most entries are now filed electronically through the CBSA Assessment and Revenue Management (CARM) portal.

4. What is SOR/2016-174?

SOR/2016-174 is the Canada Consumer Product Safety Regulations, which set mandatory safety requirements for a wide range of consumer products including toys, children's sleepwear, cribs, and household chemicals. Products that fail the prescribed tests can be detained or recalled by Health Canada.

5. What is Canada's de minimis threshold?

Canada's de minimis threshold for duty is CAD $20 and for tax is CAD $20 (or CAD $40 for goods shipped by post where certain conditions apply). Shipments above these thresholds are subject to CBSA duty and GST/HST. For business imports, de minimis rarely applies since most commercial shipments exceed CAD $20.

6. Vancouver vs Montreal — which port should I use?

Use Vancouver for British Columbia and Western Canada (14–18 days sea from Shanghai/Ningbo); use Montreal for Quebec and the Maritimes (25–32 days, often via Halifax trans-shipment); route to Toronto via rail from Vancouver or Montreal for Ontario. Match the port to your warehouse to minimize inland trucking, which can add CAD 300–1,500 per shipment.
