China Sourcing for Latin American Buyers

China sourcing for Latin American buyers means buying goods from Chinese factories and clearing them through the complex customs and tax regimes of LATAM — shaped by high and layered taxes (Brazil’s IPI+ICMS stack of 60–100%), mandatory certification (INMETRO in Brazil, NOM in Mexico), Portuguese/Spanish documentation, and currency controls in Argentina and Brazil. A LATAM-aware China sourcing agent handles certification verification, certified translations, currency management, DDP shipping to major LATAM ports, and the full tax-stack landed-cost model.

| Brazil || II 0–35% + IPI 0–30% + ICMS 17–25% + PIS/COFINS (~60–100% combined) || Santos / Itajaí || INMETRO |

| Mexico || 0–15% duty + 16% IVA || Manzanillo / Veracruz || NOM |

| Colombia || 0–15% duty + 19% IVA || Buenaventura / Cartagena || ICONTEC |

| Chile || 0–6% duty + 19% VAT || San Antonio / Valparaíso || SERNAPESCA / INN |

For electronics certification, see CE certification and FCC certification — INMETRO and NOM draw on similar EMC and electrical-safety test data.

Worked Example: Brazilian Buyer, 2,000 Electronics Units

A Brazilian buyer imports 2,000 consumer electronics. EXW China price is $10.00/unit, DDP freight to Santos is $3.00/unit, IPI is 15%, ICMS is 18%, and INMETRO certification is required.

| | Cost line || Per unit (USD) || Notes |

| EXW goods || $10.00 || Factory price |

| DDP freight China → Santos || $3.00 || Incl. clearing |

| II import duty (assume 12%) || $1.20 || On goods value |

| IPI (15% on goods+II) || $1.68 || Federal tax |

| PIS/COFINS (~9.25%) || $1.03 || Federal social contribution |

| ICMS (18%, state VAT) || $2.87 || On goods+II+IPI+PIS |

| INMETRO cert + broker || $0.50 || Per unit amortized |

| Total landed (USD) || $20.28 || ≈ BRL 65 @ ~5.2 BRL/USD |

A product that costs $10.00 at the factory lands at roughly BRL 65/unit in Brazil — more than double the factory price due to the II+IPI+PIS/COFINS+ICMS tax stack. Brazil’s tax load is the highest in LATAM and the main reason importers model landed cost before committing. Clearance takes 30+ days, so order 8–12 weeks ahead.

INMETRO, NOM, and Language Requirements

  • INMETRO (Brazil) — mandatory conformity certificate for electronics, appliances, toys, and medical devices. Requires testing by an INMETRO-accredited lab; takes 6–12 weeks. Apply before production to avoid storage costs in China.
  • NOM (Mexico) — mandatory technical standards for electronics, appliances, and safety equipment. USMCA rules of origin do not apply to China-origin goods; they enter Mexico under MFN duty (0–15%) plus 16% IVA.
  • Language — Brazil requires Portuguese invoices, packing lists, and certificates (with certified translations); Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru, and Argentina require Spanish. Labels must be in the local language.
  • Currency controls — Argentina and Brazil restrict USD access. Use offshore USD accounts, letters of credit, or verified payment channels, and lock the FX rate when placing the order.

Why a LATAM-Aware Agent Matters

Latin America’s combination of high layered taxes, mandatory certification, language requirements, and currency controls makes self-sourcing error-prone. A LATAM-aware agent:

  • Confirms INMETRO/NOM certification before production.
  • Arranges certified Portuguese/Spanish documentation and labeling.
  • Facilitates payments under currency controls (offshore USD, L/C).
  • Ships DDP to major LATAM ports, folding the full tax stack into one price.
  • Models the Brazil IPI+ICMS tax stack (60–100%) per unit before retail pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does a LATAM sourcing agent handle?

A Latin America-aware China sourcing agent handles supplier discovery, INMETRO/NOM certification verification, Portuguese/Spanish documentation, currency-control payment facilitation, DDP shipping to Santos, Manzanillo, Buenaventura, San Antonio or Callao, and AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC. They turn a factory EXW price into a fully cleared landed cost in BRL, MXN, COP, CLP, or PEN.

2. Why is Brazil customs so expensive and slow?

Brazil imposes a complex tax stack on imports: IPI (0–30%), ICMS (17–25% state VAT), plus PIS/COFINS and II (import duty). Combined, these can add 60–100% to the goods value. Customs clearance in Brazil can take 30+ days due to documentation and tax verification requirements.

3. What is INMETRO certification?

INMETRO is Brazil’s national accreditation body, which issues mandatory conformity certificates for regulated products including electronics, appliances, toys, and medical devices. Goods without INMETRO certification are barred from sale in Brazil. The certification requires testing by an INMETRO-accredited lab and takes 6–12 weeks.

4. What is NOM certification in Mexico?

NOM (Normas Oficiales Mexicanas) are Mexico’s mandatory technical standards. NOM certification is required for regulated products including electronics, appliances, and safety equipment. USMCA rules of origin do not apply to China-origin goods, so they enter Mexico under MFN rates of 0–15% plus 16% IVA.

5. What are typical LATAM duties and taxes?

Duties and taxes vary widely: Brazil duty 0–35% plus IPI+ICMS (60–100% combined); Mexico 0–15% duty plus 16% IVA; Chile 0–6% duty plus 19% VAT; Colombia 0–15% duty plus 19% IVA; Peru 0–11% duty plus 18% IGV; Argentina 0–35% duty plus 21% IVA plus country tax.

6. Can I ship DDP to Brazil from China?

Yes, but DDP to Brazil is complex due to the tax stack and clearance times (30+ days). A LATAM-aware agent with a Brazilian clearing partner can offer DDP to Santos, Rio Grande, or Itajaí, folding II, IPI, ICMS, PIS/COFINS, and clearance into one price. Always model the full landed cost before committing, since taxes can exceed the goods value. See DDP shipping from China.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Using the supplier’s test report without verification. Many factories provide fake or expired CE/FCC reports. Verify the report number on the certification body’s website before placing a bulk order.
  • Applying the wrong certification for the destination market. CE for EU, FCC/CPC for US, UKCA for UK, RCM for Australia. Shipping CE-marked goods to the US = customs seizure and $10k+ fines.
  • No test report for the exact product variant. A CE report for a red 220V version does not cover a blue 110V version. Each variant needs its own test or a report covering all variants.
  • Forgetting ongoing compliance. Regulations change (e.g., EU GPSR replacing GPSD, US CPSC updates). A cert valid today may be invalid in 12 months. Track renewal dates and budget for re-testing.
  • Under-declaring product category to skip certification. Listing a children’s toy as ‘home decoration’ to avoid CPC is customs fraud. The buyer of record (you) carries the liability.
