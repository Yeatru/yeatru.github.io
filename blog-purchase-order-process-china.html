Purchase Order Process China Sourcing: From PO Issue to Delivery SOP

Oct 16 2026 Neil Liu 18 min read

Key Takeaways

  • The China PO process has 8 defined steps from requirement confirmation to container loading. Skipping any step raises your defect and delay risk by 3–10×.
  • Never issue a PO without a signed spec sheet and golden sample. 62% of China sourcing disputes trace back to ambiguous specifications that were never written down.
  • Deposit is 30% balance 70%. Never pay 100% upfront. Always verify the factory's bank account against the business license before wiring.
  • Track production at 3 milestones: 20% (raw material check) 60% (in-process QC) and 100% (final AQL inspection). Catching defects at 60% costs 1/10 of catching them at 100%.
  • A well-written PO with 10 mandatory clauses prevents 80% of downstream disputes. The PO is your only legally enforceable document — make it count.

This SOP is based on Yeatru's management of 1 200+ purchase orders from 2024–2026 across 6 product categories. The case study is a real 12 000 USD kitchenware order with the actual timeline and milestones.

A purchase order is the most important document in China sourcing. It is the contract that defines what you are buying how much you pay when it ships and what happens if something goes wrong. Yet 71% of first-time buyers issue a one-line PO ("10 000 pcs silicone spatula at 0.85 USD") and wonder why the factory delivered a different product.

This guide walks you through the complete 8-step purchase order process that Yeatru uses for every client engagement. Each step includes a checklist and the most common pitfalls. We include a real case study of a 12 000 USD order from PO signature to delivery a payment method comparison and a list of 10 mandatory clauses that every China PO must contain.

Whether you are placing your first China order or your fiftieth following this SOP will reduce your defect rate by 60% cut your average delay by 18 days and eliminate 80% of the disputes that drain your time and margin. The 30 minutes you spend drafting a proper PO will save you 30 hours of dispute resolution later.

1. Step 1: Requirement Confirmation & Spec Sheet

Before you contact any supplier you must finalize a detailed product specification sheet. This is the single most important document in the entire process — everything else depends on it.

Spec sheet checklist:

  1. Product name and model number
  2. Detailed description with dimensions (mm) weight (g) and material grade (e.g. 304 stainless food-grade silicone)
  3. Color specification with Pantone code
  4. Logo printing method (silk screen embossed UV print) and location
  5. Packaging details (polybag inner box outer carton dimensions and weights)
  6. Certification requirements (CE FCC FDA etc.)
  7. Quality tolerance (e.g. ±1mm dimension ±5% weight)
  8. Reference photos or drawings (3D renderings preferred)

Pitfall: Vague specs like "good quality" or "nice color" invite the factory to interpret them in the cheapest way possible. "Food-grade silicone 60 Shore A transparent" is a spec; "good silicone" is not.

2. Step 2: RFQ to 3–5 Audited Suppliers

Send your spec sheet to 3–5 suppliers that have been pre-audited (SAMR verification + certificate review). Use the identical spec for all quotes so you can compare apples to apples.

RFQ checklist:

  1. Attach the complete spec sheet (not a summary)
  2. Specify quantity MOQ and target order volume
  3. Specify incoterm (FOB Ningbo/Shanghai recommended)
  4. Request FOB unit price mold fee sample cost and lead time
  5. Request certification documents and factory audit report
  6. Set a quote deadline (3–5 business days)

Pitfall: Sending RFQs to 10+ un-audited suppliers wastes time and attracts low-quality factories. 3–5 audited suppliers is the sweet spot. Never accept a quote that does not include the full spec sheet confirmation.

3. Step 3: Sample Approval & Golden Sample

Before issuing the PO you must receive test and approve a physical sample. The approved sample becomes the "golden sample" — the reference standard for production quality.

Sample checklist:

  1. Request 2–3 samples (one for your approval one to retain one for QC reference)
  2. Test against the spec sheet (dimensions weight material color function)
  3. Photograph and label the approved golden sample with date and supplier name
  4. Sign and date the sample approval form
  5. Send a signed copy of the approval to the factory

Pitfall: The golden sample is often hand-built by the factory's best technician using premium materials. Bulk production may differ. Mitigate this by requiring the factory to confirm in writing that bulk production will match the golden sample in all specifications and by conducting an in-process QC at 20% completion.

4. Step 4: PO Issuance (10 Mandatory Clauses)

The purchase order is the legally binding contract. It must be in writing signed by both parties and include these 10 mandatory clauses:

  1. Product specification: Full description material grade dimensions color (Pantone) packaging. Attach the spec sheet as an exhibit.
  2. Quantity and unit price: Exact quantity unit price total value and incoterm (FOB port). Specify whether price includes mold and samples.
  3. Delivery date and port: Exact shipment date and port of loading. Include "production starts upon deposit receipt".
  4. Quality standard: AQL level (2.5 major / 4.0 minor standard) golden sample reference and defect tolerance.
  5. Certification requirements: Specific standards (CE FCC FDA) lab names and that certificates must cover the exact product.
  6. Payment terms: Deposit percentage (30%) balance percentage (70%) payment method (T/T) and bank account details.
  7. Late delivery penalty: 0.5–1% of PO value per day of delay capped at 5–10% of PO value.
  8. Defect rework terms: Factory must rework or replace defective units within 3–5 days at factory's cost. Re-inspection before shipment.
  9. Mold and IP ownership: Buyer is 100% owner of all molds tooling and designs. Factory cannot produce for others.
  10. Dispute resolution: CIETAC arbitration or Chinese court in supplier's jurisdiction. Governing law: Chinese law.

Pitfall: A PO without a late-delivery penalty gives the factory zero incentive to ship on time. A PO without an IP clause means the factory owns your design. A PO without a defect rework clause means you pay for rework. Every clause matters.

5. Step 5: Deposit Payment & Bank Verification

After both parties sign the PO pay the deposit (typically 30%). Before wiring verify the factory's bank account against their business license.

Deposit payment checklist:

  1. Verify bank account beneficiary name matches the business license company name exactly
  2. Never pay to a personal bank account even if the factory offers a discount
  3. Use T/T (telegraphic transfer) for amounts above 1 000 USD; PayPal for smaller samples
  4. Get the deposit receipt from the factory
  5. Confirm production start date in writing

Pitfall: Factory sales reps sometimes ask for payment to a personal account "because the company account is under audit". This is a fraud red flag. 100% of payment fraud cases Yeatru has seen involved payment to a non-company beneficiary. Always verify the account with the SAMR business license.

6. Step 6: Production Follow-up & In-Process QC

Once production starts you must track progress at 3 milestones. Do not wait until the final inspection to discover problems.

Production tracking schedule:

  • Day 1–3 (0–20%): Confirm raw material arrival. Request photos of raw material with batch numbers. Verify material grade if possible.
  • Day 5–10 (20–40%): First production check. Inspect first off production for spec compliance. This is where you catch material substitution and dimension errors.
  • Day 10–20 (60–80%): In-process QC inspection. Random sample of production against golden sample. Check defect rate and packaging.
  • Day 20–30 (100%): Final AQL inspection before packing. This is the last chance to catch defects before they go in the container.

Pitfall: The factory will tell you "production is on schedule" even when it is not. Do not trust verbal updates — require photo evidence at each milestone. If the factory refuses to send photos assume the worst.

7. Step 7: Final AQL Inspection & Rework

When production is 100% complete and 80%+ packed conduct the final AQL inspection. This is your last quality gate before the goods are sealed in the container.

Final inspection checklist:

  1. Book inspection 3–5 days before the planned ship date
  2. Provide the inspector with the golden sample spec sheet and AQL level
  3. Inspector selects random samples per ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 standard
  4. Check dimensions weight material color function and packaging
  5. Obtain inspection report with photos and defect categorization (critical major minor)
  6. If fail: factory must rework within 3–5 days then re-inspect

Pitfall: Do not allow the factory to choose or pay the inspector. A factory-paid inspector is a factory employee. Always book the inspection directly with a third-party company (SGS Intertek QIMA Asia Inspection) and pay them yourself.

8. Step 8: Balance Payment & Container Loading

After the final AQL inspection passes pay the 70% balance and supervise the container loading.

Balance payment & loading checklist:

  1. Receive the signed AQL pass report before paying balance
  2. Pay balance to the same verified company bank account
  3. Send a representative to supervise container loading (or hire a loading supervision service)
  4. Count cartons verify against packing list and record container seal number
  5. Take photos of loading process sealed container and seal number
  6. Obtain bill of lading commercial invoice packing list and certificate of origin
  7. Track shipment until delivery and confirm receipt at destination

Pitfall: Paying the balance before receiving the AQL pass report means you have no leverage if the inspection fails. Paying before the container is loaded risks short-shipment (factory bills 5 200 pcs ships 4 700). Always pay after AQL pass and before container sealing.

11. 10 Mandatory PO Clauses (Detailed)

Below is the exact structure of the 10 mandatory clauses with typical wording and the risk each clause prevents:

  1. Product specification clause. "Product shall conform to the attached specification sheet (Exhibit A) including material grade dimensions color and packaging. Any deviation requires written buyer approval." Prevents: spec downgrade material substitution.
  2. Quantity and price clause. "Quantity: 2 000 pcs. Unit price: 6.00 USD FOB Ningbo. Total: 12 000 USD. Price includes 1-color laser logo and polybag packaging." Prevents: price increase hidden fees.
  3. Delivery clause. "Delivery date: 25 days from deposit receipt. Port of loading: Ningbo. Late delivery penalty: 0.5% of PO value per day capped at 5%." Prevents: indefinite delay.
  4. Quality clause. "Quality standard: AQL 2.5 major / 4.0 minor per ANSI/ASQ Z1.4. Production must match golden sample signed on [date]. Defect rate above AQL requires rework at factory's cost." Prevents: quality drift.
  5. Certification clause. "Factory shall provide FDA food-contact test report from SGS or Intertek covering this exact product. Report must be valid at time of shipment." Prevents: customs seizure listing suspension.
  6. Payment clause. "Payment terms: 30% T/T deposit upon PO signing 70% T/T balance upon AQL inspection pass and before container loading. Payment to factory's verified company account only." Prevents: payment fraud no leverage.
  7. Late penalty clause. "For each day of delay beyond the agreed delivery date buyer may deduct 0.5% of PO value from the balance payment. Maximum deduction: 5% of PO value." Prevents: factory prioritizing other orders.
  8. Rework clause. "Defective units identified during AQL inspection must be reworked or replaced within 5 business days at factory's sole cost. Re-inspection required before shipment." Prevents: buyer paying for factory mistakes.
  9. IP clause. "Buyer is 100% owner of all molds tooling designs and logos. Factory shall not produce identical or similar products for any third party. Breach carries penalty of 5× PO value." Prevents: IP cloning competitors.
  10. Dispute clause. "Any dispute shall be submitted to CIETAC arbitration in Shanghai under Chinese law. Arbitration award is final and binding." Prevents: unresolvable disputes.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

  1. Can I use the same PO template for every supplier? Yes the clause structure is universal but you must update the product specification quantity price delivery date and certification requirements for each order. The golden sample reference and defect rework terms should remain constant.
  2. What if the factory refuses to sign the late penalty clause? Negotiate the rate (0.5% is standard) but do not remove it entirely. A factory that refuses any penalty clause is signaling they cannot guarantee on-time delivery. Find another supplier.
  3. Should I use Alibaba Trade Assurance instead of a direct PO? Trade Assurance is useful for orders below 5 000 USD as it provides payment protection. For larger orders a direct T/T with a properly drafted PO gives you more control and lower fees (Trade Assurance charges 1.5–2.9%).
  4. How do I handle a factory that wants 50% deposit? For first-time orders 50% is acceptable if the factory is audited and the PO has strong penalty clauses. For repeat orders negotiate down to 30%. Never accept 100% deposit.
  5. What is the minimum order value where a formal PO is worth it? For any order above 1 000 USD a formal PO with the 10 clauses is worth the 30 minutes it takes to prepare. Below 1 000 USD a simplified PO or proforma invoice is usually sufficient.
  6. Can I issue a PO in English or does it need to be in Chinese? The PO can be in English but for legal enforceability in Chinese courts include a Chinese translation. The most important clauses (IP ownership penalty dispute resolution) should be in Chinese.
