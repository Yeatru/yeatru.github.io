How to Track a Shipment from China: Tracking Guide

Tracking numbers, carriers, common statuses, what to do if stuck.

Key Takeaways

  • Tracking numbers differ by mode: express = 10–12 digit waybill; air = 11-digit MAWB (e.g. 074-12345678); sea = BL number + 11-character container number (e.g. MSKU1234567). Use the right tool for each.
  • Best trackers: 17track.net / AfterShip for express & registered mail; the airline's cargo site (cargo.airchina.com) for MAWB; MarineTraffic / VesselFinder / the shipping line portal for containers.
  • A status that hasn't changed for more than 5 business days needs investigation — contact your freight forwarder, not the supplier. The supplier only sees the handoff scan.
  • Customs holds (origin or destination) are the #1 cause of stuck tracking. Have your commercial invoice, packing list and BL ready, and pay duty/VAT promptly to clear in 1–3 days.
  • Peak season (Aug–Oct) routinely adds 5–10 days to every mode; book 4 weeks early and buffer your customer ETA by 2 weeks.

What Does "Tracking a China Shipment" Really Mean?

Tracking a China shipment means following a cargo's status from the moment it leaves your supplier's factory through export customs, the international leg (air or sea), destination import customs, and final delivery. Each mode — express, air, sea — uses a different tracking number format and a different tracking system, so the first step in any trace is identifying which number you have. The supplier, freight forwarder, and destination carrier each see only a slice of the journey; your job is to stitch those slices together and spot stalls early.

How to Track a Shipment: 5 Steps

  1. Get the right number — ask your supplier or forwarder for the carrier tracking number (express), MAWB (air), or BL + container number (sea). A forwarder reference alone may not be traceable on public sites.
  2. Enter it on the matching tracker — waybills on 17track/AfterShip; MAWB on the airline's cargo site; container numbers on MarineTraffic or the shipping line's portal. If you don't know the carrier, paste the number into 17track — it auto-detects.
  3. Map milestones to expected transit — express: 3–7 days; air: 5–12 days; sea FCL USWC: 18–25 days, USEC: 28–35 days, EU: 28–38 days. LCL adds 3–7 days for deconsolidation. See sea freight and air freight.
  4. Flag stalls early — if the status hasn't changed for more than 5 business days, email the forwarder (not the supplier) with the number, expected ETA, and a clear question. The supplier only sees the origin handoff.
  5. Confirm delivery & inspect — once it shows "out for delivery" or "delivered", be present to sign. Before signing the Proof of Delivery, count cartons against the BL and photograph any external damage — concealed damage claims are hard after signing.

Why Tracking Gets Stuck (and How to Fix It)

A status that hasn't moved for 5+ business days usually falls into one of four buckets:

  1. Origin port congestion / peak season — Aug–Oct is peak; vessels and flights are overbooked. Cargo may sit at the origin warehouse or port for 7–14 days waiting for the next available slot. Fix: ask the forwarder for the revised ETD/ETA; buffer your customer ETA by 2 weeks.
  2. Customs hold (origin or destination) — customs wants documents (commercial invoice, packing list, BL, certificate of origin) or wants to inspect. Fix: email the requested docs within 24 hours; pay duty/VAT promptly. Most holds clear in 1–3 days once docs are received.
  3. Missing or incorrect documents — a wrong HS code, mismatched invoice value, or missing certificate (FDA, FCC, CE) will stall customs. Fix: ask the forwarder exactly which document is missing and resend corrected copies. Pre-vet your docs before shipping to avoid this.
  4. Carrier exception — weather, port strikes, vessel rerouting, or a mechanical delay. Fix: the carrier will issue a revised ETA; there's nothing to do but wait and inform your customer.

Worked Example: "Customs Clearance in Progress" for 8 Days

An Amazon FBA seller in the US sees their sea FCL shipment stuck at "Customs clearance in progress — Port of Long Beach" for 8 days. Here's how to resolve it:

  1. Day 1: Email the freight forwarder's customs broker with the BL number and ask for the hold reason. Reply: "CBP is requesting a corrected commercial invoice — the unit price on the invoice ($3.20) does not match the declared value on the entry ($4.10)."
  2. Day 1: Contact the Chinese supplier for a corrected invoice showing the actual paid unit price ($4.10) and the total paid. The supplier sends it within 4 hours.
  3. Day 2: Forward the corrected invoice + proof of payment (bank wire confirmation) to the broker. The broker amends the entry and resubmits.
  4. Day 4: Status updates to "Customs released." Container moves to the trucking queue.
  5. Day 6: Delivered to the Amazon FBA warehouse. Total delay from hold: 6 days (down from an estimated 14+ days if the seller had waited).

The key: don't wait for the system to move. Proactively ask the broker for the exact missing document and supply it within 24 hours. Most customs holds are document problems, not cargo problems.

Tracking by Mode: What to Expect

  • Express (DHL / FedEx / UPS) — scans update every few hours; full door-to-door visibility. ETA is reliable ±1 day. If it stalls, call the carrier directly with the waybill.
  • Air freight — milestones update at handoff, origin customs, flight departure, arrival, destination customs. Middle of the flight has no scan (normal). Track with MAWB on the airline's cargo site.
  • Sea FCL — updates at container load, origin port gate-in, vessel departure, transshipment ports, destination port, rail/truck to door. Vessel position is visible on MarineTraffic by container number. Expect 5–10 day gaps between scans during the ocean leg.
  • Sea LCL — fewer public scans because your cargo shares a container. Use the forwarder's portal; they have visibility into the container your LCL is riding in.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do I track a shipment from China?

Find the tracking number: express waybill (10–12 digits), air MAWB (11 digits like 074-12345678), or sea BL + container number. Then enter it on the right tool: 17track/AfterShip for express, the airline's cargo tracker for MAWB, and MarineTraffic or the shipping line portal for containers.

2. What is the most reliable China tracking site?

For express and registered mail, 17track.net and AfterShip are the most reliable multi-carrier trackers. For sea freight, MarineTraffic.com, VesselFinder, or the shipping line's own portal (Maersk, MSC, COSCO) give container-level ETA updates. For air freight, use the airline's cargo tracker.

3. Why is my China tracking number not updating?

Tracking can stall for 3–7 business days during origin port consolidation, customs inspection, peak-season backlog (Aug–Oct), or while waiting for the next vessel/flight. If a status stays unchanged for more than 5 business days, contact your freight forwarder or carrier for a revised ETA.

4. What does "customs clearance in progress" mean?

Your shipment has arrived in the destination country and is under import customs review. It may be held for document verification, physical inspection, or duty/VAT payment. Provide the requested documents (commercial invoice, packing list, BL, certificates) and pay duty promptly to clear it within 1–3 business days.

5. Can I track sea freight by BL number?

Yes. Use the shipping line's portal (Maersk, MSC, COSCO, Evergreen, etc.) or a third-party site like MarineTraffic.com or VesselFinder. Enter the container number (preferred) or the Bill of Lading number to see vessel position, ETD/ETA, and port calls.

6. How long does a China shipment take after it is shipped?

Express (DHL/FedEx/UPS): 3–7 business days door-to-door. Air freight: 5–12 days. Sea FCL to US West Coast: 18–25 days; to US East Coast: 28–35 days; to Europe: 28–38 days. LCL adds 3–7 days for deconsolidation at destination. Peak season (Aug–Oct) adds 5–10 days.

Conclusion

Tracking a China shipment is mostly about using the right number on the right tool, knowing what each status means, and proactively chasing stalls before they become delays. Express gives near-real-time scans; air and sea have gaps that are normal. The #1 cause of stuck tracking is a customs document hold — which you can usually resolve in 1–3 days by emailing the missing invoice, packing list or certificate. If you booked through a freight forwarder, their portal is your single source of truth; the supplier only sees the origin handoff.
