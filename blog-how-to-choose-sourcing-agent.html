How to Choose a Sourcing Agent in 2026: Complete Buyer Guide

Aug 12, 2026 Neil Liu 45 min read

Key Takeaways

  • The best sourcing agent has a verified business license, an office in China, and 3+ references in your product category.
  • Always ask for a written fee proposal that is all-inclusive — no hidden charges for discovery, audits, or express processing.
  • Test communication before committing. A slow reply now means production delays later.
  • Run a small trial order — also read our low-MOQ sourcing guide ($500–$2,000) before scaling to full container quantities.
  • Evaluate 2–3 agents, not 1. Comparing proposals is the single best way to avoid scams.

Why Choosing the Right Sourcing Agent Matters

Your sourcing agent controls the entire pipeline from factory identification to delivery. A bad agent can cost you thousands in defective goods, missed deadlines, or suppliers that vanish after payment. Choosing the right one is the most important decision you will make when importing from China.

Step 1: Define Your Product and Sourcing Goals

Before talking to any agent, write down exactly what you need:

  • Product category — e.g., silicone kitchenware, wireless headphones, folding furniture
  • Target MOQ — minimum order quantity (MOQ) — for low-MOQ strategies, see our guide you are prepared to commit to
  • Target ex-factory price — your budget per unit, based on competitor research or your sales plan
  • Quality standard — e.g., CE marked, FDA food-safe, AQL 2.5 inspection acceptable
  • Shipping timeline — when do you need the goods at your warehouse?
  • Special requirements — private label, custom packaging, Amazon FBA prep, DDP shipping

The more specific you are, the better an agent can tailor their service. Vague requests like "I want cheap electronics" produce vague results.

Step 2: Verify the Agent's Legal Entity and Business License

Any legitimate sourcing company registered in China has a Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码), a 18-digit number on their business license. Ask for it directly — reputable companies will provide it without hesitation.

You can verify this code on the Chinese government's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (GSXT). Enter the code or company name to confirm the business exists, is active, and matches what the agent claims.

Pro tip: The agent should be registered as a trading company (贸易有限公司) or sourcing consultancy. Be cautious of individuals operating without a company license.

Step 3: Confirm On-the-Ground Location in a Sourcing Hub

Your agent must have a physical presence in one of China's major sourcing hubs:

  • Yiwu — small commodities, home goods, accessories, toys (world's largest small product market)
  • Guangzhou — electronics, fashion, cosmetics, furniture
  • Shenzhen — consumer electronics, smartphones, tech accessories
  • Qingdao / Ningbo — textiles, apparel, chemicals, machinery

Ask for their office address and a photo or video call showing the office. Agents based outside China (e.g., a Western freelancer working remotely) cannot visit factories on your behalf and will struggle with local-language communication and problem resolution.

Step 4: Ask for Three Recent Client References in Your Category

A good agent will happily share 2–3 client names from the past 6 months, preferably in your product category. Reach out to these clients and ask:

  • Did the agent find good suppliers quickly?
  • Were prices lower than you could get directly?
  • How did they handle quality issues or delays?
  • Would you use them again?

If the agent cannot provide references in your specific category, ask how they plan to learn about your product type — their answer tells you how serious they are about your business.

Step 5: Compare Fee Structures Transparently

Request a written fee proposal (Compare with our price negotiation tips.) from each agent you are evaluating. The document should state clearly:

  • Commission percentage or fixed fee
  • Minimum fee per order
  • What services are included (supplier search, negotiation, QC, documentation, logistics)
  • What costs are not included (e.g., product samples, international shipping to you, mold costs)
  • Payment schedule (typically 30–50% deposit to start, remainder on delivery)

Step 6: Test Communication Speed and Quality

Send a test message before engaging: "Hi, I'm looking for silicone spatulas with a target price of $0.85 each and MOQ of 500. Can you help?"

  • Response within 2 hours (during business hours): Excellent — this agent is attentive and responsive
  • Response within 24 hours: Acceptable — but confirm they will be faster during actual business
  • Response after 3 days, or no response: Reject — production issues will also receive delayed responses Compare with Alibaba agents to test their claims.

Communication quality matters too. Look for agents who ask clarifying questions (e.g., "What certifications do you need?"), not those who immediately say "yes we can do that" without understanding your product.

Step 7: Run a Small Trial Order Before Scaling

Never commit to a 10,000-piece container order with a new agent. Start with:

  • Sample order (50–200 pieces) — verify quality, packaging, and delivery speed
  • Small production run (500–2,000 pieces) — test the agent's end-to-end management

A good trial order gives you confidence in the agent's process, the supplier's quality, and the logistics chain. If anything goes wrong, the financial exposure is limited and you can fix the issue before scaling.

What to Ask in the First Call

Prepare these questions for your initial conversation:

  1. How long have you been sourcing products for international buyers?
  2. What product categories do you specialize in?
  3. How do you verify the factories you work with? (See our supplier verification guide.)
  4. What happens if I receive defective goods? (Learn how we handle quality control.)
  5. Can you help with customs, duties, and DDP shipping? (Read about our logistics services.)
  6. What is your typical turnaround time from RFQ to first quotation? (Also see our sourcing agent FAQ.)
  7. Do you have a formal service agreement or contract?

How Many Agents to Evaluate

Evaluate 2–3 agents before choosing more is usually better. If you evaluate only one, you have no comparison. If you evaluate five or more, you waste time and may become paralyzed by too many choices. Two or three proposals give you enough data to compare fees, responsiveness, and expertise.

The Value of a Local Visit

If your order size is significant ($50,000+) or you are developing a new custom product and OEM development, a 3–5 day trip to Yiwu or Guangzhou with your agent is invaluable. You will visit the factory in person, meet the production manager, and see quality control first hand. Your agent will handle all local logistics — transport, translation, introductions — so you can focus on evaluation.

Red Flags to Avoid

  • No business license available — any legitimate Chinese company has one
  • Operates only on messaging apps — no company email, no website, no LinkedIn presence
  • Fees above 10% or multiple hidden charges piling up
  • No references in your category and cannot explain why
  • Pushy sales tactics — pressures you to sign today with a "one-time discount"
  • Payment to personal bank account — payments should go to the company account, not an individual
  • Claims to offer "factory-direct prices" and also be a sourcing agent — pick one: factory or agent. Agents do not own factories.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to find a good sourcing agent?

1–2 weeks is reasonable: 2–3 days to shortlist candidates, 3–5 days to communicate and collect proposals, 2–3 days to evaluate and decide. Do not rush this.

Should I choose the cheapest agent?

No. Choose the best value agent. An agent charging 6% who negotiates 20% lower prices saves you more than an agent charging 4% who only gets 5% savings. Focus on total landed cost, not fee percentage alone.

Can I switch agents mid-project?

You can, but it creates delays and potential data loss (your old agent may have supplier contacts they will not share). It is better to choose well at the start. If you must switch, get all supplier names, factory contact details, and QC records in writing before parting ways.

What if an agent offers a free trial?

A free consultation or quotation review is fine — agents like Yeatru offer this routinely. But be suspicious of agents offering "free sourcing services" — they may be factory middlemen who get paid by the supplier to push their products.

Do I need a contract?

Yes. Always work with a formal service agreement that outlines deliverables, fee structure, payment terms, liability limits, and dispute resolution. A 2–3 page document is sufficient — it does not need to be complex legal work.

Conclusion

Choosing a sourcing agent is not a one-hour decision. Take the time to verify credentials, compare proposals, test communication, and run a small trial. The cost of choosing a bad agent — in lost inventory, missed deadlines, and supplier fraud — far exceeds the effort of choosing well. Follow these seven steps, and you will find a reliable partner who saves you time and money for years to come.
