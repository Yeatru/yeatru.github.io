Amazon FBA Sourcing from China: Complete Guide (2026)

How to source products for Amazon FBA from China, compliance, FBA prep.

Key Takeaways

  • A typical Amazon FBA unit from China lands at EXW $2–$15 + DDP freight $0.50–$3.00 (sea) or $4–$8 (air) + duty 0–25% + Section 301 7.5–25% + FBA fees $2.50–$5+ fulfillment + 8–15% referral + $0.83/cuft/mo storage — a $5 EXW product often nets $14–$18 landed cost.
  • Amazon gates categories (baby, health, topicals, batteries, supplements) unless you upload the right test docs — FCC + UL/ETL + UN38.3 for electronics, CPC + ASTM F963 for toys, FDA + LFGB for food-contact, CE + RoHS + GPSR for EU. See Amazon restricted products.
  • Always verify the factory on gsxt.gov.cn before paying — see the supplier license verification guide — and use 50/50 payment terms: 30–50% deposit, balance only after AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC.
  • FBA prep is non-negotiable: FNSKU barcode on every unit, poly bag with suffocation warning on openings over 5″, bubble wrap for fragile SKUs, master carton labels for LTL/SPD inbound. China-side prep saves 60–80% vs US-side — see FBA prep in China.
  • Sea freight for 500+ kg non-urgent restocks ($0.50–$1.50/kg, 25–40 days), air freight for 45–500 kg urgent restocks ($4–$9/kg, 3–7 days). Plan reorder 45 days before hitting 30-day safety stock so your Buy Box never disappears.

What Is Amazon FBA Sourcing from China?

Amazon FBA sourcing from China is the end-to-end process of finding a compliant factory, producing private-label or wholesale SKUs, applying FBA prep, and shipping door-to-Amazon under DDP terms — with every step filtered through Amazon's Restricted Products Policy, FBA prep requirements, and US/EU compliance law. It is not the same as generic China sourcing: an FBA unit must clear Amazon's receiving rules (FNSKU labels, suffocation-warning poly bags, expiration dating) and customs (FCC/CPC/CE/FDA docs, Section 301 tariffs, HS-code duty) before it can be sold. Skip either layer and the ASIN is gated, returned, or seized.

FBA Prep Rules: FNSKU, Poly Bag, Suffocation Warning

Amazon rejects inbound shipments that fail prep rules — and the units get returned at $0.50–$2.00/unit plus refund admin fees. China-side prep at the factory or consolidation warehouse is 60–80% cheaper than US-side prep and lets you ship pre-labeled cartons straight to the FBA fulfillment center.

  • FNSKU barcode on every unit (or X00 label if Amazon provides) — not the factory's UPC. Use 600-dpi thermal labels, not inkjet.
  • Poly bag with suffocation warning on any opening over 5 inches — printed warning in English, on every bag.
  • Bubble wrap for fragile SKUs (glass, ceramics, screen-protected electronics). Use 5mm bubble, double-wall for over 2 kg.
  • Expiration dating on perishables — printed YYYY-MM-DD, not handwritten.
  • Master carton labels for LTL/SPD inbound — 2 labels per carton (side + top), with shipment ID and carton X of Y.
  • Hazmat flags for lithium batteries, aerosols, flammables — flagged at SKU creation, or FBA refuses the ASIN.

For the full prep checklist with photo examples, see Amazon FBA prep requirements and the China-side FBA prep service guide.

Section 301 Tariffs and How They Change Sourcing

The US Section 301 tariff regime adds 7.5–25% ad valorem on roughly two-thirds of China-made HS codes, layered on top of normal MFN duty. The 2024–2026 rate hikes targeted EVs (100%), solar cells (50%), lithium batteries (25%), steel/aluminum (25%), and medical gloves (50%). For a typical kitchen or home SKU, the 301 layer is 7.5–25% — enough to flip a margin from positive to negative.

Sourcing tactics under 301: (1) pick an HS code with 0% or 7.5% instead of 25%; (2) move final assembly to Vietnam/Mexico for the US market while keeping China components; (3) price the 301 into your landed-cost model before setting the Amazon retail price.

See the 2026 Section 301 tariff guide for the current rate table by HS chapter and the Vietnam-transshipment rules.

How to Source for Amazon FBA from China (5 Steps)

  1. Pick a compliant, non-gated category. Run the product through Amazon Restricted Products Policy, US de minimis ($800), EU GPSR, and Section 301 lists. Avoid baby, health, topicals, batteries, supplements unless you have the test docs. Start with FBA product research.
  2. Verify the factory and run a 3-quote RFQ. Verify the supplier's business license on gsxt.gov.cn (see license verification) and request 3 factory quotes side-by-side with FOB and DDP pricing, MOQ, lead time, and existing Amazon-seller references. Compare landed cost, not unit price.
  3. Place a 50–100 pc test order with sample approval. Validate demand before committing a full container. Approve pre-production samples first, then place the order with 30–50% deposit under the 50/50 rule.
  4. Pre-shipment QC + FBA prep. Run AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection (or full PSI for high-value SKUs), then apply FBA prep — FNSKU labels, suffocation-warning poly bags, bubble wrap, master carton labels. See FBA QC inspection.
  5. Ship DDP to Amazon FBA and time restock. Ship DDP (door-to-Amazon) via sea LCL/FCL for 500+ kg or air freight for 45–300 kg urgent restock. Plan 60–90 days from PO to in-stock for sea, 20–30 for air. Maintain 30-day safety stock. See FBA shipping from China.

Common FBA Sourcing Mistakes (and What They Cost)

  • Listing before compliance docs. Amazon gates the ASIN mid-sale; stranded inventory at the FC incurs $0.83/cuft/mo + removal fees. Cost: $200–$2,000 per ASIN.
  • Releasing balance before QC. Factory ships B-grade units; Amazon returns them at $0.50–$2.00/unit plus refund admin. On 500 units that's $250–$1,000 gone.
  • Ignoring 301 tariffs. A 25% 301 layer on a $5 EXW product adds $1.25/unit — on a thin-margin SKU that flips profit to loss.
  • US-side FBA prep. Costs $0.75–$1.50/unit vs $0.15–$0.40 in China; on 1,000 units that's $600–$1,100 wasted.
  • Air freight on heavy, low-value SKUs. A 1.5 kg ceramic mug at $7/kg air = $10.50 freight on a $3 product. Use sea freight.
  • No restock cadence. Bestseller hits 0 units, loses Buy Box rank in 48 hours. Reorder 45 days before safety stock hits zero.

Low-MOQ and Private-Label FBA Sourcing

For first-time FBA launches, low-MOQ sourcing (50–500 pcs) lets you validate demand without committing a full container. Stock-lot suppliers and 1688 back-stocks go as low as 50 pcs; OEM private label typically needs 300–1,000 pcs per SKU. For brand-registered sellers, Amazon private label from China pairs factory OEM with FBA prep and brand registry packaging — see the private-label packaging guide for carton-insert design.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How much does Amazon FBA sourcing from China cost?

A typical FBA unit lands at EXW ($2–$15) + DDP freight ($0.50–$3.00/unit sea, $4–$8/unit air) + duty (0–25% by HS code, plus Section 301 tariffs 7.5–25% on many China-made goods) + Amazon FBA fees ($2.50–$5+ fulfillment + 8–15% referral + $0.83/cuft/mo storage). A $5 EXW product often nets $14–$18 landed cost on Amazon.

2. How long does Amazon FBA sourcing from China take?

From RFQ to in-stock FBA: 5–15 days for RFQ + sample; 30–45 days production; 25–40 days sea freight + customs + Amazon receiving — total 60–90 days for sea, 25–35 days for air. Plan the reorder 45 days before you hit 30-day safety stock.

3. What Amazon compliance docs do I need from a Chinese factory?

Depends on category: electronics need FCC + UL/ETL + battery UN38.3; toys need CPC + ASTM F963; kitchenware + food-contact needs FDA + LFGB; topicals/supplements need FDA + GMP; EU sales need CE + RoHS + REACH + GPSR. Without the right test reports Amazon will gate or suspend the ASIN.

4. What FBA prep does Amazon require for China shipments?

FNSKU barcode on every unit (or X00 label), poly bag with suffocation warning on openings over 5 inches, bubble wrap for fragile SKUs, expiration dating on perishables, master carton labels for LTL/SPD inbound, and hazmat flags for lithium batteries or aerosols. China-side FBA prep at the factory or consolidation warehouse saves 60–80% vs US-side prep.

5. Do I need 50/50 deposit terms on Amazon FBA orders?

Yes — 30–50% deposit to start production, 50–70% balance only after AQL 2.5 pre-shipment QC photos pass. On a $5,000 order the wire fee is $25–$35; releasing balance before QC lets the factory ship B-grade units that Amazon will return at $0.50–$2.00/unit plus refund admin fees.

6. Sea freight or air freight for Amazon FBA restocking?

Sea LCL/FCL for 500+ kg, non-urgent restocks: $0.50–$1.50/kg, 25–40 day transit, lowest per-unit cost. Air freight for 45–500 kg urgent restocks: $4–$9/kg, 3–7 day transit, used when a bestseller is about to lose Buy Box. Express courier (DHL) only for samples or under-45 kg lots.
