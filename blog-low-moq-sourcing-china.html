Low MOQ Sourcing from China: 50-500 Pieces Guide

July 28, 2026

What Is Low-MOQ Sourcing from China?

Low-MOQ sourcing is the practice of buying Chinese products at order quantities below the factory’s standard production minimum — typically 50–500 pieces per SKU instead of the usual 1,000–10,000. It is the only way for first-time Amazon FBA sellers, TikTok Shop creators, Kickstarter creators, and small retailers to test a market without committing a full container. The trade-off is always a higher per-unit cost: factories amortize line setup, mold changes, and material minimums over fewer units, so a 100-pc run prices 15–40% above a 1,000-pc run. Sourcing agents and Yiwu-based consolidators — like Yeatru — are the bridge between foreign small-order buyers and the Chinese low-MOQ ecosystem (Yiwu Trade City, 1688.com, stock-lot wholesalers, and OEM split-MOQ).

2026 Minimum Order Reference Table by Channel

The “right” low-MOQ channel depends on what you need: cash-and-carry speed, stock-lot price, or custom private-label. Below is the 2026 reference matrix Yeatru uses to route small orders.

| | Channel || Typical MOQ || Per-Unit Premium vs 1,000 pcs || Best For |

| Yiwu International Trade City (offline) || 1 – 10 pcs || +80% to +200% || Sample buying, urgent replenishment, niche SKUs |

| 1688.com stock wholesalers (online) || 2 – 50 pcs || +30% to +80% || Small Amazon FBA launches, mixed-SKU orders |

| Stock-clearance job lots || 50 – 200 pcs || +15% to +40% || Liquidation, last-season resale, promo bundles |

| OEM split-MOQ (factory, multi-SKU) || 100 – 300 pcs/SKU || +15% to +30% || Private-label launches with logo/packaging |

| OEM standard MOQ (factory) || 1,000+ pcs || Baseline || Confirmed bestsellers, scaled reorders |

| Alibaba “Ready to Ship” (RTS) || 10 – 100 pcs || +20% to +60% || Pre-stocked catalog, 7-day lead |

The +80–200% Yiwu premium reflects the cash-and-carry convenience and stall-rent overhead. For 100-pc first orders, 1688 stock wholesalers + Yiwu consolidation is usually the sweet spot.

Where to Find Low-MOQ Suppliers in China

  • Yiwu International Trade City — 75,000 stalls over 5 districts; cash-and-carry on most categories; 1–10 pc minimums; best for sample buying and replenishment. Pair with a Yiwu agent for QC and export handling.
  • 1688.com — Alibaba’s Chinese-language wholesale marketplace; 2–50 pc stock minimums; 30–60% cheaper than Alibaba.com because it’s the domestic price tier. Use an agent for verification and DDP.
  • Stock-clearance wholesalers in Yiwu and Shantou — 50–200 pc job lots at 30–50% off list; ideal for liquidation and promo bundles. Inspect carefully: stock lots often hide defects.
  • OEM factories accepting split-MOQ — negotiate 100 pc per color × 5 colors = 500 pc total to share the setup cost. Works best when the factory already runs your product line for bigger buyers.
  • Alibaba “Ready to Ship” (RTS) — pre-stocked catalog with 7-day lead; 10–100 pc minimums at +20–60% premium vs custom OEM. Convenient but no private-label customization.

Common Low-MOQ Mistakes That Cost Buyers

  • Treating “low MOQ” as “low risk” — small orders are more likely to come from trading companies or stock lots, not the factory shown on Alibaba. Always verify on gsxt.gov.cn.
  • Paying 100% upfront to “save the wire fee” — even on $500 orders, the 50/50 rule applies; low-MOQ trading companies are more likely to substitute stock-lot defects when full cash is in hand.
  • Ignoring consolidation — shipping 5 separate 50-pc orders via express courier costs 2–3× what consolidating into one DDP air or LCL shipment costs.
  • Skipping pre-shipment QC — a 100-pc lot is exactly the size where trading companies quietly slip in B-grade stock-lot units. A $120 inspector day catches the swap.
  • Quoting EXW only — a 100-pc order at $5 EXW can land at $7.50+ DDP after $3/kg air freight and duty. Always compare DDP apples-to-apples.
  • Assuming OEM branding fits a 100-pc order — custom packaging MOQ is usually 500–1,000 pcs; private-label tooling starts at $1,500–$6,000. For 100 pcs, prefer stock products with sticker/sleeve branding.
  • Forgetting duty on small parcels — US de minimis is now $800; EU IOSS applies VAT on every order. Calculate landed cost before pricing retail.

How to Negotiate a Lower MOQ Without Paying 2× the Price

  • Split MOQ across colors or sizes — 100 pc × 5 colors = 500 pc total. The factory hits its material minimum; you get SKU variety.
  • Pay the setup fee as a one-off — instead of $6.80/unit on 50 pcs ($340), pay a $200 setup + $4.30/unit on 100 pcs ($630). You get 2× the units for 1.85× the price.
  • Use stock product + custom packaging — buy 200 pc of stock product, then print a 500-pc run of custom retail boxes. The box MOQ amortizes over the next 2–3 orders.
  • Commit to a reorder — a written commitment to a 1,000-pc reorder in 90 days often unlocks the lower 1,000-pc unit price on the 100-pc trial.
  • Bundle with a Yiwu agent — an agent’s order book across multiple buyers can hit the factory’s MOQ, and each buyer gets the lower unit price. Yeatru runs this model weekly for Amazon FBA launches.

Low-MOQ Payment & Quality Control

Even on a $500–$2,000 order, the standard defenses apply:

  • 50/50 deposit rule — 30–50% to start, balance only after AQL 2.5 pre-shipment photos clear (see 50/50 deposit guide).
  • AQL 2.5 inspection on every lot over 50 pcs — one inspector day at $120–$200 catches 90% of stock-lot swaps. Use pre-shipment inspection.
  • Bank transfer to the licensed company — never to a personal account (see fake invoice scams).
  • Sample approval before mass production — even on 100 pc orders, a $40–$80 sample plus $25 DHL beats a 100-pc mistake.
  • DDP shipping so duty, customs, and last-mile are pre-paid — see our DDP shipping guide for the full landed-cost formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the lowest MOQ I can order from China?

Yiwu International Trade City accepts 1–10 pc cash-and-carry on most off-the-shelf items. 1688.com stock wholesalers accept 2–50 pcs. Stock-clearance job lots run 50–200 pcs. OEM custom private label typically requires 300–1,000 pcs per SKU. Lower MOQ always means a higher per-unit price (15–40% premium for 100 vs 1,000 pcs).

2. Why do factories in China quote high MOQs?

Because production-line setup, mold changes, and material minimums (e.g. 500 kg resin, 1,000 m fabric) all carry fixed costs that only amortize at scale. A 100-pc run shares the same setup cost as a 5,000-pc run, so the factory charges 15–40% more per unit to keep margin. Splitting MOQ across colors or SKUs at the same factory can reduce the floor.

3. Can I get my logo / custom packaging on a 100-pc order?

Often yes, but at a premium. Print-on-package MOQ is usually 500–1,000 pcs at $0.15–$0.40/pc; on 100 pcs expect $0.80–$2.50/pc. Private-label mold tooling (e.g. a custom silicone spatula) starts at $1,500–$6,000 and requires 1,000+ pcs to amortize. For 100 pcs, prefer stock products with sticker or sleeve branding.

4. Is 1688.com safe for small orders?

1688 is Alibaba’s Chinese-language wholesale marketplace with lower MOQs (2–50 pcs) and lower unit prices, but it has limited buyer protection for foreign buyers. Use a Yiwu-based agent to verify suppliers, consolidate orders, run pre-shipment QC, and ship DDP — the agent fee (5–8%) is far cheaper than the loss from a defective stock lot.

5. How do I get freight cost down on a 50–100 pc order?

Consolidate. Ship 5–10 SKUs at 50–100 pcs each in one DDP shipment; this hits air-freight 45 kg breakpoints and sea-freight LCL minimums, cutting per-unit freight by 25–50%. A Yiwu consolidation warehouse can hold goods free for 15 days while other factory orders arrive.

6. Should I pay 100% upfront for a small order to save wire fees?

No. Even on a $500 order, the 50/50 rule applies: 30–50% deposit to start, 50–50% balance after pre-shipment QC photos clear. Low-MOQ trading companies are more likely to substitute stock-lot defects when full cash is in hand. The $25 wire fee is cheaper than losing $500 to a defective lot.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Visiting Yiwu without a pre-trip product list. The market has 75,000+ booths across 5 districts. Without a targeted list, you waste days walking and buy on impulse. Research products and target districts before you go.
  • Buying from the first booth you see. The same product is often sold by 20+ booths at different prices and quality. Walk at least 3 booths in the same district before committing.
  • No consolidation plan. Buying from 10 booths means 10 separate shipments. Arrange a consolidation warehouse before you go — it saves 30–50% on international freight.
  • Accepting ‘sample price’ as the bulk price. Booth prices are sample prices (1–5 pcs). Always negotiate the bulk (MOQ) price, packaging, and lead time in writing before leaving the booth.
  • Ignoring minimum order quantities. Many Yiwu booths have MOQs of 1–2 cartons. If you need smaller quantities, ask about mixed-carton or sample-box pricing before negotiating.
