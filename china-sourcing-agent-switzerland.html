Why one agency instead of four suppliers and a broker

A Swiss order usually fails on documents, not on product. The factory can make the goods, but it will not classify them for Swiss customs, arrange a certificate of origin, or tell you which language the packaging has to be in. Those three tasks are exactly what a procurement agency takes over.

Switzerland: three differences from an EU shipment

Customs and 8.1% VAT

Switzerland is outside the EU customs union, so every consignment is declared on import and 8.1% import VAT applies. A Swiss VAT registration and a Swiss importer of record are what make that VAT recoverable; without them it is a cost. Duty is calculated on the Swiss tariff and, for many goods, on weight rather than value — which changes the maths on heavy items.

Product safety, not GPSR

EU GPSR does not apply directly in Switzerland. Swiss product safety runs under the Product Safety Act (PrSG), with sector rules — toys under the Swiss toy ordinance, which is aligned with EN 71. CE marking is generally recognised, but the Swiss market still expects a Swiss contact point and documentation in an official language. Treat an EU-compliant product as a starting point, not as a finished Swiss file.

WEEE and batteries

Electrical and electronic goods fall under the Swiss WEEE regime, usually handled through eRecycling (SENS eRecycling), and batteries through INOBAT. Unlike the EU, Switzerland does not run a general packaging producer registration for every importer, so packaging is not the bottleneck — the electrical and battery registrations are.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ship to Switzerland the same way as to Germany?

No. Switzerland is outside the EU customs union, so the consignment is declared again at the Swiss border, 8.1% import VAT is charged, and duty follows the Swiss tariff rather than the EU one. A delivery that works for Germany needs its own Swiss customs entry and, for eligible goods, its own certificate of origin.

Does EU GPSR apply in Switzerland?

Not directly. Switzerland applies its own Product Safety Act (PrSG) and sector ordinances; toys follow the Swiss toy ordinance, which aligns with EN 71. CE marking is generally accepted, but the Swiss file still needs an official-language label and a Swiss contact. We treat EU compliance as the base and check the Swiss additions before production.

Can you get the China–Switzerland FTA certificate of origin?

Where the HS code qualifies, yes. The certificate has to be issued before export, not after arrival, and it only helps if the goods meet the origin rules. We check the code at quotation stage so the duty assumption in your landed cost is not a guess.

What is the minimum order?

50 pieces per SKU for custom and private-label orders, with mixed SKUs consolidated; stock items from one piece. Below USD 1,000 the published 10% low-order rate applies, so a small first order is still workable.

Which languages must the packaging be in?

The official language of the region where the product is sold: German, French or Italian, depending on the shelf. A national launch often uses German plus French. We set this at the label stage, because reprinting packaging after the goods are made is the most expensive correction in the whole order.
